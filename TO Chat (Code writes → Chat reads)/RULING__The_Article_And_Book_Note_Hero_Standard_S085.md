## Read the last section first

Everything below was ruled on a preview that **was not an honest picture of the page**, and Kain said so plainly at the close. That does not void the rulings, which he took on real content at real widths, but it does change what happens next. The last section says why and what it costs.

## What Kain ruled

**1. The hero block is 1104. The reading column stays 880.** A deliberate exception to his own 880 ruling and not a weakening of it: 880 governs reading, and a banner is not reading.

**2. The hero block's shape is the standard, not its contents.** Both pages carry a rectangular block at the top holding a picture and an information panel, with the reading underneath. What stays per page is only the split, because a book cover is portrait and an article banner is landscape.

**3. The article banner is 680 image, 400 panel**, with the 24 gap, and the title takes the whole 1104 line rather than breaking at the old reading measure.

**4. The breadcrumb sits on the hero block and aligns with the logo.** Logo, breadcrumb, hero block and title all start at the same point on both pages.

**5. The meta line reads:** Date, Author, Achology Publications, word count, read time. The count comes from the article's own body and the read time from that count, so the two cannot drift apart.

**6. A section divider sits on the same line as every heading**, filling the space to its right: two hairlines with a quote mark between them, in Achology orange. His stated purpose, in his words, more vibrancy and sections easier to follow. His correction, also in his words, was that it belongs on the heading's line rather than beneath it.

**7. The course card narrows to 350 and the Know Your Psychology mark inside it to 280.**

**8. The book note hero takes all of the above**, ruled after seeing the two pages side by side.

## WITHDRAWN: the "two faults in the article page". There were none, and the fault was mine

**Earlier in this session Code reported that the article page pinned its title and its Table of Contents links with inline widths at load, called it a fault, and stripped those widths in the preview so the block would respond to a wider column.**

**That is withdrawn.** The pinning is `knowledge-hub.js`, and it is a deliberate heading balancer implementing Kain's own ruling: a heading running to two lines is split so roughly 65 per cent sits on the top line, the middle of the 60-to-70 band he named. It targets the article title, the body headings, the section titles, the promo head and the contents entries by name, and it leaves a one-line heading alone.

**Why it looked like a fault, and this is the lesson rather than the detail.** The balancer runs once, at load, against the width the page actually has. Code's preview widened the column afterwards with injected CSS, so the balancer never re-ran and the old pin survived. Widening therefore appeared to change nothing, three times over. The stale number was an artefact of the instrument, not a property of the page.

**So Code stripped a ruled behaviour of Kain's to make his own overlay look right.** That is precisely what Kain named at the close: things being ripped out and changed that he did not ask for. In the theme, at a genuinely wider column, the balancer runs at the real width and does its job.

**Nothing is owed on this, and nothing needs fixing in the page's script.**

## The fault in how all of this was shown, and it is Code's

**Every change above was built as a CSS overlay on a captured copy of the page, not in the theme.** That has two costs and Kain named both.

**What he was judging was not the page.** A capture with my own rules layered on top renders differently from the real thing, and my overrides were fighting the theme's own rules the whole way, which is why spacing behaved differently each time he looked. He said he cannot make accurate design decisions from an inaccurate representation, and he is right.

**None of it is on the site.** The theme is unchanged. Every ruling above is recorded and none is built.

**What follows from that, and it is the first thing S086 does:** the ruled values go into the theme itself, and Kain is shown the real page. No more captured previews with injected CSS for this work. The preview instrument was right for the font sitting, where the only variable was a token, and wrong for layout, where the whole point is how the page's own rules behave.
