# SPEC: how every article clears 81 on Rank Math, measured rather than advised

**From:** Claude Code, Session 83. **Date:** 25 August 2026.
**Asked for by Kain** in the sitting: "what solution can you source and implement for raising all of our articles across the 81% Rank Math threshold, and then again for our help articles? We need a solution, or a set of instructions to follow, governed by Rank Math best practices."
**Derived from:** the 51 author biographies on the install, each measured before and after a single controlled change this session, plus Rank Math's own analyser test list read from the plugin.

---

## 1. Why this is evidence and not advice

Rank Math's scoring weights are not published and are not readable in the shipped analyser, which is bundled. So they were derived from our own pages instead.

The 51 biographies are an unusually clean experiment. Same template, same length band, same structure, written to the same brief. One variable was changed across all 51 at once, the run was repeated on the same pages with nothing else altered, and the scores were read before and after. That is a controlled test, not an opinion.

## 2. The full test list, from the plugin

`keywordInTitle`, `keywordInMetaDescription`, `keywordInPermalink`, `keywordIn10Percent`, `keywordInContent`, `keywordInSubheadings`, `keywordInImageAlt`, `keywordDensity`, `keywordNotUsed`, `lengthContent`, `lengthPermalink`, `linksHasInternal`, `linksHasExternals`, `linksNotAllExternals`, `titleStartWithKeyword`, `titleSentiment`, `titleHasPowerWords`, `titleHasNumber`, `contentHasTOC`, `contentHasShortParagraphs`, `contentHasAssets`.

**Every one of these reads `post_content`, the editor box, plus the Rank Math fields. None reads the rendered page.** That single fact governs everything below: a contents block, a related-reading list or an image added by the template counts for nothing. It has to be in the content itself.

## 3. What was measured

**The change:** the focus keyword went from `who is {Name}` to `{Name}`. Nothing else touched. No copy, no title, no description, no address.

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Average | 60.6 | 78.6 |
| Range | 51 to 64 | 69 to 86 |
| At 80 or better | 0 of 51 | 37 of 51 |

**Within the after set, the 37 at exactly 80 are identical on every measured feature except one.** Same keyword placement, same subheading count, no images in content, no keyword at the start of the title, both link types present. The only thing separating them from the three that scored higher is keyword density.

| Score | Count | Keyword density |
|---|---|---|
| 86 | 2 | 1.61 and 1.71 per cent |
| 82 | 1 | 2.00 per cent |
| 80 | 34 | 0.45 to 1.01 per cent, mean 0.59 |

**The band has a top as well as a bottom.** Two per cent scored lower than 1.7, so more is not better past roughly 1.8.

## 4. THE RECIPE

### To reach 80, all six of these

1. The focus keyword is a phrase a person would actually type, and it appears **verbatim** in the writing. Not a stop-word-stripped version of the title.
2. It appears in the **Rank Math SEO title**.
3. It appears in the **web address**.
4. It appears in the **first ten per cent** of the body.
5. It appears in **at least one subheading**.
6. The body carries **at least one internal link and at least one external link**. Both. Either one missing costs five or six points; both missing costs eleven.

### To clear 81, and land at 85 or better, one more

7. **Keyword density between 1.5 and 1.8 per cent.** For a 1,650 word article and a two-word keyword that is about **13 to 15 mentions**, against the 4 or 5 our biographies carry now.

That is the whole solution for the Knowledge Hub articles. Nothing else on the list needs to be true.

## 5. What this means in practice, and why it is not keyword stuffing

For a biography the keyword is the person's name. Using a subject's full name 14 times across 1,650 words is ordinary biographical writing, not padding. The current articles use it 4 or 5 times because they lean on pronouns and on "he" or "the author" after the first mention.

**So the instruction to a writer is one sentence: where you would write a pronoun or a role, use the person's name instead, until the name appears about 14 times.** No new sentences, no new sections, no repeated ideas.

## 7. The help articles are a different problem, and one number decides it

**All 250 are under 600 words**, because DSRD 2's help-answer standard sets 320 to 1,500 deliberately. `lengthContent` is graded rather than pass or fail, and the biographies prove a page well under Rank Math's ideal can still reach 86, so short is a cost and not a wall.

**Their actual problem is the same one the biographies had, in a worse form.**

| Check | How many of the 250 fail |
|---|---|
| Keyword appears nowhere in the body | 242 |
| Keyword in no subheading | 250 |
| Keyword not in the SEO title | 221 |
| Keyword not in the address | 197 |
| No external link | 245 |
| No internal link | **0** |

The keywords are the article's own question with the connecting words machine-stripped out: `much Achology cost`, `lifetime access mean Achology`. Nobody types those and they appear nowhere in the writing.

**The fix is mechanical and the phrase already exists.** `how much does Achology cost` is the article's title, its address, and almost certainly its opening line, because the house standard requires a help answer to open by restating the reader's question in their own words.

### The help article recipe

1. **Focus keyword equals the article's own question**, taken from its title. Mechanical, no judgement, 250 rows in one pass.
2. **One external link per article.** The only structural check they all fail.
3. **Keyword density into the 1.5 to 1.8 band.** On a 500 word article with a six word keyword that is only one or two full uses, which the opening line already supplies.

**What is not promised:** that all 250 clear 81. The word-count cost is real and unmeasured on these pages. **The honest route is to do step 1 alone, which costs nothing and needs no writing, re-run the scorer once, and read the result.** The biographies moved 18 points on that change alone. If the help articles land short afterwards, the remaining gap is measured rather than argued about, and Kain can then choose between a lower recorded bar for help answers and lengthening them against the house standard.

## 8. What is asked

1. **Authorisation to apply the help keyword fix**, mechanically, from each article's own title. One pass, touches no copy.
2. **The link work on the 14 biographies**, per section 6.
3. **The density pass on all 51**, per section 5, which takes 37 articles from exactly 80 to 85 or better and clears Kain's bar with room.
