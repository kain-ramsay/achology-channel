## On the method, since the commission invited comment

**Reading the comparison CSV was the right and cheapest route.** No Drive call was needed for any of the three passes.

**The ordinal definition as written cannot see either of the two serious fault classes, and it is worth writing down why.** It misses `003-134 / 003-135`, because that is a duplication inside Drive rather than a disagreement across the sources. It misses course 010 entirely, because a shifted run produces two names that are both perfectly sensible and simply belong to different lessons. **An ordinal comparison can only catch a row that argues with itself, and neither real fault in this library argues with itself.** Any future pass over this data needs all three questions: do the two names disagree, do two Drive names collide, and does this Drive name fit a neighbour better than itself.

**Comparing across different kinds of ordinal produces noise.** An early run that treated `Phase 2` on one side and `Part 2` on the other as a disagreement returned 43 rows, 37 of them the Becca and Laurie demonstration sessions, where the Drive name simply carries more structure and both sides agree perfectly on the part. The final run compares like with like: part against part, session against session, phase against phase, with a bare trailing numeral, a roman numeral and a trailing A or B all read as part markers.

**Duration remains the missing signal** and nothing here substitutes for it. It would settle course 010 in minutes and no name comparison ever will.
