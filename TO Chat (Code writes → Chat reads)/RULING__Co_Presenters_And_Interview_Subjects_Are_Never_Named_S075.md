## What was put to him

Course 028's V4 rewrite left two rows flagged: 028-001, where the course is co-presented by two people, and 028-038, an interview about the presenter's own early years. V4's rule 3 says the teacher is never named, and separately says a named guest, interviewee or case subject is named in full, and states plainly that whether a co-presenter or an interview subject falls on the first side or the second has not been ruled by Kain directly. Both rows were written on the conservative reading, treating both presenters as the teacher and naming neither, and the reading was shown to Kain in Safari on the five-row page beside the flag.

## Kain's words

> "yes"

Given to the combined question: are the five rows right, and is the conservative reading (neither presenter, nor an interview subject who is also the course's own teacher, is ever named) the one to carry forward.

## What this settles

**A co-presenter is treated as the teacher, not as a named guest, and is never named.** This applies to every course where a second person teaches alongside the primary presenter: Karen on 027, Gerard or Gerry on 011, Becca and Laurie on 014, and any other course where the credited presenter is not one person.

**An interview subject who is also the course's own teacher is treated the same way.** Where the person being interviewed is someone other than the presenter, a genuine outside guest, V4's existing guest rule still applies and they are named in full.

## What this does not settle

A genuine third-party guest, brought in for their own expertise and not otherwise teaching the course, is still named in full. Course 028 rows 043 and 044, where Ian Blackie, a chartered accountant, is consulted, are the worked example and are unchanged by this ruling.
