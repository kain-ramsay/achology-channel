## The ruling

**Asked whether the captions read well enough to leave switched on, he answered: yes.**

Under the S288 ruling that is the "reads well" branch, and its consequence is already written: **the remaining twenty seven courses run with no further caption checking, and the run does not stop for this again.**

Caption display stays on at account level. Nothing is switched off.

## What he actually watched, and why those two

**028-006, `Some Excellent News for All the Rule Breakers`**, because it carries the name Jürgen Habermas. **028-038, `An Interview with Kain: The Heart of Entrepreneurship`**, because it is two speakers in conversation.

Those two were chosen deliberately rather than at random: S288 named proper nouns as the weak point of machine captioning, and two way conversation is the other place it fails. **He was shown the worst case rather than a representative sample.** He passed it.

## What was proved by measurement alongside it

**Vimeo generates a caption track on every replaced lesson, and it is active by default.** Read from the text tracks endpoint on five lessons spread across course 028, every one carrying exactly one track, `en-x-autogen`, type subtitles, active true.

That confirms the Vimeo Support answer from 19 August by measurement rather than by documentation, and it means **about 1,950 lectures that have never carried captions will gain them as the run proceeds.**

## What this does not close

**The glossary pass still stands** as the follow on task on the Vimeo Course Refresh card: a mechanical find and replace over the harvested transcripts against a house glossary, then the corrected tracks pushed back. Kain approving how the captions read today is not a judgement that every proper noun in 2,146 lectures is spelled correctly, and the two are not the same question.

**The 196 lessons that already carried a caption track** remain the drift risk, and their old tracks are still deleted in the same swap. None of them sits in course 028, which carried no existing tracks at all.
