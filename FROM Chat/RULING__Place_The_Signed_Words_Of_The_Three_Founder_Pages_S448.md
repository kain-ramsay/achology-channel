## Founders' Letter (founders-letter)

**The one principle the page unpacks:** What the mind has learned it can learn differently, and people learn that best by working on their own lives alongside others who have gone first.

**Lead line ($ach_policy_lead):** We started Achology in 2017. This founders' letter is our own account of why we did it, what the years since have taught us, and where we're going next.

### Heading: `<h2><span class="policy-next__accent">Achology</span>: Born Out Of Frustration</h2>`

We started Achology because something bothered us both, and it still does. Psychology reaches into every part of an ordinary life: how you see yourself, how you are with the people you love, and how you make the choices that shape your days. Yet most people are never taught a word of it. They learn about their own minds the hard way, by trial and by error, and more often than not on their own.

Most of what people struggle with comes from patterns of thinking, feeling, believing and behaving that they picked up somewhere along the way: from family, from friends, from circumstances and from years of habit. What the mind has learned, it can usually learn differently. Everything at Achology is built on that one observation, and if it doesn't ring true for you, the rest of this letter probably won't either.

CALLOUT:Nobody can grow on another person's behalf. What we can do is build a room where growing comes a little easier, and fill it with people who have gone first.

### Heading: `<h2>How <span class="policy-next__accent">Achology</span> Actually Began</h2>`

It began with one video course in 2012, made to give these ideas a wider room than a lecture hall. By 2014 the Academy of Modern Applied Psychology had grown to 12 online courses, and in 2017 we founded Achology.

What surprised us was where people changed. The courses mattered less than we'd expected, and the people mattered more. Learners told us again and again that the reading helped, but what moved them was conversation, practice, and being challenged by someone who had already done the work on themselves.

So we built for that: live sessions where you work through real situations with a tutor, [practice sessions where members coach each other](/help/events-and-mentorship/achology-recommended-practice-pathway/) and hear afterwards what went well and what didn't, and a community close enough that people know one another and can speak openly.

### Heading: `<h2>What Competence Takes</h2>`

Over the years we stopped treating a certificate as proof of very much. We'd met too many people who held one and still weren't ready to sit with somebody else's difficulty. Competence is harder to show, and it's worth far more.

Our most experienced learners, the [Senior Achologists and Master Achologists](/help/certificates-cpd-accreditation/six-achology-cpd-statuses/), earn it slowly: several [practitioner-level courses](/courses/), dozens of assessed practice sessions, hours of mentorship, completed [ethics training](/about/code-of-ethics/), and years of work with real people. It's meant to be difficult.

What's been missing is a way for anyone else to find them. From 2027 we plan to grow a directory open only to those graduates, so that someone looking for help can find a practitioner whose knowledge, skill and character other people have watched and tested. Nothing here is quick, and nobody here will promise you a result.

### Heading: `<h2>What We Offer Through <span class="policy-next__accent">Achology</span></h2>`

We offer a place to learn for people who want what they study to change how they live. You study at your own pace, then put the ideas to work on the only material any of us really has: your own life, and the lives of the people closest to you. You practise with other members, you hear what they noticed, and you're expected to tell them what you noticed too.

It asks something of you. Take applied psychology seriously and it soon starts examining you as much as you're examining it. Most people feel that as discomfort long before they feel it as useful, and the ones who stay tend to be the ones who let it happen.

We've never tried to be the biggest school of psychology online. We'd rather the people who finish here are worth listening to, so that someone in real difficulty meets a person who lives what they teach.

Our hope hasn't changed since the day we started: that the people who learn here leave more grounded, more thoughtful and more able to add something real to the lives they touch, at work or at home. We're glad you've found our little corner of the internet.

## The Achology Manifesto (manifesto)

**The one principle the page unpacks:** A standard only shapes people when it is said aloud and lived by, so the manifesto is read out before every event and asks whether we live what we teach.

**Lead line ($ach_policy_lead):** The Achology Manifesto is the standard our members read aloud and are expected to live by. Here is what it holds, and why it opens every event we run.

### Heading: `<h2>What the Manifesto Is and Why It Matters</h2>`

Most communities have values. Fewer write them down, and fewer still expect to be held to them. The Achology Manifesto is written so that everyone who learns, teaches or practises here knows what they've agreed to, and is reminded of it often.

It starts from one conviction. People are driven to learn, to direct their own lives and to grow into what they're capable of, and most of what holds them back is something they've come to believe. Anything believed can be looked at again.

Knowing better changes very little on its own. What changes a life is the decisions it leads to: the conversation you finally have, the habit you replace, the work you choose for good reasons rather than familiar ones. Wisdom is what carries a person from knowing better to doing better.

So Achology is built as a community, where people who help others hold one another to a high standard, with the belonging and support that keep them going when the work gets hard. An Achologist's part in it is to act with integrity while helping people think more clearly, choose more wisely and follow through.

Good guidance shouldn't belong only to people who can afford it. Much of our work goes to reaching those who've usually been priced out, because the people with most to gain from it are so often the last to receive it.

The manifesto asks something of us too. We expect to keep growing, to do our work well and to keep becoming better, year on year, while helping others do the same. Read often enough, it keeps asking whether the standard we teach is the one we live by.

### Heading: `<h2>Read Aloud, Every Single Day of the Week</h2>`

Every day, three to five [live online events](/free-events/) run in the Achology community, where members practise what their courses teach through workshops, group discussions, practice sessions and mentoring groups. Each one opens the same way: before the work begins, a member reads the manifesto aloud to the room.

It's a small habit with a serious job. It brings every session back to what Achology is for: to educate and train responsible men and women in the art and craft of teaching, coaching and mentoring, so they can raise up others who'll do the same.

Words said aloud often enough, to people who expect you to mean them, tend to become a culture. That's why we keep saying them.

### Heading: `<h2>Our Commitment to the Code of Ethical Practice</h2>`

A manifesto says what we intend. A code makes us answerable for it. Our Code of Ethical Practice, written by the Society of Modern Applied Psychology ([SoMAP](https://thesomap.org)), rests on Aristotle's idea that good practice grows out of good character, and that character is built through humility and reflection over time.

The [Code of Ethics](/about/code-of-ethics/) shapes our training, sets what is expected of every member, and tells members what they can expect from one another. Shared standards build trust, and a learning community can't work without it.

## Achology's Code of Ethics (code-of-ethics)

**The one principle the page unpacks:** Good practice grows out of good character, so the code trains the person as well as setting the rules.

**Lead line ($ach_policy_lead):** The Achology Code of Ethics is the professional standard every Achologist practises by, and it starts with the person: good practice grows out of good character.

### Heading: `<h2>What the Framework Is and Why It Matters</h2>`

Anyone who helps another person is trusted with something fragile, and trust rarely survives careless practice. So every Achologist trains inside an ethical framework that puts integrity ahead of everything else, results included.

The code cares more about character than about compliance. It sees truthfulness, sincerity and good work as habits to be built, and those habits are what let a practitioner earn trust and make sound decisions when nobody's watching.

Members are encouraged to read the Ethical Framework for Skilled Helpers and Practitioners of Applied Psychology often, and to know it well before they practise with anyone, because a standard you live by every day serves you far better than one you look up when something has gone wrong.

### Heading: `<h2>The Principles That Shape the Standards</h2>`

Ethical standards are older than any profession. Law, religion and philosophy have worked on them for thousands of years, and they tend to arrive at the same place: character shapes conduct.

Aristotle taught that character is built the way a skill is. “We become just by doing just acts, temperate by doing temperate acts, brave by doing brave acts.” For him, virtue was something a person forms through how they choose to act, again and again.

The code asks two things of every member and student: integrity and trustworthiness. Where those hold, trust, good work and credibility usually follow. Carl Rogers, whose work shaped the helping professions, reached the same place from another direction, and found that pretending serves a helper least of all.

QUOTE:“It does not help, in the long run, to act as though I were something that I am not.”|Carl Rogers, On Becoming a Person, 1961

### Heading: `<h2>Two Codes, Two Layers of Integrity</h2>`

An Achologist works under two codes. This one governs the professional layer: the standards that keep a person's practice worthy of trust. Its companion, the {CHAR}, governs the personal layer: the character a practitioner keeps growing long before anyone sits down with them for help.

To anyone new to Aristotle's thinking on virtue, the two codes can look like one. The difference matters to us, because we hold that professional excellence rests on personal excellence, built through growth in character. Members are trained in that standard as part of their membership, so that it becomes part of how they work.
