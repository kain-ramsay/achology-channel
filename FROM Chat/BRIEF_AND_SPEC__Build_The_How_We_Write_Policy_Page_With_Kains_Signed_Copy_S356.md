# How we write

Every page on Achology.com is written for a person who arrived with a question, and every page is owned by a person who can answer for it. This page explains how the writing is done, so you can judge it for yourself.

## We use AI to help us write, and we say so

Achology's articles, book notes, quote pages, workbooks and help answers are drafted with the help of AI writing tools. We use them the way a publisher uses a research assistant and a first-draft writer: to gather sources, to draft, and to check. We do not use them to publish at volume without a person reading what goes out.

## A person approves every page

No page is published because a machine finished it. Each piece is drafted to a written standard, checked by an automated gate for accuracy, sources and readability, and then read and approved by a named person before it goes live. Kain Ramsay, Achology's founder, sets the standard every page is written to and personally approves the example page each type of content is modelled on.

## Facts are sourced, and figures belong to their owners

Where a page makes a claim, the source sits beside it. Course facts, prices and accreditation details are read from the pages that own them rather than retyped, so a number on an article is never out of step with the number at the checkout. Where a page names a study, a book or an organisation, it links to it.

## Two things we do not do

We do not publish content that no person has read. And we do not try to remove the invisible watermark that AI writing tools now place in the text they help produce; it is there so that anyone can ask how a piece of writing was made, and we would rather answer that question than hide it.

## The voices you will read

Achology publishes under a small cast of named editorial voices, each with a consistent way of writing, alongside pieces credited to Achology's instructors in their own voice. A named voice on a page tells you who is responsible for it. Where a page carries no name, it is Achology speaking as an institution, as this page does.

## If something on a page is wrong

Tell us. Every page can be corrected, and a correction is read and approved the same way the page was. Use the [enquiries](/enquiries/) page and name the page and the line.
