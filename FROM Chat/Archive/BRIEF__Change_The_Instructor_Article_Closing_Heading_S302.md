**Part one: the close keeps its heading.** He was shown the article body twice, identical in every respect except whether the final section carried its H2. He chose the version that keeps it.

## Your point 4 is answered, and the answer is not the one you expected

You reported that DSRD 9 §22.6 rules four headed middle sections and an unheaded close, that the eighteen carry five to seven headed sections and a headed close, and that the two disagree.

**They do not, and §22.6 was the thing at fault.** Read closely, §22.6 names its four middle sections as The Idea, Why It Matters, Going Deeper, and Putting It Into Practice. Those are the **book-derived** template's own sections. The S292 ruling was written into a page-level section as though it governed the page rather than the content type, so an instructor article measured against it appears to fail a standard that was never its own.

The instructor article's structure is the Hook-Book-Look-Took arc as a reflective essay (DSRD 2 §3.7, and the `instructor-article` skill), whose understanding movement may run to more than one reader-facing H2. **Five to seven headed sections is the correct output, not a deviation.** Nothing about the eighteen needs reshaping.

**§22.6 now says so**, and carries both halves of Kain's ruling. It is written and filed.
