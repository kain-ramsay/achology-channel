**H1:** What It Means That Life Comes With No Rulebook

**Standfirst:** If you have ever looked at someone else's life and quietly felt you were behind, this quote was written for you: “People live life in different ways because life comes with no rulebook or prescribed way of ‘doing life’ in a purposeful, satisfying, or rewarding way.”

**Body from the provenance paragraphs on:**

This quote by Kain Ramsay was taken from his book, *The Ultimate Life Coaching Handbook*, and its first part helps clarify the second. People are different not because some are wrong, but because there's never been a single correct way for any human being to truly 'be'.

The statement, "People live life in different ways because life comes with no rulebook or prescribed way of 'doing life' in a purposeful, satisfying, or rewarding way," appears to acknowledge this absence as a starting point rather than something to be corrected. Our [comprehensive article on this book](/learn/helping-people/book-notes/the-ultimate-life-coaching-handbook/) provides a more in-depth discussion, if you would like to go deeper.

## What the Quote Might Be Saying

Put simply, this is an argument against comparing your life with other people's, and you have probably done a fair bit of that without noticing. If there were one correct way to live, other people's lives would be useful measuring sticks. You could look across, see where you stood, and adjust. Because there is no such way, the comparison tells you far less than it feels like it does, and a great deal of the worry you carry rests on a rule that nobody ever wrote down.

You are in good company, because psychologists have a name for the habit. Leon Festinger called it [social comparison theory](https://www.simplypsychology.com/articles/social-comparison-theory): when we have no fixed standard to measure ourselves by, we measure ourselves against the people around us instead. It is a very human thing to do, and noticing it is most of the work.

Picture two people of your age from your own town. One has three children and no savings. The other has savings and no children. Neither of them is behind, because they are not on the same road, and a rulebook is the only thing that could have put them on one. The same is true of you and whoever you have been comparing yourself with.

Notice the three words Ramsay chooses: purposeful, satisfying, rewarding. They are three different things. Your life can be strong in one and thin in another without that being a failure, and nobody is keeping score in any case.

## What Can We Take Away From It?

The comforting half of the quote is that you are not doing life wrong. Take a moment with that, because it is easy to skim past. The more demanding half is that nobody can tell you that you are doing it right, either. With no rulebook, there is no authority to check your answer against. You decide what a purposeful life looks like for you, you commit to it, and then you live with having been the one who chose.

That is a heavier thing to carry than it first appears, and it is fine to admit that. A borrowed set of rules, whatever its faults, hands the weight to somebody else. If you followed the expected path and it went badly, the path was wrong. If you chose your own and it went badly, the choice was yours.

This is why so many of us would rather inherit a set of rules than write our own. Inheriting is easier, right up to the day the borrowed life stops fitting and you realise you never actually agreed to it. So here is the gentle takeaway: set your own measure of a life going well, hold it loosely enough to revise it, and be kind to yourself while you work it out, because you are doing something nobody ever handed you instructions for.

## A Question Worthy of an Honest Answer

Before you move on, it is worth sitting with this for a moment, because the answer tends to be more revealing than it first appears. Most of us inherited our measure of a good life long before we were old enough to question it, from parents, teachers, friends and the people we watched growing up. It arrived quietly, and it has been running in the background ever since.

So, whose rules are you measuring your life against, and did you ever agree to them?
