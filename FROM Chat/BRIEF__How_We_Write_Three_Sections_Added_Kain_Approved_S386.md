1. **After** the section "Everything we publish comes from what we teach" (before "Our editorial team approves every page"):

> ## How a page gets from our courses to your screen
>
> Every page starts with a question someone is already asking. Before we write a word, we check that people really do search for it, so we are answering what readers want to know rather than guessing.
>
> Next, we find where our courses already teach the answer. That lesson becomes the source for the page. If our courses don't cover a question, we don't write an answer to it just to fill a gap.
>
> An AI writing assistant then helps turn that teaching into a first draft, following our written standards for tone, length and structure. The draft is checked by an automated system for readability and for any claim that has no source beside it. After that, a member of our editorial team reads the whole page, corrects it and approves it. Only then does it go live.
>
> Pages don't stop there. When a course is updated, or a figure changes, the page that relies on it is updated too.

2. **After** the section "Facts are sourced, and figures belong to their owners" (before "Two things we do not do"):

> ## Why we use AI writing tools at all
>
> We teach thousands of lessons, and every one of them could answer a question somebody has. Writing each answer by hand from nothing would take years, and many of those answers would never be written. AI writing tools let our editorial team turn what we already teach into clear, readable pages much faster.
>
> What AI writing does not do is decide what we believe or what we teach. The ideas come from our courses and from the thinkers we credit by name. The tool helps with the wording. Our people decide whether the wording is right.

3. **Just before** the section "If something on a page is wrong":

> ## What this means for you as a reader
>
> You can read any Achology page knowing where it came from: our own training, checked and approved by people who know it well. Where a page names a study, a book or a thinker, follow the link and read the original for yourself. We would rather you check than simply take our word for it.
>
> If a page helps you and you want to go deeper, the course it came from teaches the full subject, lesson by lesson. That is the difference between reading about an idea and learning to use it.
>
> This is how we write. If you ever want to know more about how a particular page was made, ask us and we will tell you.
