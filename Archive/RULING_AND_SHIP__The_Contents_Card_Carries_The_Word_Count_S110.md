# RULING AND SHIP: the contents card carries the word count, not the reading time

**From Code, S110. Date: Thursday 10 September 2026. Shipped at theme 0.283.0,
deployed and measured on both an article and a book note.**

His words:

> "I've just noticed that in the table of contents component now, there are two
> kind of subheadings, the five sections and six minute read. I think what we
> might be better off doing is actually removing the six minute read and putting
> the word count there instead, that way we're just reducing duplication because
> we've got six minute read twice. Leave a six minute read there, and then
> replace the six minute read from within the article navigation panel with the
> word count instead."

**Owning documents:** DSRD 9's record of the contents card and DSRD 8's component

**Board card:** none of its own; site-wide.

## What is on the page

Under the heading: the section count, then the word count. The reading bar above
the article is untouched and still carries the date, the word count and the
reading time, which is the half of his ruling that asked for nothing to change.

## The two numbers are the same number, and that was the thing to check

The card counts words with the identical expression the reading bar counts with,
not a second expression that agrees most of the time. They now sit on one page in
plain sight, so a card saying 1,176 beside a strip saying 1,178 would be a fault
nobody could explain and everybody would see. Measured after deploy: the book note
reads 1,176 in both places, the article 1,278 in both.

## What he was fixing has moved rather than gone, and he should know that

The duplication he saw was the reading time, said once in the strip and once in
the card. It is gone. But the word count is now the fact said twice, because the
strip carries it too. **This is exactly what he asked for**, and he corrected
himself mid-sentence to keep the strip whole, so it is his call knowingly made
rather than an oversight. It is recorded here because the next person to look at
this card will see a repeated number and want to know whether anybody noticed.

The card is also the better home for it than the strip is, if that question ever
reopens: the strip is a row of facts about the article, and the card is the thing
a reader consults to decide whether to start.
