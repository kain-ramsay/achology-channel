# RULING: the 249 help article addresses stay exactly as they are

**From:** Claude Code, S051. **Date:** 2026-08-08.
**Filed under Harness Rule 14.**
**Closes:** the slug half of `RULINGS__Previews_Link_Ceiling_Keyphrases_S245.md`
§3 and of DSRD 2 §2.24, "Before any keyphrase is set, Code reports how many

**Sits with:** `REPORT__The_80_Point_Keyphrase_Count_S051.md`, which is the count.

## What was put to him

The report's finding, in his own language rather than the mechanism: the plan
was to name each article after the question people actually search for, but the
single word "achology" is the top search term for 64 different articles, and
70% of the articles carrying any data share their top term with another
article. A handful of the top "searches" are error strings that leaked into the
export, and forty rest on one or two people across sixteen months.

Put to him with one recommendation and shaped as a yes or no: leave the 249
addresses alone, because re-slugging buys one Rank Math check, costs every
existing link and redirect, and rests on evidence that does not hold.

## Kain's ruling, his word in full

> "yes, leave them as they are."

## What it settles

**No help article is re-slugged.** The addresses, and every redirect that
points at them, stay as built. This closes the slug question that has been open
since S245 and it is now decided on a real count rather than on an argument.

**What it does not settle**, because he was not asked and it is not his:
whether keyphrases are set at all, and by which route. My recommendation in the
report stands for you to accept or reject: set a keyphrase only where an
article has a distinct query of its own, which is 47 of 158, and let the
opening-line route cover the rest. Nothing has been set either way.

## One consequence worth writing into DSRD 2 §2.24

The section's rule reads as though the top query is per article. On this data
it is very largely per section: people search the brand and land wherever
Google puts them. If §2.24 keeps the top-query route, it needs the qualifier
that a top query shared with other articles is not that article's keyphrase,
or the rule will be re-derived into 64 identical keyphrases by whoever applies
it next.

That is the same shape as the two mechanisms that failed this session: a rule
that is correct on paper and wrong against the machine. Flagging it in the same
terms you used at S254.
