verbatim, including the typing slip in the first line.

## What it rules

**The shape is four parts, not three.** Question line, then a paragraph, then a second
paragraph, then a closing line that names the lesson.

**The closing line says what the lesson or video covers.** This is the part that did not exist
before, and it is the point of the ruling.

## What it overturns in V3, and both are deliberate

**1. The ban on "In this lesson" and "In this video" is lifted.** V3 says, under "The rules the
shape runs on": "The three clichés stay banned. 'In this video', 'In this lesson' and 'Kain
discusses' never appear." Kain's structure line requires one of them by name and his worked
example ends on the other. His word governs. Whether "Kain discusses" is also released is NOT
settled by this ruling and Chat should put it to him when V4 is written.

**2. The 90 to 120 word ceiling is lifted.** V3 says: "Total 90 to 120 words." His example runs
to about 180. The three rows Code rewrote to the new shape and Kain approved by eye run 157,
168 and 185 words.

**Everything else in V3 still binds** and was applied to the approved rows: nothing invented,
the lesson title never repeated inside the description, the Tier 1 machine-written and
marketing word lists, British spelling, and no long dashes.
