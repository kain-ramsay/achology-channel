## What changed, and why it matters for the next 28

Two faults, both about how the piece reads rather than what it says:

**Every section closed on the same line.** Six of seven sections ended "the lessons in this part of the guide go further into X," worded almost identically each time. Vary the close. Sometimes point forward, sometimes just end on the content, sometimes ask something. A reader should not be able to predict the last sentence of a section from the first one.

**Thinkers arrived in a queue rather than one at a time.** The original ran three or four names through a single paragraph with barely a transition between them, Locke then Korzybski then the Pygmalion effect in a few lines. Let one idea or one thinker breathe before the next one arrives. Where two genuinely connect, say so directly rather than just placing them next to each other.

One more thing, found on the second pass, worth carrying into every hub guide from the start: **do not open by diagnosing the reader.** A line like "you're stuck because X" presumes something about who is reading that a hub guide, unlike a personal crisis piece, has no business presuming. A concrete scene works ("replay the last conversation that kept you up"); a verdict about the reader's state does not. Open on a claim about the subject or a scene the reader recognises, never on a claim about them.

The approved record is the pattern. Read it before starting the next one, the same way you'd read any other exemplar.
