## 1. What a course quote is, and why it is new

Every quote page on the site so far takes its quote from a book. A **course quote** takes it from a lecture: Kain's own spoken words, from an Achology course, on the record in the transcript.

**This is safer ground than a book quote, not riskier.** There is no publisher, no edition, no page number, and nobody to misquote. The transcript is the source and the words are Kain's own.

**Two things about the page differ from a book quote, and both are already settled.** The cover image is the course's own cover, not a book cover. And the page's closing source block points at **the course**, not at a book note. **Kain ruled that the quote page carries a course slot**, in a live sitting with Claude Code. Do not design around its absence.

## 3. Step one: the candidate list, before any drafting

**Send back a candidate list first and stop there.** Kain approves it before a single record is written. **Do not draft twenty pages and then ask.**

Aim for about **thirty candidates** so there is room to cut to twenty.

For each candidate, one row: **the line exactly as spoken**, the **lecture key** it came from, and **one sentence on why it stands on its own**.

**The test for a line, and it is the whole job:** it has to make sense to somebody who has never taken the course and never heard the sentence before it. A line that needs its lecture to mean anything is not a quote. **Prefer the line that states something true about people over the line that explains what the course will cover.**

**Spread them.** No more than two candidates from any one lecture, so twenty quotes do not all come from the same forty minutes.

## 4. The tidy rule

**Cut filler and false starts. Never change a word that carries meaning.**

Spoken English carries "you know", "so", "kind of", repeated openings and abandoned half-sentences. Those come out. Everything else stays exactly as said, including grammar a copy editor would tighten.

**If a line has to be rewritten to work, it is not a quote. Drop it and pick another.** There are 2,146 transcripts across the courses and no shortage of lines.

Show the tidy in the candidate list: the line as spoken, and the line as it would be published, wherever the two differ.
