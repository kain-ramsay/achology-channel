## The stance words (copied from the vault, 5 October 2026; the files are `02-Achology/The Achology Stance On Mental Health.md`, `... On Emotional Health.md`, `... On Self Responsibility.md`, `... On Mental Wellness.md`)

**Mental Health.** "Mental health is the condition of a person's inner life, and over time it shows in the life they live. Achology does not treat it as a fixed state a person either has or lacks. It is the running result of how a person sees the world, what they think, what they believe, how they handle what they feel, and what they then do, day after day." "It can be grown, by anyone, at any age. Nobody is too old, too hurt or too far behind to begin." "It begins with self-knowledge. A person cannot change what they cannot see." "Much of what people call poor mental health is a pattern of inaccurate thinking that has never been examined." "A person's quality of life is what shapes how they judge their own mental health... many go to a doctor and come away with a name for it. Achology does not argue with that name and never offers one of its own." "A person is never their condition." "Every person is responsible for their own mental health and emotional wellbeing... Responsibility is about what a person does next. It is never blame for what happened, and it never means doing it alone. Asking for help, including from a doctor, is one of the most responsible things a person can do." **The practitioner definition, carried once in every mental health Help answer, in the answer's own words (Part 17.7):** "A mental health practitioner, as Achology trains one, is someone who has learned this relationship in their own life first. They are not a miracle worker and they heal, fix and cure nobody. They help a person understand their own mind, see where they are and what they want instead, and take responsibility for the next step. They understand people rather than label them." **Scope:** "Achology teaches about the mind, in education. It does not teach about the brain or medicine, nothing it teaches treats any condition, and no course replaces a doctor, a psychologist or a therapist. A person under professional care or taking medication is never asked to stop or change that." **Say:** mental health is the condition of your inner life; it shows in the life you live; it can be grown, by anyone, and it takes time; a thought can be checked; a hard season of the mind is something you are in, not something you are; you are not your condition; a practitioner understands, never labels. **Do not say:** heal, cure, treat, fix or recover from a condition; that any diagnosis is wrong, false or made up; anything about doctors or psychiatry being wrong; anything about the brain except to set it apart from the mind; anything about medication for or against; "mental illness" as a label for a reader; any promise of wellness.

**Emotional Health.** "A feeling is a feeling. It is never an illness, and it is never a fixed part of who a person is. Anxiety, stress, sadness, anger, guilt and shame are emotional states. A state is something a person is in. It is never something a person is." "Every feeling comes from somewhere. It follows from what a person is thinking about, and from the meaning they have given to it." "So a hard feeling is information. It is something to read, never something to obey and never something to cover up." "Emotional reactions are learned, mostly without the person noticing. What was learned can be understood... This is why Achology teaches understanding before techniques." "People can grow out of emotional states that once ruled them. Achology holds this about anxiety as firmly as about any other feeling."

**Self Responsibility.** "Every person alone is responsible for whether they take the steps that would improve their life. Nobody can grow on another person's behalf. No course, no practitioner and no certificate can do it for them." "Responsibility is about what a person does next. It is never about blame for what happened." "Maturity is the point where a person stops making excuses for their results and for how they feel, and starts taking responsibility for the changes they want to see." "This is good news, never a burden. If a person's life is theirs to own, it is theirs to change." "Asking for help is a responsible act. Responsibility never means doing it alone." "This is why no Achology practitioner can promise anyone an outcome."

**Mental Wellness.** "Mental wellness is not something that happens to you. It is the outcome of what you believe about yourself and what you do, day after day. You can only be as mentally well as you believe you are able to learn, solve problems, change, grow and mature." "Feeling anxious is not the problem. Every human being feels anxious at times... The problem starts when someone takes the feeling and turns it into who they are. 'I feel anxious' becomes 'I am an anxious person.'" "This is the heart of mental wellness: knowing that feelings are just feelings. They come, and they go." "So mental wellness is a skill you learn. It is the ability to manage your mindset in the moment, to steady yourself, and to keep control of what you do even when you are feeling something you would rather not feel." "Mental wellness is also the ability to change your mind. About yourself... About your circumstances... About other people." "In the end, mental wellness is a sign of mental maturity." **Do not say:** that a person who is struggling is choosing to, is immature or is to blame; that a person who calls themselves anxious is wrong to, or that anxiety is not real; "just think positive".

## Rule 3 of the foundation, question by question: the common view, and what Achology teaches

Every piece sets the common view beside Achology's wherever they differ (Standard, foundation rule 3, rewritten by Kain S401). Where they do not differ, teach Achology's view and invent no disagreement. Course: 018, the Mental Health Practitioner Diploma Course, named in full from DSRD 5 at first mention, linked, no price.

**Help answers (bring the 20 drafts to the Standard):**
1. How do you become a mental health practitioner? Common view: a clinical role reached through a degree, registration and a job in a health service. Achology: a practitioner is someone who has learned the relationship between mindset, thoughts, emotions and behaviour in their own life first, then helps others understand theirs; competence before any certificate.
2. Do you need a degree? Common view: yes, and the title belongs to clinicians. Achology: no degree is needed to learn and apply this understanding; the fair word on other routes (one or two sentences), then the Achology teaching in full (C27); no statement about any one country's law.
3. What does a practitioner actually do? Common view: assesses, diagnoses, treats. Achology: understands rather than labels; helps a person see where they are, what they want instead, and take the next step; heals, fixes and cures nobody.
4. Therapist or counsellor? Common view: all three are the same clinical family. Achology: never "therapist" for anyone Achology trains; a practitioner educates and helps a person understand their own mind; the fair word on counselling and therapy.
5. Can you train online? Common view: this learning needs a classroom and placements. Achology: understanding your own mind is learned by turning every idea on yourself first, which is done in your own life; online only, never a comparison with in-person study (Kain, S376).
6. Can you study mental health online, and is it worth it? Common view: worth it only if it leads to a job. Achology: worth it first for the person themselves, because mental health begins with self-knowledge and every course leads people towards it.
7. Is a free course with a certificate worth it? Common view: a certificate is the point. Achology: the certificate is never the aim; competence from applying it to yourself is; fair word on free courses.
8. How do you choose a good course? Common view: accreditation badges and hours. Achology: choose one that teaches you to understand your own mind before anyone else's, and that promises no outcome, because nobody can grow on another person's behalf.
9. What do you learn? Common view: conditions, symptoms, signposting. Achology: the chain from mindset to thoughts to emotions to behaviour to quality of life, applied to yourself first; no lesson numbers or titles (Part 13 rule 6).
10. How long does it take? Common view: a fixed term. Achology: the hours are a plain DSRD 5 fact; growing in self-knowledge takes as long as it takes and is never quick (the Mental Health stance says so plainly).
11. How much does it cost, and why? Common view: price decides value. Achology: no price figure in the answer (C24); the reader is pointed to the course page; the "why" is what the learning is for.
12. What can you do with a certificate? Common view: a licence to practise clinically. Achology: it evidences learning; what a person can do rests on competence built through practice; no promise.
13. Accredited or recognised? Common view: a regulator's stamp settles it. Achology: the worldwide regulation stance from Recipe 7 (no one country's law); what recognition means in plain words.
14. Different from first aid? Common view: the same thing. Achology: mental health first aid teaches how to respond to someone in crisis; Achology teaches how the inner life works and how to grow it; both named fairly.
15. Level 2 or level 3? Common view: a UK ladder everyone must climb. Achology: levels are one country's framework; name that fairly and once, then what the learning itself asks of a person (worldwide readers, Kain S393).
16. Udemy or Coursera? Common view: cheaper is the same. Achology: competitors named fairly, differences never deficiencies (Part 18 rule 1); what Achology adds is its frame: you are the first person you study.
17. An introduction for beginners? Common view: start with the conditions. Achology: start with yourself; self-awareness comes before every other kind of growth.
18. A course for managers or teachers? Common view: spot the signs in others. Achology: you can only help others see what you have learned to see in yourself first (the practitioner goes first).
19. What is a mental wellbeing course, and who is it for? Common view: relaxation and self-care. Achology: mental wellness is the outcome of what you believe about yourself and what you do; a skill learned, not a feeling chased.
20. Can you train as a wellbeing coach or practitioner online? As 5 and 20 combined: never "therapist"; helping is never treatment; a guide, not a guru.

**Articles (draft the 9; type hub question article, Part 17.1; Evelyn Montgomery; 1,500 to 2,000 words; one piece of checked human wisdom from the library named in each record's brief before drafting, Part 4; a Sources list where outside sources are used, Part 11; the help line in the pilot's approved international shape where the subject touches wellbeing, Part 7 rule 15):**
21. Mental health or mental wellbeing: what is the difference? Common view: two words for one thing, or wellbeing is the soft version. Achology: mental health is the condition of the inner life; mental wellness is the outcome of what you believe and do, and a sign of maturity.
22. How do you improve your mental wellbeing, day to day? Common view: habits that make you feel better (sleep, exercise, breathing). Achology: start with the next feeling and the next choice about what to do with it; feelings are just feelings; never "just think positive"; no breathing or savouring as the teaching.
23. Can mental health improve without therapy or medication? Common view: either it needs treatment or it will not change. Achology: the inner life can be grown by anyone, by understanding one's own mind; nothing here is advice to stop or avoid a treatment chosen, and asking for help is responsible (Part 7 rule 4: labelled as Achology's view, beside expert consensus).
24. Is mental health genetic, or shaped by how you live? Common view: it is in your genes. Achology: no claim about the brain or genetics (mind, not brain); what Achology teaches is the part a person's thinking, beliefs and choices play, and that this part is theirs to work on; the research named fairly and labelled.
25. Illness, spectrum or continuum? Common view: you are either ill or well. Achology: a running result, not a fixed state; a hard season is something you are in, not something you are; Achology argues with no diagnosis and offers none.
26. Why does mental health matter so much in everyday life? Common view: it matters when something goes wrong. Achology: the quality of the inner life produces the quality of the outer one; work on the inside first and circumstances tend to follow.
27. Should mental health be taught in schools, and how? Common view: teach the conditions and the warning signs. Achology: teach how a mind works, that a thought can be checked and a feeling has a cause; the Self Awareness ground; no claim about any country's curriculum policy.
28. Can mental health affect your body, sleep and energy? Common view: a medical cause-and-effect. Achology: no medical claims; what a person thinks and believes shapes what they feel and do, including rest and energy; sourced research named fairly, nothing about the brain.
29. Is mental health getting worse, and if so, why? Common view: a crisis caused by the world. Achology: what happens outside and what a person makes of it are two different things; the research on prevalence named fairly with its limits; Achology's answer labelled as its own.
