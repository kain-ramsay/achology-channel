# RULING: the pronoun "i" is capitalised in student reviews (Kain, S059)

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** not a page spec.
**From:** Claude Code, S059. **Date:** 2026-08-15.
**Filed under:** Harness Rule 14, a ruling given directly in a Code session, acted on and filed the same session.
**Belongs with:** `REPORT__Reviews_Editorial_Pass_Two_Applied_S059.md` section 9, which put the question, and `COMMISSION__Reviews_Editorial_Pass_Two_All_897_S264.md`, whose authorised list this extends.

## The ruling, in Kain's words

The question put to him was that 298 reviews wrote the pronoun "i" in lower case mid-sentence, that capitalising it was not one of the six authorised fixes, and that the result was reviews reading "This course is helping me. i learned a lot."

His answer, quoted in full:

> "Yes, please do Claude - that is just a good use of English, thank you for catching this."

**So the authorised list for the review bank now has a seventh fix: the pronoun "i" is capitalised.** It sits within the ruled principle rather than beside it: the same word the student wrote, correctly cased, nothing added and nothing removed.

**Two exclusions, both found by reading the output rather than by trusting the rule.**

1. **Non-English reviews are skipped entirely, all 87 of them.** In Italian "i" is the definite article. The first dry run turned "lo seguo con i sottotitoli" into "con I sottotitoli", which corrupts the sentence. The pronoun rule is an English rule and is now applied only where the review is English. Verified after the write: review 32792 still reads as its author wrote it.
2. **"i.e." is excluded**, which is six reviews. It is not the pronoun.

Verified after the write: 11 reviews still contain a lone lower-case "i", and they are exactly those two exclusions, 5 non-English and 6 carrying "i.e.". No English pronoun was missed.

## One defect in my own guard, recorded because it is the third of its kind

The first version of the pass refused 215 of its own correct changes. Its safety check compared the two texts with every "i" masked out, which meant any review that already contained a capital "I" failed it. The counts looked like a cautious pass working; it was a broken check rejecting valid work.

It is the same family as the green test that cannot fail, in its opposite form: a guard that is wrong in the safe direction still hides the truth, and it was only visible because the before-and-after pairs were printed and read. The guard now compares position by position and permits exactly one transformation, a lower-case i becoming an upper-case I, and nothing else.

## What this does not do

It does not touch spelling, grammar, or any other casing. "ken" and "udemy" in lower case, and the rest of what the six fixes leave alone, are untouched and remain outside the authorised list.
