## Why it was hanging off in the first place

It was hard against the tile's edge on purpose: that is the mini card component's
own watermark geometry, from its build sheet, where a large mark deliberately
runs off the side of a card. Reusing an approved pattern was the right instinct
and the wrong result. **A mark that bleeds off a 96 tall card reads as bled; the
same mark on a 60 tall tile reads as cut.**

This is the third time today the same shape of fault has come up on this one
glyph, which is why it is worth a line rather than a shrug: it was sliced by the
tile's overflow at the top edge this morning, it landed on the first word of
every quote when it moved to the left, and now it was hanging off the side. The
lesson each time is the same. **A mark is placed against the smallest thing it
will ever sit in, not the largest.**

## What is on the page

8 in from the left edge, centred on the tile, 38 square at 12 per cent.

**38 rather than 46, and the number comes from the shortest tile in the set.** A
one line quote stands about 44 inside its padding, and a mark centred in that has
three to spare above and below. Any larger and the tile that clips is the short
one, which is the tile nobody looks at while checking.

## Read back, and it is a machine check rather than an eye

At 1440, 900 and 390, for every one of the three tiles: the mark's box sits
inside the tile's box on all four sides. Desktop leaves 19 clear above and below
and 9 to the left; the phone, where the tiles are shorter, leaves 11 and 11 and
9. Nothing is clipped, the card is untouched at its ruled 640.
