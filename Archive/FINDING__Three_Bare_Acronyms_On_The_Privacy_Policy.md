# FINDING: three acronyms on the Privacy Policy are never spelled out

**From:** Claude Code, S047. **Date:** 2026-08-05. **Theme:** v0.38.16.
**Page:** https://achologytest.com/policies/privacy-policy/ , page 1 of the S047 walk.
**Needs:** replacement copy from Chat. Rule 8 puts every published word with you.

## The standard

DSRD 6 section 1, quoted from the canonical document read this turn:

> "**Acronyms carry one extra step.** An acronym may be used only after its full canonical name has already appeared in the page's visible text, with the acronym in brackets at that first appearance: "Virtual Achologist Led Training Sessions (VALTS)". Every use after that, on the same page, may be the acronym alone. This holds for Achology's own terms and outside ones alike, so NLP, CBT and CPD are spelled out on first use exactly as VALTS and PALS are. Outside terms in common public use need the full name only, unless the page turns on what that term means; Achology's own terms always take the identification as well."

And the reason the chapter gives for it:

> "**One new rule lives here: every page is a front door, so nothing is left to guesswork.** Readers arrive cold from a search result or an AI answer, having read nothing else on the site and never having heard of Achology.com."

## What passes

Four acronyms on this page are handled exactly right, and are the model for the fix:

- "the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR)"
- "the EU General Data Protection Regulation (EU GDPR)"
- "the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003 (PECR)"
- "the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO)"

## What fails

Three appear bare and are never spelled out anywhere in the page's visible text:

| Acronym | Where it appears, verbatim from the live page | What the rule wants |
|---|---|---|
| **HMRC** | "including law enforcement agencies, courts, and regulatory bodies (such as HMRC), where disclosure is required or reasonably necessary to comply with..." | An outside term in common public use, so it needs the full name at first use |
| **CV** | "we may collect personal data when you: submit a CV or application materials; or provide information relevant to an employment..." | Same |
| **IT** | "suppliers who provide services on our behalf, such as marketing support, IT services, business administration, and technical support" | Same |

Not counted, because the chapter's chrome carve-out exempts them: CPD and IQ, both of which appear only in the mega menu, where "a short form such as CPD, FAQs or SoMAP may stand alone in a chrome label". Also not counted: ALL, BIG, FREE and LIVE, which are emphasis in chrome rather than acronyms.

## What would close it

Three approved replacement sentences, or approved wording for the three phrases, and I will place them verbatim and re-gate the page.

One question for you while the copy is being written, since the answer changes how the rest of this walk reads: the rule as written catches ordinary business English, and CV and IT are the clearest examples of that. If the intent is that genuinely everyday abbreviations sit outside the rule, that is a distinction DSRD 6 section 1 does not currently draw, and drawing it explicitly would stop it being re-argued on every page that mentions a CV. HMRC I would fix regardless: it is a UK-specific body name that a reader outside the UK will not know, which is exactly the front-door failure the chapter was written to stop.
