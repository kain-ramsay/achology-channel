So does anything here make automated decisions about you? No. Achology does not use personal data for automated decision-making or profiling that produces legal effects, or similarly significant effects, on individuals, as those terms are defined under UK GDPR. Decisions about access, participation and services are made with human involvement. The Privacy Notice states this directly.

What the two terms actually mean

They sound interchangeable and are not.

Automated decision-making is a decision made about you entirely by a system, with nobody reviewing it and nobody able to override it. Automated credit scoring and algorithmic job-application screening are the familiar examples.

Profiling is the automated analysis of personal data to evaluate or predict something about you, such as your behaviour, interests or likely choices.

UK GDPR gives people specific protections wherever either one produces legal or similarly significant consequences, which is the threshold this answer turns on.

What Achology's position covers

No algorithm alone decides whether you can enrol, progress or reach any content, and no system scores or categorises you in a way that carries legal or similarly significant consequences.

Standard website analytics and cookies operate for performance and security, described in the Cookie Policy, and are a separate matter from making decisions about individuals.

The one place a judgement is genuinely made about you is the verification of professional development claims, and that is deliberately the opposite of automated: every claim is reviewed by committee members who are experienced practitioners, which is exactly why the record means anything to somebody outside Achology.

Why this is worth stating at all

Because in online education a great deal now is automated: what gets recommended to you, what a platform predicts you will buy, and in some cases who is quietly filtered out. Saying plainly that none of that happens here is only useful if it is checkable, which is why it sits in the published Privacy Notice rather than in marketing copy.

It also fits the wider position: personal data is not sold, and it is shared only with the parties needed to run the service.

Your rights if this ever changed

If processing of this kind were ever introduced, the UK GDPR protections would apply, including the right to obtain human intervention, to express your point of view and to contest a decision.
