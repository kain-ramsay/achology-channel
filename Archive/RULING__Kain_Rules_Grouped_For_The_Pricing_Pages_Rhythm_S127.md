# RULING: Kain rules Grouped for the pricing page's rhythm

**From Code, S127. Theme session. Shipped v0.584.0 and v0.585.0, deployed and
verified at desktop and phone.**

---

## 1. What he ruled

The page's rhythm was the last design item on this page in his own running
order, deferred at S126 until the page was built. It was put to him as four
rendered ways the page could breathe, each changing exactly one thing, one on
screen at a time. He ruled:

> "Go with grouped"

## 2. What Grouped is

**The page is three movements rather than six blocks:** how to buy, then what
there is to buy, then everything after the decision. A separator marks a
movement and nothing else, so two remain, at the course list and at the
membership panel. The other three junctions meet on air alone, at half the
distance a separated junction carries.

**Nothing in this sitting moved the air around a separator, and that was
settled before the options were built rather than checked after.** DSRD 7
section 4.3, and Kain's own standing rule restated across several sessions,
fixes 48 above and 48 below, and 32 on a phone. Every option was built to obey
it, which is why the choice in front of him was about where a line belongs and
never about how much air it sits in.

## 3. The three that lost are deleted

They are in the theme repository's history at v0.584.0. **Even** was the page
as built, the control, with an identical junction at all five; a rhythm can
only be judged against the one already in front of him, which is why the
control was on the bar rather than described. **Quiet** dropped the separator
wherever a tinted panel edge already marked the boundary, which left one line
on the whole page. **Framed** made the opposite argument: put every flat block
on the ground the other three already wear, and the page needs no separators at
all.

**One thing Framed exposed is worth keeping.** It could not frame the questions
block without tinting a tint, because that block is already a set of framed
cards with their own tinted headers. A block made of objects does not take a
ground the way a block of flat content does, and that is a general fact about
this page's six rather than a fault in one option.

## 4. The fold-in changed the base rule, on purpose

The winning option won wearing a class the shipped page does not have. Folded
in as a bare class it would have tied with the rule it had just beaten and
drawn nothing, which is exactly what cost a round at S126. So the page's own
rule changed rather than gaining a layer on top of it, **and the result was
measured on the rendered page after the fold-in and not only before it:** two
hairlines, each carrying 48 above and 48 below at desktop and 32 at phone,
identical to the option he chose.

## 5. The fold-back

The prototype in the Pricing Page folder is the ruled page, exported this
session, per Harness Rule 14. This page has no build sheet of its own: it is a
page rather than a component, and its design folder's prototype is what
governs.
