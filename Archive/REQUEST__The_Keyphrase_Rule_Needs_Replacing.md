## The option

**Take the phrase from the article's own opening paragraph rather than from its title.**

The opening already restates the reader's question and lands the answer, in the house voice, in copy you and Kain approved. So the shortest run of two to six words in it that names the subject is a phrase that: reads like something a person types, exists verbatim in the article, and was written by you rather than derived by me. Selection, not invention, which is the line I will not cross alone.

Against your own worked example the two rules agree: "How do I cancel my Achology membership?" gives "cancel your Achology membership" from the opening, against "cancel Achology membership" from the title. That agreement is why the title rule looked sound when it was written.

Against a case where they disagree: "What are the major milestones in Achology's history?" The title rule gives "major milestones Achology's history", which scores 8 and appears nowhere in the article. The opening gives "Achology's history".
