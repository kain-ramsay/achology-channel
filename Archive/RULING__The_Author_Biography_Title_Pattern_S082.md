# RULING: the title pattern for every author biography

**Ruled by:** Kain, in the Code S082 sitting, 25 August 2026.
**Applied:** 50 of the 51 live drafts, verified off the install.
**Board card:** the author biographies.

---

## His pattern, in his own words

> Who is Carl Jung? His (Her for Females) Biography, Ideas and Life Works

Copied character for character. Nothing about the wording was edited, tidied or improved.

## What it replaced, and why he changed it

The article title was the bare name, "Carl Jung". Code raised that this reads as a missing title rather than a short one wherever the title travels: in the Related Further Reading list it sits among real article titles, and it appears the same way on cards and in the breadcrumb.

**Kain first asked whether the existing search titles should become the article titles.** Code advised against taking them whole: "Who Is Carl Jung? Life, Ideas and Legacy Explained" is a results-page string, and "Legacy Explained" is doing nothing for a reader already on the page. He then wrote the pattern above himself.

**The search titles are unchanged and stay as they are.** Two different phrasings reach more queries than the same words twice.

## The pronoun is read, never inferred

**His and Her come from Wikidata's P21 on the same entity the portrait came from, not from the first name.** Publishing the wrong pronoun for a living person on their own biography is a mistake nobody catches until that person reads it, and a set of 51 contains A. C., Thich Nhat, Gabor and Nassim alongside Judith and Brené.

Two women were returned across the 51: **Brené Brown and Judith S. Beck.**

**One row is deliberately not written.** Wikidata has no entry for Kain Ramsay, so his own biography still carries the bare name. Code refused to assume it rather than guess correctly by luck. One word from him closes it.

## What Chat is asked to do

**Carry the pattern into the records.** The site and the records now disagree: the drafts carry the new titles and the 51 records still carry the bare name, so a rebuild of the upload CSV would revert every one of them. That is the only thing making this ruling fragile.

The rule is mechanical, so it needs no judgement: `Who is {post_title}? {His|Her} Biography, Ideas and Life Works`.

## Two things worth Chat's eye while it is in there

- **All 51 search titles say "Who Is", including Aristotle, Freud, Nietzsche and Tolstoy.** "Who Was" is right for about half the set. Raised at S082 and not ruled; Kain chose "Who is" for the article titles knowingly, and the search titles were not discussed.
- **One search title runs to 62 characters**, Brené Brown, and may clip in results.
