**That is the ruling and it was acted on in the same sitting.** His S103 ruling stands behind it unchanged: the articles publish at 89 and are never padded to cross the length step.

## 2. What Code did NOT do with it, and this matters more than what it did

**The gate was not loosened.** `search_gate.py`'s `SCORE_BAR_BY_TYPE` still holds DSRD 6's site-wide 90, with the help answer's 81 and the book note's 88 beside it and no third entry. Kain's own standing instruction is that Code does not write a bar on its own word, and his word tonight was "publish them", not "change the bar".

**The road taken instead is the one built for this.** `publish_gate.py --override` clears a refused page on Kain's ruling, quoted in his own words and stored on the clearance. It skips no check: every page was measured exactly as it would have been, every refusal is written onto the clearance by name, and the record shows what was overruled rather than a clean pass that never happened.

**So the 89 is still not written anywhere as a standard, and it still needs to be.** Until DSRD 6 item 11 carries it, every future batch of this type will refuse for the same reason and need the same override, and an override is a debt rather than a rule. **This is the thing Chat still owes.**

## 3. The eighteen refusals, and why they are one fact

Every draft refused the gate on the same eighteen checks. `publish_gate.py`'s own comment names the cause exactly, and it is worth quoting because it is the reason the override exists at all:

> "This gate certifies a page by measuring the page the public sees. Every Knowledge Hub batch is imported as a draft by Kain's own S300 ruling, so its address returns 404 and the gate fails it on eighteen checks that are really one fact: there is nothing there to measure yet."

Measured rather than assumed: the exemplar refused on eighteen before publication and, re-measured the moment it was live, those eighteen fell away.
