# RULING: every profile bio takes the same shape, all ten, and the shape already exists. Eight bios are outside it.

**From:** Claude Code, Session 097. **Date:** 3 September 2026. **Session type:** factory.
**Ruled by:** Kain, in the S097 sitting, after reading a profile page.
**His words:** *"the bios on these pages must be the same shape for everyone"*, and when the two real people were put to him as a possible exception, *"same shape for all ten"*.
**Filed under Harness Rule 14.**
**Board card:** the Our People page card.

---

## The ruling

**One shape for every profile bio, with no exception for the two real people.**

## The shape already exists, measured off the ten live pages

Kain's reading was that each bio takes a different shape. The measurement says something more useful: **eight of the ten already share one shape, and they are remarkably tight to it.**

| | paragraphs | words | shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| The eight pen names | 3, every one | 99 to 117 | short, long, short |
| Kain Ramsay | 2 | 80 | long, short |
| Gerard Egan | 1 | 62 | long only |

Every one of the eight opens with a short paragraph of 20 to 27 words, carries a middle of 57 to 66, and closes on 22 to 30. Every one of the ten carries exactly one link.

**So there is nothing to design.** The shape is: **three paragraphs, roughly 20 short, 60 long, 25 short, about 100 to 115 words in total, one link.** It has simply never been written down, which is why nothing protects it and why the two pages written outside it drifted with nobody noticing.

## What follows, and who owns each part

**Chat writes the shape down**, into the document that owns the Our People page, as a standard with those numbers in it. Until it is written it is a habit, and a habit is what allowed two pages to leave it.

**Chat or Cowork rewrites the bios that are outside it.** That is eight pieces, not two, and the count is the point of the next section. It is writing, so it is not Code's under Rule 8.

**Code adds it to the machine checks once it is written down**, and it is a good candidate: a paragraph count, three word bands and a link count are all things a machine counts exactly. It cannot judge whether a bio is any good, and the standard should say so rather than pretending the check is the whole test.

## Eight, not two, because six more are coming

Kain asked in the same sitting for the six Community Eldership members to get profile pages. **They are already in the theme's people registry**, with their names, their role line, their portraits and their bios, all his own words given at S062 and S296. They are switched off by one flag each, set when he ruled at S062 that those cards link nowhere for now.

**Every one of the six bios is a single paragraph.** So switching them on today would put six more pages on the site outside the shape he has just ruled, on the day it was ruled. **The writing comes before the pages, not after.**

## What blocks Code from creating those six pages, reported rather than worked around

Each profile is a real WordPress page, a child of Our People at page 182, carrying `template-author-profile.php`, with the registry supplying its content by matching the slug. Read off the install this session.

Harness Rule 8 allows Code to create pages as drafts from an enumeration, and the registry is one: six names, six slugs, one fixed parent, one fixed template. **H9 refused it.** The publishing wall treats `wp post create` as a publishing verb and cannot tell a draft from a live page, which is deliberate: unknown is a refusal.

**The route it offers, minting a publish-gate clearance, is not the answer**, because a clearance for six empty drafts would be passing a gate on pages that have nothing in them yet. That is gaming a wall built after 116 pages went live unchecked, and it is not done.

**So the six pages are Kain's to create, six at a time in WordPress, once their words exist.** That is the honest answer to the question he asked, and it is not a limitation worth changing H9 for: he creates a page in a few clicks and the wall is protecting something real.

**The six slugs, so nothing is guessed when he does:** `alec-wells`, `andrew-nelson`, `erika-nadeau`, `gaby-tzeschlock`, `gary-kennedy`, `jonathon-frost`. Each a child of Our People, each on the author profile template. Code flips the six registry flags in the same change set, once the pages exist, so the hub cards start linking on the same day rather than pointing at nothing.
