What mindfulness is at Achology

Mindfulness is the discipline of looking inward. It is being aware of yourself in the present moment: how you are conducting yourself, and the reasons behind it. It is a blend of self-awareness, self-reflection, self-knowledge and self-understanding, and it exists for one purpose: to understand yourself well enough to take greater responsibility for living a mature, productive and responsible life.

Where this comes from in the courses

The course lessons say it in their own words. Lesson 10 of Mindfulness for Mental Health, Personal Growth and Inner Peace opens with "Mindfulness is a form of self-awareness. It's not religious, mystical, or magic." Lesson 3 teaches the Mindfulness Full-Cycle Model for self-awareness, lesson 4 contrasts conscious awareness with autopilot, lesson 47 is The Seven Decisions of Maturity and Character, and lesson 49 asks you to trace your own part in your outcomes. In the Mindfulness Practitioner Diploma Course, Section 2 is Mindfulness for Self-Awareness and Perceptiveness, Section 3 is Mindfulness for Self-Discipline and Personal Growth, lessons 66 to 68 teach the Six Stages of Personal Reflection, lesson 69 the Seven Choices that Lead to Maturity and Character, and lesson 112 the Five Degrees of Self-Reflecting. Lesson 2 says outright that mindfulness is much more than being at peace, zoning out or turning off the mind.

The Achology Stance On Self Responsibility gives the purpose: nobody can grow for you, maturity is where you stop making excuses and start owning your part, and responsibility is always an invitation and good news, never blame.

What Achology does not teach as mindfulness

Breathing practices, meditation, body scans, tasting or savouring food, relaxation, and being at peace or zoning out. Meditation appears in the courses only as a contrast: lesson 13 of the Diploma Course and lesson 12 of Mindfulness for Mental Health both set mindfulness against meditation and describe them as different things. Two passing phrases (lesson 4 of the Diploma Course lists "physical sensations" among the things to notice, and lesson 21 of Mindfulness for Mental Health says "take a deep breath") are not teaching of a breathing or body practice, so no article builds on them.

How it differs from most mindfulness teaching

Much of what is published centres on calm, relief from stress and accepting things as they are. Achology centres on understanding yourself so that you can take responsibility for your life and mature. Where an article describes what other teaching says, it says so once, fairly, from that teaching's own pages, and never as a put-down.

The rules every article and Help answer is checked against

  • The answer to the question is given from this frame first. The course is the frame of the article, never a small section at the end.
  • Outside sources appear only to show how other teaching describes the subject, labelled as other teaching.
  • No instruction to breathe, meditate, scan the body, eat or taste something, relax, or slow down for calm, anywhere, including openings and closing practices.
  • Scenes and closing practices are about looking at yourself: a question to ask, a reason to find, a decision to own, drawn from the course's self-reflection lessons.
  • Every course fact comes from the lesson list and lesson description of the course named, read in the same session.
  • Responsibility is offered as an invitation and good news, never as blame, never as a promise of an outcome, and the mind is never confused with the brain.
  • Only courses 015 and 016 are named, and Achology does not claim to train mindfulness teachers.
