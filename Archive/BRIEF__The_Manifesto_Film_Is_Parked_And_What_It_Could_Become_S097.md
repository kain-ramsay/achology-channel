## The visual language, which is the thing worth keeping

It was arrived at by Kain rejecting the first version outright: *"you've kinda spliced together some videos, and you've followed the script, but this isn't really video editing."* He then pointed at Achology's own 2022 promo, and watching that settled it.

**The white is the film.** Everything else lives inside it. Footage is a component in a design, never the canvas. Type is the main event, large, in Como, with the important words heavier and Achology orange while everything around them steps back. Whole frames go solid orange or charcoal as punctuation. Footage is drained almost to black and white so the only real colour left anywhere is orange, which is what stops eighteen stock clips looking like eighteen stock clips.

**Nothing cuts and nothing is ever still.** Every join dissolves, only backgrounds cross, type never dissolves into type, and every frame carries slow movement: the words drift one way and the Achology mark drifts the other behind them.

## Why this is worth a skill, and Kain's own point

His words: if we can agree what one perfect, finally approved film is, we can write a skill from it and use it for a range of other films, **course promotion films being the obvious first use.**

He is right, and the economics are the argument. **The expensive part of tonight was not the film, it was the language.** Roughly a dozen rounds of him rejecting things and me correcting them produced a set of rules that now sit in one place. A second film built to those rules costs a fraction of the first, and a twentieth costs almost nothing.

**So the skill should not be written yet.** It is written from a finished film Kain has approved whole, not from a good opening. That is the sequencing, and it is his.
