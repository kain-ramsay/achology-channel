# RULING AND SHIP: the small quote cards carry their mark on the left, and their words take more room at the sides

**From Code, S110. Date: Thursday 10 September 2026. Shipped at theme 0.265.0,
deployed and read back on the live page at 1440 and 390.**
**Ruled by Kain** live in this sitting. His words:

> "could you move the glyph to the left had side of the small cards, and also add
> a little bit extra padding to the left and right side of the text container
> please?"

**Owning document:** the quote page's build sheet in its design folder.
**Board card:** Quote page template.

---

## The two halves of that are one thing

The mark was on the right for one version, and only because on the left, at the
padding the tile then had, it landed on the first word of every quote. **The
extra padding he asked for in the same breath is what makes the left possible:**
the mark now sits in the margin that padding opens, and the words start clear of
it. Asked for separately, they only work together, which is why they shipped
together.

The tile now carries 18 at its sides against 12 at its top and foot.

## What it cost, and how it was paid

**The text box narrowed from 151 to 139, so fewer characters fit on a line.** This
column only ever offers quotes short enough to show whole, and how long that is
was measured on the render rather than calculated. So it was measured again: the
limit went from 69 characters to 67.

**The cap stays at 62 rather than dropping to 60 to restore the old margin, and
that is a trade made with open eyes.** Of the 25 quotes on the install only two
sit under 60, so a cap of 60 would leave fewer than three eligible on every page
and the block would be absent across the whole bank. 62 is proved on the render
instead, and the three-line clamp is the seatbelt behind it.

**The instruction to re-measure was already written into the template and the
build sheet before this change,** and keeping it is the only reason the number is
still true. That is the whole point of writing a re-measure trigger down rather
than remembering one.
