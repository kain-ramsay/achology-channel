# RULING: the twenty six replaced covers are approved, and best available is the standard for an old book

**From:** Claude Code, Session 089. **Date:** 31 August 2026.
**Ruled by:** Kain, in the sitting, on the before and after sheet in Safari.
**Filed under Harness Rule 14**, in the same session the ruling was given.
**Reads with:** `REPORT__Every_Book_Cover_Is_Sourced_And_Rendering_S089`.

---

## 1. The ruling, in his own words

> "I think these are all good now Claude, many are old books, lo just use the best ones you've been abto to find."

Given on the sheet showing all twenty six placeholders beside the real printings that replaced them, after he had found four of the placeholders himself.

## 2. What it settles

**The twenty six swaps stand.** No cover goes back.

**And it sets a standard wider than these twenty six**, which is why it is filed as a ruling rather than as an approval: for an old book, the best available real printing is the answer, and the page is not held open waiting for a better one. A photograph of a dark Victorian cloth binding is dim, and it is the book, and it is preferred to a cleaner picture that is not.

**What it does not do is licence a generated cover.** What he approved is a real printing that is imperfect. Apple's placeholder, a gradient with the title typed on it, stays refused: that is the fault he raised, and this ruling closes it rather than reopening it.

## 3. The four still open, and they stay open

Plato's `apology`, `gorgias`, `meno` and `phaedo` still carry the placeholder. Every edition found for each is an anthology carrying seven other dialogues, and an anthology cover on a single dialogue's page is the same class of error as the placeholder: a picture that is not the thing. A Meno anthology cover was taken during the run and reverted for exactly that reason.

None of the four is live. They are named rather than quietly carried.

## 4. What Chat is owed, and it is a judgement rather than a job

Nothing, unless DSRD 8 section 20.2 should carry the best-available rule.

That section says a missing cover is a data error that blocks the page. It says nothing about **what counts as a cover**, and that silence is the gap thirty placeholders walked through: every one of them satisfied the specification exactly. Whether Kain's sentence above belongs in 20.2 is Chat's call.
