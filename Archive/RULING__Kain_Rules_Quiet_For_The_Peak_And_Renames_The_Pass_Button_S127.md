# RULING: Kain rules Quiet for the page's peak, and gives the pass card's button his own words

**From Code, S127. Theme session. Shipped v0.586.0 and v0.587.0, deployed and
verified on the rendered page.**

---

## 1. How this one started, and it matters for the record

Not from a brief, and not from Code proposing a change. Kain asked, on the whole
page rendered in Safari:

> "Can I ask you to look at the page and tell me if you really like its layout?
> What do you like about the layout? What don't you like about the layout? And
> what can you see needs to be improved?"

**Harness Rule 8 says Code never critiques work Kain has designed and approved.
He set that aside by asking directly, which is his to do**, and the assessment
was given straight. The thing named first was that the page has no peak: every
block weighs about the same, and the most valuable product on it is the eighth
cell of a grid, at the size of a school costing a third of it. He asked for four
options on exactly that.

Three other things were named in the same answer and he has not acted on them,
so they are open and they are his: the middle of the page runs long and unbroken
from the course heading to the membership panel; the saving is the quietest
thing on the school cards, so the card whispers its best argument and shouts its
second best; and the page ends on the same four cards it opens with, word for
word.

## 2. What he ruled on the peak

> "Go with Quiet"

**Quiet is the only one of the four that does not touch the pass at all.** The
pass was already the only dark card in the block; what stopped it being the peak
was that the seven around it were just as loud. The seven now take an outlined
action and the pass keeps its filled one, so there is exactly one solid button in
the block of eight, and it is on the thing the page most wants chosen.

**The finding worth carrying: the peak was made by quietening everything else,
not by making the peak bigger.** He was shown three ways to enlarge the pass and
took the one that left it alone.

## 3. The three that lost are deleted

They are in the theme repository's history at v0.586.0. **Stage** gave the pass
the whole width of the block beneath the seven; **Lift** gave it the whole width
above them, so the block opened on everything and then offered parts of it;
**Apart** was Stage standing clear of the grid on its own air.

**All three widened the card, and that is where the trap was.** The pass card's
ticket is sized as a share of its card, and Kain settled that drawing by eye over
four passes at the card's usual width. A full-width card would have taken it from
168 across to 298 and undone his own ruling silently, which is the same
arithmetic that bit this page at S126. Every widening option therefore named the
size he settled, and it was measured on all five renders rather than trusted: 168
on the control and 168 on each option, with the same air above and beside it.

## 4. His copy for the button

Given directly in the sitting and typed as he gave it, capitals and all:

> "in the Access All Areas card, please replace Enrol Now in the CTA button with
> Unlock All courses"

It replaces a label that was also his: at S125 he put Access This School on the
seven school cards and on nothing else, and Enrol Now stayed on the pass because
that card is not a school.

**This card only, and the exception is named rather than left as a surprise.**
The dark Access All Areas band further up the same file still reads Enrol Now. It
is not called on the page, by his own S125 ruling, and is kept only as the thing
the card treatments were compared against. One word from him and it follows.

## 5. The fold-in and the fold-back

Folded in on the page's own base selector rather than as a copy of the option's
class, because the rule it has to beat is the shared button rule and a bare copy
would have tied and drawn nothing. Measured on the rendered page after the
fold-in: seven outlined actions, one filled, and the filled one reads Unlock All
courses.

The prototype in the Pricing Page folder is the ruled page, exported this
session, per Harness Rule 14.
