# RULING AND SHIP: the help answer's polish pass, and the five items declined

**From Code, S110. Date: Thursday 10 September 2026. Shipped at theme 0.311.0,
gated, and measured in both browser engines at three widths.**
**Routed in by Kain**, who passed a written review of this page from another
assistant and asked me to "implement whichever fit the existing template and
design standards" and to "explain any recommendations you decide against".

**Owning documents:** DSRD 6 section 7 for the accessibility items, DSRD 7
section 12.4 for the touch floor. DSRD 1 section 8 for the /help/ close.
**Board card:** none.

---

## What was built

**The picture stops floating on a phone.** At 390 the column is 350, so the
picture left 170px for the words: three or four words a line for the first six
lines of every answer. It sits above the writing now.

This was the review's own highest priority and it was also the fault I had put to
Kain myself an hour earlier, when he asked for the picture to be enlarged and I
reported that the phone would suffer. Both arrived at the same place
independently, which is the best reason to trust it.

**The feedback buttons meet the floor and can be seen.** Two real faults, found
by measuring rather than by reading the review: they drew 38 tall against the 44
touch floor, and their outline was the hairline token, about 1.2 to 1 against
white where a control's own boundary answers to 3 to 1.

**A visible focus ring, which this whole section had nowhere.** The review asked
for focus states to be verified. There were none to verify. Every interactive
thing in the foot and the feedback strip now shows where the keyboard is.

**And the two support routes read as actions**, with an arrow at the far end of
each. One mark for both, though one is a mailto and the other opens a tab:
drawing an external mark on one and an arrow on the other would put two symbol
languages side by side, which is the exact fault this foot was rebuilt to remove.

## The five items declined, and the ruling of Kain's each would have overturned

**Rewrite the opening so it does not restate the question.** Kain's own standing
rule, given 2026-07-28 and described by him as gold dust: open every article in
the reader's words, one short second-person line restating the question, then the
answer immediately. The review recommends the exact opposite of a standard he
set. It is also content, which does not come from me.

**Reduce the picture's prominence on desktop.** He enlarged it by a fifth twenty
minutes before the review arrived.

**Shorten "Still Have Some Unanswered Questions?" and replace the lead.** His
copy, his own rewrite at S296. He rewrote the two route descriptions this same
sitting and left the heading alone, which is a decision rather than an oversight.

**Reorder the related questions and drop the CIPS one.** That list is each
answer's own hand-picked set, lifted from the end of its body and topped up from
its category. Reordering is a per-answer content decision, made by whoever writes
the answer.

**Set a 65 to 75 character reading measure.** This is the one I would most like
to act on and cannot. Measured on the page: the body runs about 94 characters a
line. But 880 is Kain's ruled reading column for every page on this site, so this
is a site-wide question about the reading standard, not a polish on one template.
**It is worth putting to him properly**, and it is the one item from the review I
would carry forward.

**Two more, noted rather than declined.** The action row for someone ready to
attend is a new component plus new copy plus verified destinations, which is a
brief rather than a polish. And the breadcrumb's long current-page label is
already commissioned: the short-name brief is sitting in FROM Chat from S356.

## Two faults of my own, both caught by rendering

The picture was first built full width at 16 by 9. These files are 480 squares
holding a circular illustration and a name plate, so a wide crop cut the circle
top and bottom and sliced the words off the plate.

And **for the second time in this sitting a comment I wrote landed outside a
comment block**, which malformed the stylesheet and silently killed the phone
rule: the float read as right on a page whose own stylesheet said none. A
permanent memory note now carries it, and a comment-marker and brace check now
runs before a CSS deploy.

## One thing the review raised that is real and is Kain's to rule

The page's remaining accessibility violation is the UKRLP credit line's contrast.
It predates all of this, it is on every help answer, and it is a genuine AA
failure. Raised with him rather than swept into this change set.
