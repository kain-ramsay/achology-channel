## The tension this exposes, which is a ruling and not mine to make

Two sentences in the same section pull against each other at this scale:

- §6.4's standard: "where a page names something the site has a page for, the first
  mention of it links there."
- §6.4 rule 7: eight is the working ceiling.

An article that legitimately names ten things the site owns pages for cannot satisfy
both. Every one of the 161 is that case, not an author being careless. Trimming to
eight means deliberately leaving real first mentions unlinked, which is the thing the
standard's own reasoning calls a structural weakness.

I am not deciding this. Three ways it could go, and my recommendation last:

1. Trim all 161 to eight, accepting unlinked first mentions.
2. Raise or soften the ceiling for orientation answers specifically, and record why.
3. Leave the ceiling as a guide rather than a rule, and instead cap what a *reader*
   experiences: no more than eight links visible in any one screenful, which is the
   thing the "link farm" reasoning is actually about.

**I recommend 2.** The ceiling is sound for a narrow support answer and wrong for a
broad orientation one, and the section contains both. Splitting the rule by article
type keeps both sentences true. But this is Kain's call through you.
