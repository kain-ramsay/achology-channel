# RULING AND SHIP: every mark in the quote page's more quotes column is the card's own mark, and the title is a step larger

**From Code, S110. Date: Thursday 10 September 2026. Shipped at theme 0.264.0,
deployed and read back on the live page at 1440 and 390.**
**Ruled by Kain** live in this sitting, looking at the column on his phone. His
words:

> "why not just use the same glyph as whats been backed into the main card - and
> maybe, for consistency, you could just use the exact same glyph in the small
> cards too - instead of the one we used in the older version of the main card. I
> think the title can be made larger too"

**Owning documents:** DSRD 7 section 5.2 (the icon registry), and the quote
page's build sheet in its design folder.
**Board card:** Quote page template.

---

## He caught something precise, and it is worth writing down

The three small cards were carrying the **typographic quotation character**,
which is exactly the mark the big card carried until S109. He ruled it out there,
on the render, because Como draws that character as two thin slanted strokes, a
typographic mark rather than a shape, where his own mockup carried the round form
with a tail. The registry's `quote` glyph is that shape.

So this column had been built with the mark he had already rejected, one version
late. A ruling that lands on one component and is not carried to the next thing
built beside it is the same drift this project's harnesses exist to prevent, and
he saw it before any check did.

## What is on the page

**Four marks in the column, all of them the card's:** the registry's `quote`
glyph, hollow, turned half a turn so that it opens a line, in the brand orange.
One drawing, from the registry, which is what DSRD 7 section 5.2 requires and
what makes it impossible for the column to drift away from the card beside it.

**The title is a step larger**, and the soft tile behind the header's mark has
gone with it. The tile came from the book panel's header, where Kain chose it at
S108 from five presentations. It is removed because it was the one thing in this
column still presenting the glyph in its own way, which is the inconsistency he
was pointing at. That is a consequence of his ruling rather than an extra, and it
is named here rather than slipped in.

**Where the mark sits in each tile is the small card's, not the big card's.** The
card's own top left placement was built first and measured: it put the mark
straight through the first word of all three quotes. The card can hold it there
because it is 336 tall and its writing starts a long way down; a 77 tall tile has
no such room, and buying the room would have cost 78 of the column's 336. So the
drawing is the card's and the placement is the mini card component's approved
watermark position, from its own build sheet. That is the component precedence
chain doing its job.

## What was thrown away, so nobody looks for it

Four header treatments were built behind the workbench key while he was typing
this: no glyph at all, the label sitting on the rule, a `library` glyph meaning a
collection, and the words alone one step larger. **His instruction answers the
question they were asking, so all four are deleted rather than shown or left as a
switch.** One of them had already failed on the render and is worth one line: a
label knocked out of a rule needs a flat colour to paint with, and this band is a
photograph, so it rendered as a dark rectangle sitting on a bookshelf.

column's marks, their placement, and the reason the placement differs from the
card's.
