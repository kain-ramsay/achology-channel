## Why this file exists, in his words

> "I simply want one design standard that we can apply to all block components
> in all article type pages"

and, said in the same sitting after a long exchange of one question at a time:

> "i am so tired of trying to guess with you"

**That is the fault this standard exists to end.** Every block on these pages has
been settled one at a time, in its own sitting, against its own reasoning, and
the result is four page types that agree by accident where they agree at all.
The standard below is written so that no future block is a fresh question.

## What he settled first, and it is the test everything else is judged by

Four answers, each given as a yes in this sitting:

1. **The job of the top of an article page:** a reader decides, in a few seconds,
   whether the piece is for them and worth their next five minutes. Everything up
   there either helps that decision or is in the way of it.
2. **What the top carries, and nothing else:** where they are, what this is, one
   plain sentence saying what they will get, then the facts that say what it
   costs them (date, writer, length, and the listen control). Then the writing.
3. **A picture earns its place only by telling the reader something the words
   cannot.**
4. **A reader reaches the first sentence of the writing without scrolling.**

## THE STANDARD

### Part one: the top of every article-type page

In this order, and nothing else above the writing:

| | what it is | rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | the trail | where the reader is, in the reading column, aligned with the title |
| 2 | the title | the page-title step, on white, never on a picture or a dark band |
| 3 | one sentence | at reading size and in reading colour, saying what the reader gets. Never caption-sized and never grey |
| 4 | the facts | the reading bar: date, writer, length, and the listen control where a recording exists, with its rule under it |
| 5 | the writing | begins here |

**The page's own picture sits beside those words, never behind them**, and only
where it says something: the book's cover on a book note, the quote card on a
quote page, nothing on a help answer or an article whose picture is decoration.

**Test:** at a laptop's first screen, with no scrolling, the reader can read the
first sentence of the writing.

### Part two: the eight rules every block obeys

1. **One column.** Every block sits in the reading column. A block spans the page
   only where Kain has ruled that one, by name.
2. **One gap, owned once.** The space between two blocks is the same value
   everywhere, and only one of the two supplies it. Two blocks never both add
   space. Every hairline keeps 48 above and 48 below.
3. **Three text sizes.** The page title, the block heading, the reading text.
   Nothing sits between them. (DSRD 7 section 3: "Every text size on the site
   sits on a step; nothing sits between steps.")
4. **A picture earns its place** by telling the reader something the words
   cannot, or it is not there.
5. **No block repeats what the page has already said.** No label repeating the
   breadcrumb, no second name beside the title.
6. **One job, one block, drawn once.** The same job on two page types is the same
   component, never a second drawing of it.
7. **Nothing to show means nothing drawn.** No empty frame, no placeholder, no
   stand-in.
8. **Every block runs the same way down:** its heading, then its content, then
   its one action if it has one.

**Tests, so this is a standard and not a preference:**

- rule 1: a block outside the reading column names Kain's ruling for it.
- rule 2: measured on the rendered page, the gap between any two blocks is the
  ruled value, and one of the two declares zero.
- rule 3: no rendered text size is off the scale.
- rule 5: no string appears twice above the writing.
- rule 6: two page types drawing the same job share one class name.
- rule 7: no rendered block is empty or carries placeholder text.
