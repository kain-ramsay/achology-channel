## The standard being applied, quoted

DSRD 2 section 3.0: "No em dash (U+2014) and no en dash (U+2013) appears anywhere in Achology copy: page copy, articles, headings, metadata, captions, CSV fields and emails alike, across every page and content type this document specifies, with no exception. Use a colon, a comma, brackets or a full stop instead. The check is mechanical: a piece containing either character is not ready."

## The rules I applied, so the editorial judgement is visible and reviewable

The document offers four replacements and does not say which to use where, so I fixed the choice mechanically rather than deciding sentence by sentence:

1. **A pair of dashes inside a sentence becomes a pair of commas.** It is parenthetical: "tools we use, such as Google Analytics, are third-party".
2. **A dash straight after a bold label becomes a colon.** "To understand how the site is used: anonymous-style analytics".
3. **Every other dash becomes a comma.** Nearly all of them introduce an explanation, and a comma carries that without changing the sentence.

**No word was added, removed or reordered anywhere.** Only the punctuation mark changed. Where a dash sits inside a PHP comment rather than in copy a visitor reads, it was left alone, because a code comment is not copy, and that is why three files that contain the character never failed the page gate.
