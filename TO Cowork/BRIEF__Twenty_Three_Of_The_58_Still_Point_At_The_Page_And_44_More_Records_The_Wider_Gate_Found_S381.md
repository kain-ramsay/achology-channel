## The one rule, again, with the test

A sentence may not have the page as its subject, and may not point at the page. "Here", "this", "throughout", "in one place", "told honestly", "worked through", "what matters here" all point at the page when they mean the article. The fix is to say the thing about the subject itself. Where the sentence only described the article, cut it.

Good: "Mischel's 1970s study found that children who waited longer later scored higher on some tests." Bad: "Both halves matter here." The test: read the sentence aloud with the article's title hidden. If it still says something true about psychology, it passes.
