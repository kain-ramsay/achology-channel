# RULING: every article is written for a human reader first. The gates are the floor, never the target

## The rule

Every article, book note, quote page, workbook, help answer and biography on Achology.com is written for a real human being: someone who arrived with a question, a worry or a hope, and who will decide in the first paragraph whether this page respects them. The page exists to be helpful, approachable and informative to that person. Nothing else it does matters if that fails.

The gates (`content_gate.py`, the Rank Math bar, the paragraph cap, the DSRD 6 record, the house-copy checklist) are the floor. Every piece meets them, without exception, because they catch real faults. But a piece that meets every gate and reads like it was assembled to meet them has failed. Ticking a machine's boxes is not writing. A keyword placed where a reader would not put it, a subheading bent to carry a phrase, a paragraph split at a count rather than a thought, a sentence that exists because a checklist asked for it: each of these is a fault, and the gate passing it does not make it right.

## What this means when you draft

- Write the piece a knowledgeable, kind human would write for the reader in front of them. Then check it against the gates and fix what they catch. Never write to the gate and hope a human survives it.
- Read every paragraph aloud in your head. If it sounds like a machine describing the topic rather than a person explaining it, rewrite it before it goes anywhere.
- A rule about wording (keyword position, paragraph length, heading form) is met by finding the natural sentence that happens to satisfy it. If none exists, say so in your DONE rather than forcing one.
- The house voice is warm, plain and direct, and treats the reader as a capable adult. Filler, hedging, stacked adjectives, and sentences that restate the heading are not voice; they are padding.
- If a gate rule and a good reader experience genuinely conflict on a page, the reader wins and the conflict is named in your DONE for Chat to rule on. Never quietly serve the gate.
