## 2. The standard, in one paragraph, from DSRD 2 section 2.24

A help answer is finished when a first-time visitor leaves with fewer questions than they arrived with. The answer in the first paragraph. No paragraph longer than three sentences or 60 words, whichever it reaches first; list items are not paragraphs. Every heading is the reader's next question, never a label. A list wherever an answer has more than three parts. Every unfamiliar term explained in one plain sentence where it first appears. Written to "you", warmly, still institutional: no author, no first person. Facts stay on the page that owns them; a help answer links to the page rather than restating it. 625 words is the target, 320 the floor, nothing padded. One relevant external link (already present on all 250; keep it). UK English. No em or en dashes anywhere.

## 3. What you do to each record

1. Read the `measured_at_S117` line, then the body.
2. Split or rewrite every paragraph over the cap. Splitting alone is often enough; rewrite only where the sense needs it.
3. Turn every label heading into the reader's next question. The test: would a reader type it into a search box?
4. Where an answer has more than three parts running as prose, make it a list.
5. Remove "plainly" wherever it appears; simply is the ruled swap (RULING S356) where a swap is needed at all.
6. Explain each unfamiliar term in one plain sentence where it first appears. CIPS, PALS, VALTS, CCaC, ALTs, DiMAP, SoMAP and UKRLP are the ones that recur.
7. Move toward 625 only where a real reader question is unanswered on the page. Nothing is padded to reach a number.
8. Touch nothing in the Page fields table. Title, address, category, SEO title and description are settled and stay.
9. Check the whole body against the cap by count before you call it done: every paragraph three sentences or fewer and 60 words or fewer. Write that count into the record's `measured_at_S117` line as a second line, `corrected_S{session}`, with words, paragraphs over cap (must be 0), label headings (must be 0), machine tells (must be none).
