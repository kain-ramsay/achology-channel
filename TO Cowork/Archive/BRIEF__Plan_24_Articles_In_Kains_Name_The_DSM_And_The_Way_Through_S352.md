## 1. What Kain asked for, in his words

Twenty four articles by Kain Ramsay, all interconnected as one series, yet each standalone and independent as a thought-provoking piece, using the first two chapters of his unfinished book Mental Health Rebellion as context, and leading into the relevant, appropriate concepts he has taught in the Mental Health Practitioner course, for which all context is in the course transcripts. Every article answers a hard, unconventional question that is being asked about the validity of the DSM and the mental health conditions defined in it. **The purpose is not to dismiss the DSM, and not to validate it, but to reference it as a source that only defines problems and offers no solutions, unlike the ideas taught in Achology's Mental Health Practitioner course.** The courses recommended in the block beneath every article are the Mental Health and Wellbeing Practitioner Diploma Course and the Diploma Course in Modern Applied Psychology (DiMAP).

## 3. What the plan must contain

- **Twenty four questions, each a real interrogative a person types or asks aloud**, proved at stage 0 with a live search (autocomplete, People Also Ask, one assistant asked the seed), each with its evidence line and its proposed focus keyword (the short phrase inside the question), each keyword checked against the register and claimed. Rule 6 of The Publish Ready Pipeline governs: a label or a thesis statement is not a title.
- **For each question: the DSM's part and the course's part.** One line on which DSM idea or condition the article puts under the question, referenced as the thing that defines the problem; and one line naming the course lesson or concept the article leads into as the way through, by lesson number and its question line. Every one of the 24 leads into a real lesson; none leads into a slogan.
- **The series shape.** The order the 24 sit in, so a reader who follows them travels from "is the DSM even measuring anything" through to "what a person actually does with their own mind", and a sentence on why each stands alone for a reader who arrives at it cold.
- **The hard line the series keeps**, stated once at the head: the DSM is neither dismissed nor validated. Where a question would only be answerable by attacking the manual or by defending it, it is not on the list.
- **The sourcing register.** Every factual claim the chapters make that an article would lean on (the WHO definition, the orexin exception, the Spitzer interview, the 2004 subthreshold study, the 2020 inflation study, the eating-disorder BMI finding, and so on) is listed with its original source found and dated, or marked "not found, do not carry", because the chapters are four years old and the articles publish today under his name. Nothing from the chapters travels into an article without its source re-found.
