## Job 1. Nineteen book note records: the five body headings take three hashes

**What is wrong.** Twenty-five drafted book notes pass the content gate and Kain has given his word to publish them. Six import cleanly. **Nineteen are refused, and all nineteen fail for the same single reason:** they write their five body sections at two hashes, as siblings of `## Body`, where the importer requires three hashes inside it. The importer therefore reads their body as empty and reports all five sections missing.

The difference, and it is the whole of it:

| Imports cleanly | Refused |
|---|---|
| `## Body` then `### What this Book is Actually Saying` | `## Body` then `## What this Book is Actually Saying` |

**The fix.** In each of the nineteen, the five locked section headings become three hashes. **The headings' words do not change**, and they are locked verbatim and in order by Kain's S314 ruling, so a heading that is reworded rather than re-levelled breaks the book note page's own contents list.

**Nothing else in the record moves.** Not a word of the body, not a field, not a keyword, not the metadata.

**Why three and not two, so nobody re-opens it.** The importer's converter lifts three hashes to H2 on the rendered page, which is the correct heading level. Three hashes in the record is not a quirk, it is the input the machinery was built to read, and it is what all 65 published book notes already use. The alternative, teaching the importer to accept both shapes, was refused: two correct ways to write one thing, forever, on a corpus heading toward 620 records.

**When the nineteen are back, all 25 import together.** Code will not send six of twenty-five, and he is right not to.

## Job 2. Thirty-nine author biographies: the five section headings become H2

**This one is an accessibility fault on live pages, not a tidiness job.**

Read off the live page at `kain-ramsay`: the page emits H1, then **The Short Version, Life and Formation, Body of Work, Influence and Legacy and Explore Their Work all at H3**, and the first H2 on the page arrives afterwards from the theme's own furniture. **So the page jumps H1 to H3 and back to H2.** To a screen reader that is a missing level, and under DSRD 6 chapter 7 it is a hierarchy failure.

**It reaches 39 published biographies**, measured on the install: 39 carry H3 in their stored content with no H2 anywhere in it.

**The fix, at source in the records rather than on the install:** the five section headings move to H2, so each page reads H1, five H2s, then the theme's own H2 furniture. That is a correct hierarchy rather than a patched one.

**The five headings' words do not change either.** They are the reader-facing H2s, written verbatim, recorded at DSRD 2 section 3.9.

**One thing you may hit and should report rather than solve:** Code is confirming whether the biography importer runs the same hash-to-H2 converter the book note importer does, or takes the record's level literally. If his answer changes what the record should carry, it comes to you as a correction before you re-import. **Make the record read the correct heading level either way; the converter question decides only how it is expressed.**

**Twelve of the 51 biographies are not in the 39.** Do not assume they are clean; Code is reading what they carry. Work the 39 he names and leave the twelve until his list lands.
