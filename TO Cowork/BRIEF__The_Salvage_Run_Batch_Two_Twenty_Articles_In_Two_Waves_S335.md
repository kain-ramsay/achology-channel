## Four things the recipe does not say, specific to this batch

1. **Most of these rows rest on one named study.** Hawthorne, Loftus on false memory, Milgram, the invisible gorilla (Simons and Chabris 1999), Robbers Cave (Sherif), the visual cliff (Gibson and Walk 1960), split-brain (Sperry), cognitive maps (Tolman 1948), the Stanford prison experiment behind Zimbardo's *The Lucifer Effect*, and the misattribution of arousal study (Dutton and Aron 1974). The original paper or book is the source, named and linked beside the claim. Where a paper cannot be fetched live, say so in the batch report and write the link only on strong cross-matched corroboration.

2. **Four rows rest on a person's framework rather than one study:** Egan's skilled helper model, Carl Rogers on person-centred counselling, Frankl's *Man's Search for Meaning*, and Gardner's nine intelligences. The source is that person's own key work, named with its year.

3. **Rows 13, 15, 22, 28, 29 and 34 are broader think pieces** (theories of motivation, the meaning of life, practical learning, the history of behaviourism, character traits, freedom and autonomy). These still fail the unique value test if the central idea is not traced to a named original. Find the real source and credit it. Where no real source exists, skip the piece under Rule 5, log it, and carry the batch on. Never rewrite an idea as if it were Achology's.

4. **Zimbardo (row 32) carries a live scholarly dispute.** The Stanford prison experiment's methods and conclusions have been seriously challenged since 2018 (Le Texier's archival work, among others). The article names the challenge rather than presenting the 1971 findings as settled. Doing so is not a weakness of the piece; it is the piece's contribution.
