## 1. The rule, in Kain's own words

> "We can't ever use vague terms such as 'Hold Up'. This doesn't mean anything. We must use more mature and adult language, such as: Does Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs Still Prove Relevant Today?"

**A title never carries a colloquial or idiomatic phrase.** Achology writes to adults, and the title is the first sentence of the brand a reader meets. A phrase that reads as casual speech, or that carries no precise meaning read cold, is not written.

**The test, so this is a rule rather than a preference:** read the title aloud with no context. If any phrase in it would not appear in a serious piece of professional writing, or if a reader could not say precisely what it claims, rewrite it. "Hold up", "check out", "still work", "fake" and "have it all" all fail that test. "Still prove relevant today", "withstand scrutiny" and "scientifically sound" all pass it.

This is a judgement about register, not a banned-word list, and **no machine currently enforces it.** The content gate checks a title's length and its keyword; it does not read a title for register at all, and its vocabulary lists run against the body only. That check does not exist and is being asked of Code separately. Until it does, this is a human read on every title before the record leaves your hands.

## 3. The rule reaches every field a title lives in, not only post_title

Three of the seven carried the same slang a second time, in `rm_seo_title` or `rm_seo_description`, where nobody had looked. Chat corrected those too.

**When a title is rewritten, check and correct all four places the phrasing hides:** `post_title`, `rm_seo_title`, `rm_seo_description`, and the record's own H1 line at the top of the file. Where the body echoes the title's phrase, correct it there as well.

## 4. Three mechanical constraints that bound any rewrite

A better title that fails the gate is not a better title. Every rewrite holds all three:

1. **`rm_seo_title` stays at 60 characters or fewer**, with the focus keyword inside its first 50. `post_title` is not length-gated, so where a good title runs past 60, carry it as `post_title` and keep a shorter compliant `rm_seo_title` beneath it. That was done this session on `psychology-history-timeline`, whose ruled title is 65 characters.
2. **The focus keyword is never dropped from `rm_seo_title` to make room.** The keyword was proved at stage 0 against live search and the slug is built from it; a title that loses it loses the page's search claim.
3. **`post_name`, `address` and `old_address` never move.** A title is not a slug. DSRD 1 section 11.9 fixes every Group A salvage rebuild at its old slug.

## 5. One honest cost, recorded rather than hidden

`Is the Stanford Prison Experiment Fake?` was the demand-proven question form: "fake" is a word people genuinely type. `Was the Stanford Prison Experiment Scientifically Sound?` is the better sentence and the weaker magnet. Kain was told this plainly before he ruled, and ruled the change anyway. That record's focus keyword, "the lucifer effect", is untouched, so its search claim survives.

**The general point, for every future rewrite:** where the mature phrasing costs real search demand, name the trade in your batch report. Do not quietly keep the slang, and do not quietly drop the demand.
