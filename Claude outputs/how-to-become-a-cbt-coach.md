## Body

What is CBT coaching? It's using CBT's tools inside a coaching relationship, aimed at where a client wants to go next, not at treating an illness.

How to become a CBT coach isn't governed by a single fixed licence, the way an NHS CBT therapist route is. Most people build it from two real things instead: genuine skill in CBT itself, and a genuine coaching practice.

## What Actually Separates Coaching From Therapy?

A global body for coaching draws this line clearly. Coaching looks at a client's present and future, at performance and potential they haven't reached yet.

Therapy looks the other way, at past experience and at diagnosing and treating dysfunction. That's a real difference, not a matter of which word sounds nicer.

A CBT coach works well within the first description. They use CBT's structured way of examining thoughts and behaviour, but pointed forward, at a goal, not backward, at a diagnosis.

Think of it like two different jobs done with the same toolbox. A carpenter builds a new shelf, and a carpenter repairs a broken one. Same tools, same skill, aimed at genuinely different work.

A coaching client might come in stuck on a career decision, with no illness involved at all. A therapy client might come in with genuine, diagnosable distress that needs treating first. Both can benefit from CBT's tools, just pointed at very different starting problems.

## What Might a CBT Coaching Session Actually Look Like?

In practice, it often starts with a stated goal, not a symptom. A client names something they want to change or achieve, and the coach helps them notice the thoughts getting in the way of it.

From there, it looks a lot like CBT proper: testing whether a thought is actually true, and trying a different behaviour to see what happens. The difference is the aim, a goal rather than a diagnosis, not the method itself.

## Why Does CBT Fit Coaching So Well?

Because CBT was always built around a practical loop: notice a thought, test it, change the behaviour that follows. That loop works just as well for someone chasing a goal as it does for someone managing distress.

A coach borrowing CBT's tools isn't stretching them into something they weren't built for. The same structure that helps someone manage anxiety also helps someone stuck on a decision, or repeating a pattern that isn't serving them.

## Is There a Formal Coaching Accreditation for This?

Bodies like the International Coaching Federation offer their own credentials for coaches generally, not one built specifically for CBT coaching. Achology's own courses don't award one of those general coaching credentials themselves.

It wouldn't be honest to imply otherwise. What Achology's courses give instead is the underlying skill, in both CBT and coaching, that a coach would bring to any credential they later choose to pursue.

Some people pursue a general coaching credential afterwards, and some never do. Either path is genuinely fine, since the credential proves something different from the skill itself, and this page has never claimed the two are the same thing.

## So, How to Become a CBT Coach: The Real Route

Two things, in practice, not one qualification with that exact name on it. First, real training in CBT itself, so the tools are used properly rather than borrowed loosely from something skimmed online.

Second, real coaching skill. That means learning how to ask well, how to listen properly, and how to hold a client's own goal steady, rather than quietly turning every session into therapy by accident.

## Does Achology Offer a Route Into This?

Yes, built from two of its own courses rather than one course claiming to cover both. The CBT side comes first, then the coaching side builds on it.

Neither course promises a coaching career or an income figure of any kind. What they build is the underlying skill, and what anyone does with that afterwards is entirely their own.

Someone finishing both might go on to build a coaching practice, or simply use the skill in a job they already have. Either way, the courses themselves make no claim about which one happens.

That's a deliberate choice, not a gap in the offer. A course that promised a coaching career would be making a claim it can't actually back up, and Achology would rather be honest about that than sell a story instead.
