## Body

What does a CBT course cover, in a full one? Six real teaching areas, built in order, not a random pile of modules.

Each area builds on the one before it. You start with how your thinking shapes what you feel, and end with using the method in your own daily life.

Think of it like building a house. You need solid ground before you can add the walls. A CBT course works the same way.

That order matters. A course that skips straight to techniques, without the groundwork underneath them, tends not to stick once real pressure shows up. That's how skill-building generally operates, one incremental layer at a time.

## What Are the Six Areas a Full CBT Course Covers?

Achology's own [CBT Practitioner Course](/academy/cognitive-behavioural-psychology/cbt-practitioner/) groups its curriculum this way, across more than 31 hours and 119 lessons. More than 169,000 people have already taken it.

- **How your thinking shapes your life**: the basic link between a thought, a feeling, and what you do next.

- **Where your beliefs actually come from**: the roots of the assumptions you carry, often from years earlier.

- **The model at the heart of CBT**: the ABC model, connecting an event, a belief, and a consequence.

- **The psychology behind the method**: Aaron Beck's cognitive distortions, and Albert Ellis's ABC and ABCDE frameworks.

- **Putting CBT into practice**: Socratic questioning, and tools like the STOPP card, used in the moment.

- **Building self-awareness from the ground up**: turning all of the above into a habit you actually keep using.

## How Long Does It Take to Get Through the Whole Curriculum?

There's no single fixed length, because self-paced courses let you move at your own speed. Achology's own CBT Practitioner Course runs to more than 31 hours across 119 lessons, covering all six areas in full.

Working through it for an hour or two most weeks, most people complete the entire curriculum within a few months. Someone with more time available can move through the curriculum considerably faster.

## Do You Need Any Background in Psychology First?

No. A well-built course starts from the first area and assumes nothing. Each stage explains what the last one needs before moving on.

Someone with no psychology background at all can follow it end to end. That's the whole point of teaching it in order rather than jumping straight to techniques.

You don't need a degree of any kind. You don't need to have studied psychology at school either. The examples throughout draw on ordinary, everyday situations, not clinical case studies.

## Is It Just Theory, or Real Practice Too?

Both, and the theory exists to support the practice, not to replace it. Aaron Beck's work on cognitive distortions, and Albert Ellis's ABC and ABCDE frameworks, explain why a thought misleads you.

Socratic questioning and tools like the STOPP card are what you actually use in the moment. Say a work email leaves you assuming your manager is annoyed with you. The practice is asking what else could explain it, before reacting to a guess as if it were a fact.

That single pause, checking a guess before acting on it, is what most of the course is really teaching you to do. It's a small habit, but it's the one that matters most.

## What Does the NHS Say CBT Actually Involves?

The NHS's own description covers similar ground. It typically runs for between 5 and 15 sessions, questioning unhelpful thoughts and beliefs, and considering other ways of looking at things.

It also involves changing avoidance behaviour through practical activities between sessions. A self-paced course covers the same territory, just without a fixed number of sessions attached to it.

That's a useful check for any course you're weighing up. If a course skips these basics, it probably isn't teaching real CBT at all.

## What Does a CBT Course Cover, in Practice?

All six areas together, not any one of them alone. Skipping the early ones and jumping straight to the techniques is exactly the shortcut that tends to leave people falling back on old habits.

Taken in order, the six areas turn a set of ideas into something you can actually reach for the next time you need it. That's what separates a structured curriculum from a loose collection of techniques.
