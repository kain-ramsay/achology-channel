## Body

How to become a CBT therapist, in plain terms: yes, you need a degree. You also need a real mental health qualification. Then you need specific CBT training on top of that.

There are two main routes in the UK. One runs through the NHS. The other runs through a university.

Both take years, not months. Neither is a quick weekend course. Anyone who tells you otherwise isn't being straight with you.

Achology's own CBT courses teach the same core ideas. But they sit on neither route. They were never built to.

That gap matters more than it looks. The rest of this page sets out why.

## What's the NHS Route to Becoming a CBT Therapist?

Most people start as a psychological wellbeing practitioner. This is the first step. That training runs about 45 days of study over roughly a year.

It runs alongside real, supervised practice. You're already working for an NHS Talking Therapies service while you train.

Graduates usually complete a postgraduate certificate for this stage. Non-graduates can reach it a different way. They use an equal qualification or an apprenticeship route instead.

Since 2022, everyone in the role must register with a body. That's either the British Psychological Society or the BABCP. This keeps a floor under the training's quality.

After qualifying, most people need two more years in that role. Only then can they access NHS-funded training as a full CBT therapist.

It's a long road. It's built in stages, not all at once. Each stage has to finish before the next one opens.

## What's the University Route?

The University of Oxford, among others, offers a postgraduate diploma in CBT. It runs 12 months, part-time. It asks for a strong degree.

It also asks for at least a year of clinical work after you qualify. And it asks for a real mental health job already in hand.

Applications usually need three references. One must come from your current clinical supervisor. Another is an academic reference.

This isn't a course you apply to straight after your degree. The clinical work has to come first.

This route suits someone already working in mental health who wants to add CBT. It isn't a route in from a standing start. Universities offering it are usually clear about that in their own entry rules.

This path suits people already working as a psychologist, nurse, social worker or counsellor. It lets you add a recognised CBT qualification onto a career you already have, rather than starting again from nothing.

## Do You Need a Degree?

Generally, yes. Most UK employers of CBT therapists look for BABCP accreditation. That asks for a real mental health job first.

Then it asks for specific CBT training on top. Together, that's at least four years of training.

That figure covers both stages. First comes your mental health qualification. Then comes the CBT-specific training layered on top.

Neither stage alone gets you accredited. Both have to be done, in order, before the BABCP will recognise you.

There's no shortcut for the clinical, patient-treating role. Anyone telling you otherwise, whether that's an ad for a weekend course or a forum post promising a fast track, is stretching the truth.

This is why Achology is careful never to describe its own courses as clinical training. The accreditation route exists for good reason, and shortening it would only lower the standard of care patients actually receive.

## How to Become a CBT Therapist a Different Way: Is There a Shorter Route?

Not into the clinical role, no. It wouldn't be honest to pretend otherwise. What Achology offers is a different thing.

Its courses teach you to use CBT on yourself. They teach you to use it on the people around you too. None of that involves treating patients.

Coaches, teachers, managers and parents tend to get the most from this route. They're using CBT in conversations they're already having, not in a clinical setting with a caseload of patients.

That's a real and useful skill. It doesn't need a clinical licence. It doesn't need years of extra training either.

Want to know exactly how that differs from the therapist role? [This answer](/help/comparisons-and-alternatives/cbt-practitioner-vs-cbt-therapist/) sets it out in full. It explains why the two titles mean different things, and why that difference matters in practice.
