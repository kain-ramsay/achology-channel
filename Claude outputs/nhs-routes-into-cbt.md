## Body

What are the NHS routes into CBT? There are two, and they run in a set order, one building on the other.

The first is low intensity, through a role called a psychological wellbeing practitioner. The second is high intensity, as a fully trained CBT therapist.

Most people start at the first and move to the second only after real experience. Skipping straight to the second isn't how the system works.

## What Is the Low-Intensity Route?

This is the psychological wellbeing practitioner role, sometimes shortened to PWP. It delivers guided self-help, a lighter-touch version of CBT's ideas, for common problems like mild anxiety.

Training runs about 45 days of academic work over roughly a year, done while already working for an NHS Talking Therapies service. It's a real, structured route, not an informal one.

Graduates usually complete a postgraduate certificate for this stage, while non-graduates can reach it through an equivalent qualification. Either way, it's paid work from day one, not unpaid study beforehand.

## What Is the High-Intensity Route?

This is full CBT therapy, for more complex cases than the low-intensity route handles. It's what most people mean when they picture a CBT therapist.

Most trainees reach it only after at least two years working as a psychological wellbeing practitioner first. It's a genuinely long road, built in stages rather than all at once.

Since 2022, everyone in these roles has also had to register with a recognised professional body, which keeps a floor under the training's quality throughout.

## Why Does the NHS Split CBT This Way?

Because not every case needs the same intensity of help. Splitting the work this way means more people get some support sooner, while the fullest training goes where it's needed most.

It also means the route into becoming a high-intensity therapist runs through real, paid clinical experience first, not straight from a classroom. That experience is what the second stage of training actually builds on.

## So, What Are the NHS Routes Into CBT, Overall?

Two routes, in a fixed order: low intensity through a psychological wellbeing practitioner, then high intensity as a fully trained CBT therapist. Neither is a weekend course, and both take real years of commitment.

## Does an Achology Course Sit on Either Route?

No, and it wouldn't be honest to say otherwise. Achology's own CBT courses teach the same underlying ideas, but for using CBT on yourself and the people around you, not for NHS clinical practice.

If your aim is the NHS route, this page has named where to start. If your aim is understanding CBT well enough to use it yourself, an Achology course does that without the years first.
