## Body

How much do CBT therapists earn? In the NHS, it depends heavily on where you sit on the training ladder, and there's a real, published figure for each stage.

Achology doesn't make its own promise here. This page names the actual NHS bands with their source, then says honestly what an Achology course offers instead.

## What Does a Trainee Earn?

At the low-intensity stage, a trainee psychological wellbeing practitioner starts on Band 4, around £28,392 a year. Once qualified, that moves to Band 5, from £32,073 up to £39,043.

At the high-intensity stage, a trainee CBT therapist starts on Band 6, around £37,338. That's a genuinely different figure from the low-intensity trainee rate, and it reflects the extra clinical experience already required to get there.

Think of it like a shop's own pay ladder. A junior assistant earns less than a supervisor, and a supervisor earns less than the manager who trained for years to reach that post. NHS CBT bands work the same way, just with clinical titles instead of shop-floor ones.

## How Long Does It Take to Reach the Top of the Ladder?

Reaching the top of this ladder takes real years, not a fast track. Most trainee CBT therapists spend at least two years working first as a psychological wellbeing practitioner, gaining the clinical experience the high-intensity route requires before that training even starts.

So the full climb, from a Band 4 trainee to a fully qualified Band 7 CBT therapist, is closer to five or six years than one. Nobody reaches the higher figures on this page overnight.

## What Does a Qualified CBT Therapist Earn?

Once fully qualified, a high-intensity CBT therapist sits on Band 7, from £46,148 up to £52,809. With enough experience, some move into Band 8 roles, starting from £53,755.

These figures include a London supplement in some areas, worth an extra 5 to 20 percent depending on the location. The core figures above are for the rest of the country.

That supplement isn't small. On a Band 7 salary, the higher London rate can add several thousand pounds a year, simply for working in a more expensive part of the country.

## Does Everyone End Up on the Same Band?

No. Each band has several pay points inside it, and moving up those points depends on length of service and an annual pay review, not a single fixed number.

Two therapists on Band 7 can genuinely earn different amounts, both correctly described by the same figures above. The band sets the range, not the exact number everyone lands on.

A therapist with ten years in post typically sits higher on their band than someone who qualified last year, even on the same job title. That difference is ordinary NHS pay progression, not a separate figure to look up.

## What About Work Outside the NHS?

The bands above are NHS clinical pay, and they don't stretch to cover CBT work done outside it, in private practice or elsewhere. What someone earns there depends on their own client base and reputation, not a single published figure this page could quote fairly.

That's a genuinely different question from the one this page answers. The honest answer is that no single reliable figure exists for it, which is exactly why this page has stuck to the published NHS bands instead.

## Does Achology Promise Any of This?

No, and it wouldn't be honest to suggest otherwise. These figures are NHS clinical salaries, tied to the NHS routes this page has just described.

Achology's own courses don't lead to a clinical job, and they carry no salary of their own attached to them. What they offer is different: real skill in using CBT, in whatever job or role you already have.

That might mean managing a team more calmly, coaching a client through a setback, or simply making better decisions yourself under pressure. None of that shows up as a band on a pay scale.

## So, What's the Actual Return on an Achology Course?

Not a salary figure, honestly. The return is a better-managed working week, calmer decisions under pressure, and a genuine skill you can bring to coaching, managing, teaching or parenting.

For some people that leads indirectly to career moves worth more than any single number here. For most, it simply makes the job they already have easier to do well.

Someone building a coaching practice, say, might charge more for their own sessions over time. That's a real outcome, but it's built from skill and reputation, not handed out by a course completion certificate alone.

## So, How Much Do CBT Therapists Earn, Overall?

Four figures, in order: around £28,392 training as a psychological wellbeing practitioner, £32,073 to £39,043 once qualified in that role, around £37,338 training as a CBT therapist, and £46,148 to £52,809 once fully qualified, with Band 8 roles reaching £53,755 and beyond. Every one of them is a real, published NHS figure, not an estimate.
