## Job 1: the ranked demand list

The fifteen candidates in READ_ME_FIRST section 5, each with the ranking evidence seen. No reputable tool's published search-volume figure could be pulled through this session's search tool (that needs an Ahrefs/Semrush login this session doesn't have); every line below is ranking evidence from live search, as the brief's own method allows when no published figure is available.

**The mind**

1. **Cognitive biases.** A crowded, open field: dedicated quiz sites (biasquiz.com, cognitivebiaslab.com, ethicalhq.com) sit alongside one major branded competitor, Psychology Today's own cognitive bias test. No single dominant brand owns this space.
2. **The nine intelligences (Gardner).** Broad, international demand; several small test-farm sites (MentalUP, IDRLabs, JobCannon, multiIQtest.com, PrismaTest) plus a government careers-service version (Alberta ALIS, reviewed directly). No dominant brand.
3. **A Master Intelligence Test (the five IQ sites).** The single highest-volume category of the fifteen. Dominated by test-farm networks (123test, myiqtested, iqscore.io, fastlearner.ai, intelligencetest.com) as well as the five sites Kain shared, three of which (accurateiqtest.com, reliableiqtest.com, and by structure iqcertificate.org/realiqtest.net) turned out to be doorways into one shared underlying product. Confirmed as the hardest of the fifteen to fit the one rule, exactly as READ_ME_FIRST flagged, because the entire competitive field sells a fixed number as an identity.
4. **Learning style.** Solid, well-established demand tied to study-skills searches. One dedicated original brand (vark-learn.com) plus many quiz-farm clones.

**The self**

5. **Personality traits (Eysenck).** A smaller, more clinical-adjacent niche than personality type below: IDRLabs' PEN test, psytests.org, similarminds.com, and two dedicated Eysenck-branded domains. Real but modest demand.
6. **Personality type.** The single largest personality-test brand category online, dominated overwhelmingly by 16Personalities and the wider MBTI ecosystem (Crystal Knows, Cloverleaf, Boo, MyPersonality). VisualDNA is a smaller, partly-retired competitor by comparison. Confirms 16Personalities as the benchmark competitor named in the brief.
7. **Growth mindset (Dweck).** Solid demand with several credible branded competitors already active (Psychology Today, Gyfted, IDRLabs, psychology.com).
8. **Types of self.** The weakest-evidenced candidate of the fifteen. Search results were almost entirely academic (Wikipedia, Brainscape flashcards, Study.com lesson pages) rather than consumer quizzes people take for fun. This is a real gap worth naming plainly to Kain: either genuine unmet demand Achology could be first to fill well, or a sign this exact phrase ("types of self") is not how people actually search, and a different working title should be tested before production resource commits to it.
9. **Self sabotage.** A decent, coaching-adjacent field (Psychology Today, The Ford Institute, Modern Consciousness, a Dr Judy Ho PDF assessment).
10. **Values and moral self-reflection, or career aptitude.** Values assessments have a real but smaller field (123test, Values Institute, a Schwartz PVQ-40 adaptation). Career aptitude has a larger, more commercially established field (Coursera, Truity, 123test, Naukri). On the evidence alone, career aptitude is the stronger demand signal, but the plan itself names this as a choice for Kain to make "by demand," so it is put to him as a decision rather than settled here.

**Others**

11. **Emotional intelligence.** The most competitive field of the "others" family: all seven of Kain's named competitors are live and active, plus more found independently (LEADx, IHHP, elevateleadership, eqscore.org). Strong, well-established demand.
12. **Listening.** A real field exists but is noticeably thinner and more academic or training-oriented than emotional intelligence or communication style (an ecampusontario academic PDF, training-course material sites, a handful of quiz-maker pages).
13. **Communication style.** A solid field, comparable in size to growth mindset (Work It Daily, JobCannon, RedSeed, LeadershipIQ, several dedicated domains).
14. **Leadership style.** A solid field, comparable in size to communication style (a bunch.ai roundup naming six competing quizzes, MindTools, 123test, a dedicated leadership-style-quiz.com domain).

**Purpose**

15. **Ikigai and purpose.** Real, currently active demand, with ikigain.org appearing as the closest thing to a dominant brand, alongside several smaller dedicated domains (ikigaiquiz.com, japaneserituals.com, skillsetcentre.com). FranklinCovey's mission-statement builder, reviewed directly, is confirmed as the right model for the companion-tool half of this candidate rather than a scored test.

**Anything larger and inside Achology's ground, not already on the list of fifteen.** Nothing surfaced in this research that is both plainly larger than these fifteen and squarely inside Achology's growth-frame ground. The one candidate worth flagging as adjacent but outside that ground: clinical screening instruments (for example depression or anxiety screeners in the PHQ-9/GAD-7 family) surfaced tangentially while researching self-sabotage and emotional intelligence. These carry real search volume, but they are clinical screening tools, not growth-frame self-understanding tools, and sit outside what Achology's one rule allows a test to do. Seen, and named here as explicitly out of scope, not silently dropped.

**The headline findings across all seventeen:**

1. **The IQ cluster (five sites) is a network wearing five names, and it breaks Achology's one rule in the plainest way in the whole set:** a fixed number, printed on a certificate with the person's name on it, sold back to them as their identity, with the certificate itself as the paid unlock.
2. **16Personalities is the benchmark to beat for series 6,** both because it is the single biggest personality-test brand encountered and because it breaks the one rule just as plainly as the IQ cluster: a fixed four-letter type presented as identity, with a paid Premium tier behind the free reveal.
3. **The tools that already come closest to Achology's own rule are the least commercial ones:** the Alberta careers-service intelligence quiz (a spread, not a label), the Global Leadership Foundation's EI test (an explicit "this is the start of a journey, not a verdict" disclaimer), and the Gyfted growth mindset quiz (two named trait scores, no binary label). None of these three have any funnel at all, which is Achology's clearest opportunity: keep their honesty, add the funnel they lack.
4. **The FranklinCovey mission-statement builder is the right model for the Ikigai/purpose candidate:** a kept document, not a score, which is the closest thing to "no verdict" possible because there is no verdict to begin with.
5. **Two access models sit at the extremes worth naming to Kain directly.** TalentSmart gates its entire EQ test behind a book purchase (zero free value before payment); Truity gives a free brief report then paywalls the full one (partial value, then a paywall). Achology's planned funnel, step 5 in READ_ME_FIRST (free report, email, video, course) sits at neither extreme: full free value up front, the course earned afterward rather than paid for upfront. Worth stating plainly in the planning session as the deliberate contrast.
6. **Three sites could not be fully reviewed this session** and are named rather than guessed at: swiei.com's own test page actively blocked automated visits (bot protection); Greater Good Berkeley's quiz and MindTools' quiz both hit an access gate that did not clear in time. Each review file says exactly what could and could not be confirmed, and each names the same next step: a follow-up visit through a live browser session (built-in browser or Chrome) rather than a direct page fetch, which should get past what blocked this session.
7. **The you.visualdna.com/quiz/whoami link Kain shared did not resolve** (likely retired or migrated); the review was completed instead against the live parent site, visualdna.com/quizzes, which still describes the same "Who Am I" mechanism. Worth a quick word to Kain that the exact link has gone stale, in case he wants to reshare it from wherever he originally found it.
