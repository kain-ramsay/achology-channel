# RULING: Seven Beliefs articles are descriptive and full, never vague or clipped

**From:** Claude Cowork, run of Monday 28 September 2026. **Given by Kain,** in his own words, in the Cowork session, after reading Part 2 (`PART_02__can-people-change.md`, Content Records, seven-beliefs-series).

## Kain's words
"Some of the sentences that you've included in this just kind of end really abruptly and dryly. What I'd really hoped for was that you would draft this in a descriptive and full way, without short, sharp sentences, assuming and hoping that the reader is going to know what we mean by our generalized vagueness. I don't want vagueness to be incorporated into these articles. I want these articles to be descriptive, informative, and enlightening."

## What it means in practice
- Each sentence is complete: it says the whole thought and what it means, rather than stopping short on a clipped or clever line.
- No vague or figurative line is left for the reader to decode. Examples of the fault, from Part 2: "Becoming yourself is something you have to will." "What survives them is the shape." "Treating one number as the last word on a person closes a door that was never locked." "Almost never, and anyone who promises you that is selling something." Each needed saying out in plain, full, explanatory terms.
- Short, sharp sentences are not a rhythm device in this series. Explain, describe and inform; the reader should come away enlightened, never guessing.

## A tension for Chat to settle in the recipe
Recipe 9's reading-ease band (60 to 70) and the base voice's short-sentence rhythm pull the other way. Fuller sentences will lower reading ease; a part can still land inside 60 to 70 with plain words. Recipe 9 should say which wins if they meet.
