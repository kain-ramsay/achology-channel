**4. Two gate mechanics worth writing into `rank-math-90` or `instructor-article`, both found and confirmed
repeatedly across Wave 2.** First: a keyword's word count for density purposes follows the gate's own
tokenising, not a human count — a hyphenated compound like "people-first" counts as one word, so
"people-first leadership" computes as a 2-word keyword, and a short (2-word) keyword needs roughly two to
three times the hit count of a longer (5-word) keyword to land in the same 1.0-to-1.5% density band at a
similar word count. Every Wave 2 row with a short keyword (K09, K11, K12) needed 8 to 10 hits; longer-keyword
rows needed 4. Second: a keyword or working vocabulary with high syllable-count words (entrepreneurial,
systemise, recognise, distinction) measurably drags the Flesch reading-ease score down, and the fix each time
was the same: a short pass of word-level simplification plus one or two deliberately short, plain sentences,
never more content in the same register. Both are now proven across five rows (K08 to K12), not a one-off.

**5. A recurring drafting-process error, self-caught every time but worth naming so it stops recurring.**
Nearly every word-count or Flesch fix this session, across K08, K09, K10, K11 and K12, took an edit intended
to add a new paragraph and instead merged it into an adjacent existing paragraph, because the matched text
spanned the paragraph boundary without an explicit blank-line break. Every instance produced an over-length
or over-sentenced paragraph the gate caught, costing an extra fix round each time. K12 used a Python
diagnostic script to count each paragraph's words and sentences against the standard before spending a gate
run, and caught three such breaches before they reached the gate rather than after. Worth carrying that
diagnostic-before-gate habit into future drafting sessions as standard practice, not just when trouble is
expected.
