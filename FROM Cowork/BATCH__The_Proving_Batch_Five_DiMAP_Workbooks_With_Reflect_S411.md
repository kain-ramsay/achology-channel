## The Reflect part, as written (what Kain judges)

A one-line lead-in sits above each set, the same on all five (Cowork's proposal, to be ruled once as fixed copy): *Spend a few minutes with each of these before the exercise. They ask about your own life, and what you write is for you alone.* Each part then carries four questions, each about the reader's own life, none repeating an exercise step or a discussion question (the fresh checkers compared each one against all twelve).

**Karpman Drama Triangle Workbook**

1. Which of the three positions did you see most often in the home or workplace you grew up in, and who held it?

2. Think of a time when you helped someone and felt resentful afterwards. What did you want from them that you didn't receive?

3. Think of a time when you felt blamed by someone. Looking back, what might they have been feeling that you couldn't see then?

4. Recall a time when you turned down a role someone was offering you: the one who fixes, the one who is blamed, or the one who suffers. What made that possible?

**Ladder of Inference Workbook**

1. Think of a time when you were completely sure about someone and later found you were wrong. What had you missed, and what showed you?

2. Which people in your life do you find it easiest to give the benefit of the doubt, and which hardest? What do you think explains the difference?

3. Recall a moment when someone judged you quickly and got it wrong. How did it feel to be on the receiving end?

4. When do you tend to climb fastest: when you are tired, rushed, stressed, or with certain people? What do you notice in yourself just before?

**Johari Window Workbook**

1. Think of an occasion when another person told you something about yourself that you hadn't noticed. What was it, and how did you respond to that information at the time?

2. Recall an occasion when you disclosed something you had previously kept to yourself. What followed, and would you make the same decision again, knowing the outcome?

3. Think of a talent or a reaction you only discovered when life placed you in an unfamiliar situation. What did it take for that discovery to emerge?

4. In which part of your life do you feel most free to be yourself, and in which do you feel most guarded and watchful? What distinguishes those two environments from each other?

**Thoughts Feelings Behaviour Workbook**

1. Who in your life reacts very differently from you to the same kind of event? What do you think they bring to it that you don't?

2. Think of something that upset you years ago and barely troubles you now. What changed: the event, or the way you read it?

3. Which feeling were you taught to hide when you were young, and where does it still show up in your life?

4. Which of your usual reactions would you most like to hold more loosely, and what do you think you would gain?

**Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs Workbook**

1. Think of a time in your life when one need was so unmet that nothing else mattered. What was it, and what did it do to the rest of your life?

2. Which kind of need do you neglect most in everyday life, and what do you tell yourself about why?

3. Whose approval or company have you chased hardest, and was it closeness you wanted from them, or recognition?

4. When have you felt closest to becoming who you are capable of being, and what was in place around you at the time?
