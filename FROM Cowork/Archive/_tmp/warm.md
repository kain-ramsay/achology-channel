## Body

Yes, you can learn psychology on your own, at home. You don't need to enrol anywhere or ask anyone's permission, and you can begin tonight. What studying at home won't do is make you a registered psychologist, because in the United Kingdom that title has rules of its own.

So it helps to know which of the two you're after. Learning for the love of it is yours to start whenever you like. Working under a regulated title is a different road, and a later section shows what that road looks like.

## Where can you learn psychology on your own for free?

A free course is a gentle place to begin, and two well-known ones are easy to find. [OpenLearn](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/health-sports-psychology/psychology/starting-psychology/content-section-0?intro=1) is the Open University's free learning site. Its course called Starting with psychology is introductory, takes about five hours of study, and offers a free statement of participation.

OpenLearn describes that course as a look at the different explanations psychologists have put forward for why people feel, think and behave as they do. Read that description first. You'll soon see whether those are the questions you'd like answered.

Yale University's [Introduction to Psychology](https://www.coursera.org/learn/introduction-psychology) is taught by Paul Bloom on Coursera, an online learning platform. Its page says no prior experience is required, and that it's designed for two weeks at 10 hours a week. It adds that you can go at your own pace.

You can enrol for free, and the Certificate has to be purchased. Have a look at the enrolment page first, so you can see what the free option includes. It's a short read.

## How might you start learning psychology on your own?

Start with an introduction, and see whether psychology holds your interest. A notebook beside you is handy for the moment an idea reminds you of someone you know. Jot it down, and carry on.

When you study alone, it's nice to have someone to talk things over with. Think of learning a language from an app: you can pick up plenty of words by yourself, but saying a few aloud is a different thing. Telling a friend about a new idea is a good way to try it.

## Will learning psychology at home make you a registered psychologist?

Not on its own, and it's handy to know that before you begin. In the United Kingdom, the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC) says there are [nine protected titles](https://www.hcpc-uk.org/news-and-events/blog/2023/understanding-the-regulation-of-psychologists/) for practitioner psychologists, and that anyone using one must be on its Register. They include registered psychologist and clinical psychologist.

The same HCPC page says the job title psychologist, by itself, isn't protected by law. The British Psychological Society (BPS) [says](https://www.bps.org.uk/psychologist/significant-restriction-liberty) the title psychologist isn't a designated title in the legislation, although registered psychologist is. Both pages are about the United Kingdom.

Achology's own answer on [replacing a university degree](/help/outcomes-and-expectations/can-achology-replace-university-degrees/) describes a degree as the main door into regulated jobs such as clinical psychology, and into research. If you live somewhere other than the United Kingdom, your own country's regulator or professional body is the place to ask what its titles need.

## Is it still worth learning psychology if you don't want the title?

Of course it is, and a job doesn't have to be the reason. Maybe you'd like to understand why a parent bristles when you offer advice, or why you go silent in a tense meeting. Learning how minds work can start with moments just like those.

Achology holds that a practitioner goes first: they try what they learn on their own life before sharing it with anyone else. So a free course, or a first Achology course, can be a fair test of whether a longer road, such as a degree, is one you'd like to take.

## Where could you go next with Achology?

Achology teaches applied psychology through self-paced online courses, and none has prerequisites, so a complete beginner is welcome. The [Diploma Course in Modern Applied Psychology (DiMAP)](/academy/neuro-linguistic-programming/diploma-modern-applied-psychology/) has more than 51 hours across 175 lessons. It includes a lesson called The 7 Primary Schools of Psychological Thought, then a section on each of six schools.

The diploma is education, and it isn't a licence. Its certificates are never presented as a degree or a statutory qualification, and [what an Achology certificate proves](/help/certificates-cpd-accreditation/what-achology-certificate-proves/) sets out what each one shows. You're welcome to read around the subject first in [Applied Psychology Explained](/learn/psychology/articles/applied-psychology/).
