## Part C: the series shape

The 24 travel in four movements, from "is the DSM even measuring anything" to "what a person actually does with their own mind." A reader can start anywhere and finish any single one; the order below is for a reader who reads them in sequence.

**Movement one, questions 1 to 6: does the measurement itself hold up.** No blood test (1), no biological cause named (2), agreement is not truth (3), the actual number behind a criterion was picked by consensus, not discovered (4), the core definition changed under the same manual (5), the manual admits its own edges blur (6).

**Movement two, questions 7 to 11: how the list of categories actually grew.** Zoom out to the full historical count (7), one vote in 1973 that took decades to fully land (8), a diagnosis that spiked because of a film rather than biology (9), a modern rise that may be counting, not disease (10), and the mechanism that ties 7 to 10 together, a false epidemic nobody had to lie to create (11).

**Movement three, questions 12 to 19: what the system's own incentives and limits produce.** Whether inflation is even real, checked fairly both ways (12), how far the defining habit has spread past clinical categories into everyday words (13), what it now costs to keep revising the list (14), how much rests on what a person says about themselves (15), how little time exists to tell physical from psychiatric (16), how often the label is simply wrong (17), an everyday mood confused with a criterion (18), and the same confusion, sharper, in a child (19).

**Movement four, questions 20 to 24: what a label does to a person, and what the system's own better answers look like.** What receiving a label does to someone, label aside from accuracy (20), a label's absence gatekeeping help entirely (21), the most universal version of the boundary question, is my grief a disorder (22), a real named alternative that already exists (23), and the close: a label can be weak science and still useful, so what matters is what you do with your own mind next (24).

One line per article on why it stands alone to a reader who arrives at it cold: every DSM fact and every course fact needed to make sense of the piece is explained inside that piece, from primary sources restated in full, not assumed from an earlier article in the series. None of the 24 says "as we covered in article 6." A reader who found article 19 through a search engine and has read nothing else gets the whole argument in that one piece.
