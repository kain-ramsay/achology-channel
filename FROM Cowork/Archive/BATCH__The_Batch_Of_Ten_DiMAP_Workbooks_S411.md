## The Reflect questions, as written (what Kain judges)

The lead-in is the same on all ten (Cowork's proposal, still to be ruled as fixed copy): *Spend a few minutes with each of these before the exercise. They ask about your own life, and what you write is for you alone.*

**Bloom's Taxonomy Workbook**

1. Think back to an idea that you rejected immediately and later came to value. What put you off at first, and what eventually changed your mind about it?
2. Recall something about how to learn, work or live that you believe only because someone you trusted told you, and that you've never checked. Where did the belief come from, and what has it given you?
3. Which kinds of people or subjects are hardest for you to listen to with an open mind? Think back over the last few occasions, and consider what you did in the moment before you reacted.
4. When did you last learn something and put it into practice within a week? What made that particular idea stay with you, when so much of what you've read hasn't?

**Pygmalion Effect Workbook**

1. Think of someone who held very high or very low expectations of you, and describe how you behaved around them because of it.
2. Which beliefs about yourself did you pick up before you were old enough to question them, and whose words or reactions do you remember behind each one?
3. Recall a time when you surprised yourself by doing something you'd thought you couldn't, and say what you had been telling yourself beforehand and what changed afterwards.
4. Is there someone you decided long ago was capable of only so much, and how do you treat them because of that?

**Ikigai Workbook**

1. Think of a time when you, or someone you know, spent years pursuing a direction that proved to be mistaken. What revealed the mistake, and how long did it take to recognise?
2. Recall someone whose way of living you've admired. What did they seem to contribute to other people, and what does that admiration reveal about your own values?
3. Consider a time when your effort felt wasted and a time when it felt worthwhile. What was different about the circumstances?
4. Looking back over your adult life, which decisions did you make mainly for financial reasons and which for other reasons? What did each decision give you, and what did it cost?

**Id Ego Superego Workbook**

1. Which rule you were given as a child do you still follow without ever having decided to? If someone asked why it matters, how would you explain it?
2. In which area of your life do you most often give in to an urge: food, spending, screens, your temper, or something else? What usually happens beforehand?
3. Remember a time you wanted something intensely and were later grateful that you hadn't pursued it. How did you feel that evening, and how do you feel today?
4. Looking back over the past year, which part of you has made most of your important decisions: the wanting, the rule keeping or the weighing up? Does that answer surprise you?

**Defence Mechanisms Workbook**

1. Think of a time when facing something hard changed you for the better. What did you have to accept first before anything could move, and who or what helped you accept it?
2. When you're alone with nothing to distract you, what do your thoughts turn to, and how comfortable is it to stay with them? How okay are you with yourself in those moments?
3. Which family member have you most wanted not to resemble? In what ways do you notice them in yourself, and where did you learn those ways?
4. Is there a grudge you still carry? What has it protected you from, such as feeling exposed or losing control, and what has it cost you?

**Kohlberg's Stages of Moral Development Workbook**

1. Looking back over your childhood, which were the first rules you remember living by, and what happened on the occasions when you broke one of them?
2. Over the years, whose approval have you worked hardest to keep, and has the group of people whose opinion matters to you changed as you've got older?
3. Which rule that you were taught when you were young do you still follow today without ever having decided for yourself that it was right, and where do you think it came from?
4. Whose sense of right and wrong have you admired most, and what did you see that person do in a difficult situation that showed you what they stand for?

**Perceptual Positions Workbook**

1. Recall an occasion on which you felt certain that someone had treated you unfairly, and later learned that they'd seen the same event very differently. What changed your mind, and what helped you see it?
2. Which of the three positions do you spend most of your days in, and in which kinds of situations do you find the other two hardest to reach?
3. Recall someone who helped you see a situation from outside your own view, such as a friend or a coach. What did they say or ask that made it possible?
4. After a hard conversation, when do you tend to replay it, and what do you hope the replaying will show you about yourself or the other person?

**ABCDE Model Workbook**

1. Think of a feeling that has come back again and again in your life, and write about when you first noticed it and what was happening around you at that time.
2. Growing up, how did the adults around you explain their own moods, and what did you learn from them about where a feeling comes from?
3. Which of your beliefs about yourself or other people have you held for so long that they now feel like established facts, and where do you think each one came from?
4. Think of a time when you changed your mind about something important to you, and write about what made the difference and how long the change took to become settled.

**Rogers Core Conditions Workbook**

1. Who in your life do you feel free to be completely yourself with? What's it about them, or about the way you're around them, that makes that possible?
2. Think of a time when someone gave you the benefit of the doubt after you'd let them down. What did it change in how you acted afterwards?
3. When has someone tried to solve your problem before they'd understood it? How did it feel, and what did you want from them instead?
4. Whose trust in you has grown slowly over the years? What did they see you do, again and again, that earned it?

**Choice Theory Workbook**

1. Recall a stretch of life when someone close to you kept trying to change you. How did the way they went about it affect what you did next?
2. Who has listened to you so well that you came away feeling understood, and what did they do that other people don't?
3. Which of the habits from Part One filled the house you were raised in, and which was hardly ever seen there? What did that teach you about how people treat one another?
4. When you feel let down by someone you care about, what do you usually do first, and where do you think you learned to do it?
