Course, book or app: how should you learn mindfulness?

Perhaps a friend mentioned mindfulness, or you saw an app one quiet evening, and now you're wondering where to begin. There are three common ways in, and each suits a different kind of beginner. So how should you learn mindfulness: with a course, a book or an app?

An app gives you short guided practices, a book gives you ideas, and a course puts both in a set order. Plenty of people use more than one, and nobody gets mindfulness right on day one, so there's no wrong place to start.

What can a mindfulness app do for you?

A mindfulness app puts a guide in your pocket, so you can try a short practice almost anywhere, and beginning that way is a perfectly good choice. Headspace says it offers more than 1,000 expert-led exercises, and Insight Timer describes itself as a library of guided meditations from many different teachers.

What an app finds harder is helping you make sense of what you notice. Masaryk University in the Czech Republic publishes a guide to mindfulness practice which says that in the vast majority of cases with apps there's no reflection on what you experience in your mindfulness practice.

What can a book give you that an app can't?

A book lets you slow down and think about why mindfulness matters, which suits anyone who likes to understand something before trying it. Jon Kabat-Zinn's Wherever You Go, There You Are is one place you could begin, and its publisher, Balance, has released an updated edition with a new introduction and afterword.

What a book can't do is notice whether you're really practising. Reading about attention can feel like progress, so it's easy to finish a chapter and carry on exactly as before.

What does a course add that an app or a book doesn't?

A course puts the practice and the understanding in a set order. Each lesson builds on the one before it. Some courses are live groups with a teacher.

The Masaryk guide describes an eight-week programme that meets weekly and asks for about 30 minutes of home practice a day. Other courses are self-paced and online, so you study at your own speed.

Which one is right for you?

Say you realise you've walked home from work and can't remember any of it, because your mind was on tomorrow's meeting. An app could give you a two-minute practice for that evening, and a book could explain why attention drifts. A course could go further and teach you to notice the drifting as it happens.

If you're curious and want a gentle introduction, an app is a sensible beginning. If you enjoy ideas and like to think them through slowly, a book will suit you. If you want to learn mindfulness by understanding your own mind, a course is made for that.

How does Achology.com teach mindfulness?

Achology.com is a school of applied psychology that teaches online, and Kain and Karen Ramsay founded it. Its self-paced courses are open to the general public, and a learning community lets students practise what they study with one another instead of only reading about it.

Achology.com teaches mindfulness as applied psychology, which means using it to understand your own mind rather than following a tradition. It's taught in plain language and doesn't ask you to follow any religion, and you learn it on yourself first. That order matters, because nobody else can spot your attention drifting on your behalf.

Achology.com's courses are online and self-paced, so they suit you less when what you want is a live teacher and classmates in the same room. Otherwise, you don't need any background in psychology to begin.

Where can you learn mindfulness with Achology.com?

Two of the mindfulness courses at Achology.com are made for people who want to learn mindfulness for themselves and grow more mindful day to day:

  • Mindfulness Practitioner Diploma Course (Beginner to Expert): the full course, 33+ hours across 124 lessons, including lesson 12, Contrastive Analysis: Conscious Awareness Vs. Autopilot.
  • Mindfulness for Mental Health, Personal Growth and Inner Peace: a shorter course of 14+ hours across 52 lessons.

The Diploma suits you if you want to study the whole subject from beginning to end, because it starts from the foundations and builds gradually. The shorter course suits you if you'd like a smaller first step before committing to more hours.

Finishing the Diploma earns a certificate of completion, which shows you worked through the whole course. A certificate of achievement is separate, showing progress along the accreditation pathway, which the Society of Modern Applied Psychology (SoMAP) checks independently. Neither is a licence, as this guide to what each certificate proves explains.

Whichever way you begin, give yourself permission to be a beginner for as long as it takes. A small first step could be five quiet minutes tonight, phone put away, noticing where your attention wanders and gently bringing it back. That's a fine place to start, and you can build from there at your own pace.

Both courses are taught by Kain Ramsay. Achology is also listed on the UK Register of Learning Providers (UKRLP) with the UK Provider Number UKRLP 10099815.

Answer 2 · keyword: mindfulness courses · about 799 words

Which mindfulness course should you take, and why?

You've probably got a few tabs open by now, each one describing a different way into mindfulness, and it isn't easy to tell which one is meant for someone like you. Feeling a little overwhelmed by that is completely ordinary, and it doesn't mean you're choosing badly.

So which mindfulness course should you take? The right one is the course whose format matches how you like to learn, because mindfulness courses run from teacher-led groups to university programmes to self-paced lessons at home. None of them is a wrong door, because each was built with a different kind of learner in mind.

They differ in how long they take, who guides you, and how much explanation comes with the practice. Someone who wants a teacher in the room will want a different course from someone who prefers to study alone. What matters is which setting helps you keep going, and that's personal to you.

What are some examples of how mindfulness courses are run?

Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) was developed at UMass Memorial Medical Center, and the centre's site says its certified instructors guide participants through eight weeks of shared discovery. Other organisations run courses of their own, and the Oxford Mindfulness Centre lists group courses, one-to-one courses, retreats and live online meditation. It's a not-for-profit organisation that partners with the University of Oxford.

Bangor University in the United Kingdom lists master's degrees in mindfulness and compassion that you can study online, alongside short courses. Degrees suit academic study, while a self-paced course is the closer match if you'd rather learn at home, and there's nothing lesser about choosing that.

Which mindfulness course fits your own life?

Say you're standing in a supermarket queue and notice you've become irritated with the person ahead of you, and you'd like to stay calmer. You can practise that at home in your own time, or with a group beside you. Achology's course has a lesson for moments like that, called "How to Create a Space Between Stimulus and Response".

If you know you'd stick with it better on a set evening with other people, choose a group course, because Achology isn't the right fit for that: its courses are self-paced and online. If your evenings change from week to week, lessons you can open any time suit you better, and it's fine to choose what fits your real life.

What does Achology's mindfulness course teach?

Achology.com, founded by Kain and Karen Ramsay, teaches applied psychology through self-paced online courses. Applied psychology means using what's known about the mind in everyday life. Its Mindfulness Practitioner Diploma Course (Beginner to Expert) has 124 lessons in five sections.

The course teaches mindfulness as a way of understanding how your own mind works. The first section covers the foundations, with lessons such as "Debunking the Common Myths About Mindfulness" and "The Nine Fundamental Attitudes of Mindfulness". The second turns to self-awareness, and the third to self-discipline and growth.

The fourth section looks at mindfulness through psychology, including "The Fundamental Ideas of Cognitive Psychology". The fifth turns to other people, in lessons such as "Learning How to Listen Mindfully (and Hear!)". You'll receive a certificate of completion at the end, which shows you finished the course and isn't a licence to work in a regulated profession.

Where should you begin among Achology's mindfulness courses?

Two of Achology's mindfulness courses suit someone who wants to learn mindfulness itself, and both are self-paced and online. Neither asks for any earlier study, so you can start straight away. Which one you start with depends on how much you want to take on at once.

  • Mindfulness Practitioner Diploma Course (Beginner to Expert): the whole syllabus, which runs to more than 33 hours in 124 lessons.
  • Mindfulness for Mental Health, Personal Growth and Inner Peace: the shorter course, with more than 14 hours in 52 lessons.

If you'd like everything in one place, the Mindfulness Practitioner Diploma Course (Beginner to Expert) is the one to take. For a smaller first step, the shorter course is under half the length. Both let you fit the lessons around your own week.

Whichever course you choose, you've already done something useful by thinking about how you learn. A small next step is to open the page of the course that caught your eye and read its list of lessons, to see whether the topics appeal to you. There's no hurry, and you can return whenever your week has more room.

Can you learn mindfulness online, or even for free?

Maybe you'd like to try mindfulness, but money feels tight this month or your evenings are full, and you're wondering whether you can learn it online without paying. You can, and there's nothing awkward about asking. There's a full free mindfulness course with eight weeks of practice and study, and there are free guided meditations recorded by a university centre.

Each of them does a different job, and starting with something free is a sensible and respectable way to begin. Learning mindfulness has two halves, and free material covers the first half very well. That half is practising, which means sitting with a recording or a lesson and paying attention on purpose.

The other half is understanding why your attention drifts and what that looks like in an ordinary day, and a structured course suits that half better. Most people want both halves in time. Beginning with the first one, on your own terms, is a perfectly good place to start.

Is there a free mindfulness course you can take online?

Yes, and the best known is Palouse Mindfulness, a free online Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) course. MBSR is an eight-week programme founded by Jon Kabat-Zinn, and the Palouse course is based on it and was created by a certified MBSR instructor. It was made for people who can't get to a live class because of money or practical difficulties.

If that sounds like you, you're exactly who it was made for, and there's nothing second-rate about choosing it. The course is self-guided and self-paced, with eight weeks of practice and study and 13 guided meditations. Nobody sets your pace, so it asks for some self-discipline, and you can begin today.

If you finish and send in the materials the site asks for, you can request a certificate of completion at no cost. It's worth choosing a weekly rhythm that fits your life, and a slow start is still a start. You're allowed to go at the speed your days allow.

Where can you find free guided meditations from a university?

UCLA Mindful, the mindfulness education centre at UCLA Health, offers free guided meditations to stream or download, from 3 to 19 minutes long in English. They're recorded by the centre's director, Diana Winston, and many other languages are offered as well. A free iOS and Android app carries them too.

They give you practice rather than a course to follow, so they suit a slow, gentle start. A few minutes on a quiet morning is a perfectly good beginning, and nobody's checking how long you sit. If you miss a day, you can simply pick it up again the next.

What does a free mindfulness course leave for you to work out?

Say you finish a 10-minute recording and feel settled, then spend the evening replaying something you said in a meeting. That's a human thing to do, and it doesn't mean you're doing it wrong. The recording gave you practice at steadying your attention, while replaying the past is a separate pattern you have to understand before you can spot it.

Understanding that pattern is where a course helps, because it explains why the mind loops and how to notice it happening. A recording isn't built to explain that, so the explaining is usually left to you. Achology's smaller course has a lesson on exactly this, lesson 5, "The Endless Spiral of Self-Awareness (and Over-Thinking)".

What does Achology Gateway give you if you want to start free?

Achology Gateway is the free level of the Achology learning community, so you can join without paying. Inside, you'll find free events on weekdays, a board where members offer free coaching, a discussion forum and an ethics session that runs every quarter. It isn't a mindfulness course, but you can look around before you spend a penny.

How can you learn mindfulness online with Achology?

If you'd like to learn mindfulness properly, the longer of Achology's two courses is the one to take, and both are for becoming more mindful yourself. Neither trains you to teach it, and they don't promise a result. Both are online and self-paced, and neither is free.

Achology's courses concentrate on how your own mind works, and they ask you to try each idea in your own day. If you came looking for something free or cheap, the smaller course is a lower-priced way in, and the longer one can follow. Wanting to keep the cost down is a sensible thing to want.

  • Mindfulness Practitioner Diploma Course (Beginner to Expert): the one to take to learn mindfulness properly, with more than 33 hours across 124 lessons.
  • Mindfulness for Mental Health, Personal Growth and Inner Peace: the smaller, lower-priced way in, with more than 14 hours across 52 lessons.

You don't have to decide everything today. A few weeks with one free recording will show you whether daily practice suits you. If you'd like a small first step, choose a quiet ten minutes this week, press play on one of the UCLA recordings, and notice how it feels.

Is a mindfulness course or qualification really worth it?

A friend mentions the mindfulness course she took, and afterwards you find yourself wondering whether a mindfulness qualification is worth the time and the money for you. Asking that is a wise question, and plenty of thoughtful people ask it before they spend anything. The honest answer depends on what you're hoping to get from it.

If you want to learn mindfulness and become more mindful in your own life, wanting that for yourself is reason enough, and a good course can suit you well. If you were hoping a certificate would let you teach mindfulness to others, that's a different road, and it's kind to know so before you spend anything.

Achology.com is an online school of applied psychology that Kain and Karen Ramsay founded. It teaches members of the public through courses you take at your own pace. Alongside the courses runs a learning community, where students try out new skills together.

Its Mindfulness Practitioner Diploma Course is a learning qualification. The diploma records that you studied the whole course and finished it, and it isn't a teaching licence. Achology doesn't train mindfulness teachers.

Does a mindfulness qualification let you teach mindfulness?

Not on its own, and that's true of learning courses everywhere, not just Achology's. Teaching a set programme such as Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) has its own teacher training. A certificate from a general mindfulness qualification doesn't replace that training.

Around the world, trainers work together to offer MBSR teacher training. The Global Mindfulness Collaborative launched in November 2022 with 15 members in 13 countries, and each trains teachers in its own country and language. If teaching is your hope, that's a lovely aim, and the member nearest you is the place to start.

What does the road to teaching MBSR ask of you?

In the United States, for example, the Brown University Mindfulness Center's pathway asks for at least two years of your own meditation practice. It also asks for an eight-week MBSR course and a first silent retreat before training starts. Once a trainee finishes the first level, they're qualified to teach the eight-week programme.

In the United Kingdom, the British Association of Mindfulness-Based Approaches (BAMBA) is one example. It says it's the UK's main professional body for mindfulness practitioners, teachers and teacher trainers. Its guidelines ask teachers for at least 12 months of training, daily practice of their own, yearly retreats and regular supervision.

What is a mindfulness course worth if you don't want to teach?

Most people who ask this want to learn mindfulness for themselves, and a mindfulness qualification suits that aim well. Wanting it for your own life is a complete reason, and it needs no career plan behind it.

Picture someone in a long meeting who notices they've spent 10 minutes planning a reply instead of listening. A good course teaches you to notice that and come back to what's in front of you.

A course gives you a set order to learn in, exercises to practise and a clear finish. That structure suits people who like to follow a planned route from start to end. If you'd rather choose your own order, a course may feel too fixed.

What does the Diploma certificate prove to someone else?

A certificate of completion shows that you worked through the whole course and finished it. Achology's separate certificate of achievement comes only after an outside body has checked your progress along its own pathway. You'll receive the certificate of completion when you finish the Diploma.

Achology's accreditation comes from the Society of Modern Applied Psychology (SoMAP). SoMAP sits outside Achology and checks the practice behind each certificate of achievement. If an employer or client asks what your certificate means, you can say it shows the study you finished, and that only the achievement certificate has had its practice checked by SoMAP.

What kind of course is Achology's Diploma?

The Diploma is a mindfulness qualification that teaches mindfulness as a way to know your own mind. It uses everyday language, and it's for people who want to become more mindful in their own lives. If you're hoping to teach it to others, ask the teacher training group what earlier study it accepts, because Achology can't speak for it.

Which Achology mindfulness course should you start with?

Two Achology courses teach mindfulness for your own use, and you can take both at your own pace. Start with the Diploma for the full course. If you'd rather take a shorter step first, begin with the smaller one:

  • Mindfulness Practitioner Diploma Course (Beginner to Expert): the full course, with more than 33 hours and 124 lessons.
  • Mindfulness for Mental Health, Personal Growth and Inner Peace: the smaller first step, made of 52 lessons that add up to more than 14 hours.

If you'd like a small first step, write down in a sentence or two why you want to learn mindfulness. That sentence will help you see which course fits, and whichever you choose, you can take it whenever suits you.
