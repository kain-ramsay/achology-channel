## kain-ramsay

**intro**
Kain Ramsay, together with his wife, Karen, founded Achology in 2017 and created its current curriculum of twenty-eight online training courses, many of which he personally teaches. He first received training in teaching after his promotion while serving in the Royal Engineers, part of the British Army.

**bio**
Kain is Achology's Chief Technical Officer and leads a monthly mentorship group that all active Achology members can participate in. When Kain writes, he does so from the perspective of a seasoned practitioner with decades of experience in applied psychology, coaching, counselling work, and a deep understanding of what professional competency in the field entails. His work integrates philosophy, theology, and contemporary psychological insights, aiming to help readers better understand themselves.

**disclosure**
Kain is the author of two popular books: The Ultimate Life Coaching Handbook: A Guide to the Methodology, Principles, and Practice of Life Coaching, and Responsibility Rebellion, a Practical Guide to Personal Empowerment.

**intro**
Karen Ramsay co-founded Achology with her husband, Kain, in 2017, and leads the academy as its Chief Executive Officer. Before that, she was a singer and songwriter, with two albums of her own still available on Spotify, and she came to this work from a life spent creating valuable things that benefit people.

She built the academy from the ground up alongside Kain and still makes decisions the same way she did at the start, by asking what a change means for the people it affects rather than what it does for the growth of the business. Achology members can hear from her directly, which is unusual at this size and is entirely deliberate.

**disclosure**
Karen sets the standard for how Achology welcomes its members and is responsible for the events calendar that ensures the community meets regularly each year, promoting strong connections and active engagement.

## gerard-egan

**intro**
Gerard Egan wrote The Skilled Helper, the most popular counselling textbook worldwide, and teaches at Achology as a guest instructor. He is Professor Emeritus at Loyola University Chicago, where he dedicated most of his career. Philosophy, rather than a university department, influenced his path to psychology.

**bio**
Gerard developed the three-stage helping model used in counselling courses worldwide, refining it across eleven editions over more than forty years. As a master, he revisited the same questions throughout his life, gradually understanding them better. His work reflects a clarity that comes from confidence and experience. He firmly believes that helping is a set of learnable skills rather than innate talents, which led him to dedicate his later career to teaching these skills to people outside the professional helping fields.

**disclosure**
Gerard filmed his course with Achology in Scotland in 2019, allowing students, for the first time, to learn the skilled helper model from the man who created it rather than from someone else's secondhand description or account.

## amelia-sinclair

**intro**
Amelia A. Sinclair serves as the senior overseeing editorial voice of the Achology Knowledge Hub and the guardian of how everything published there reads. She sets the standard every article, book note, and quote page is held to, and nothing reaches you, the reader, without her direct approval.

**bio**
Amelia writes with concise precision, drawing from extensive reading to justify each element's importance. Her criteria are strict: if a part doesn't offer the reader something practical, thought-provoking, or debatable, it won't be published. She cares more about whether a sentence earns its spot than about grammatical correctness, and she isn't afraid to remove even admired paragraphs if they weaken the overall piece. While other writers may excel in specific styles, Amelia sets the standard that all must meet.

**disclosure**
Amelia openly acknowledges that her editorial style is heavily influenced by the works of William Strunk Jr., whose classic guide, The Elements of Style, can still be accessed for free via Project Gutenberg.

## benjamin-lockwood

**intro**
Benjamin Lockwood writes the book notes for the Achology Knowledge Hub, weighing each work fairly and placing it within the tradition that it belongs to. He leads the academy's book research, deciding which titles earn a place in the library and what an honest account of each of them should look like.

**bio**
Benjamin summarises each book into a straightforward overview of its main message and shortcomings, aimed at curious readers who prefer learning its core idea in five minutes rather than attending a lecture. He explores broadly across psychology, philosophy, and personal growth, treating weak and strong books alike by stating their content in plain and simple terms. He only recommends books he personally values and does not dismiss titles just because they have become unpopular among other reviewers.

**disclosure**
Benjamin openly acknowledges that his approach to appraising books is deeply influenced by the philosopher Anthony Kenny, whose A New History of Western Philosophy is published by Oxford University Press in Britain.

## charlotte-avery

**intro**
Charlotte J. Avery writes the research-led articles in the Achology Knowledge Hub, the ones that draw on real studies, frameworks, and evidence. She grounds her claims in solid reasoning, and she says so plainly whenever the evidence turns out to be thinner than the popular narrative might suggest.

**bio**
Charlotte approaches her work like a curious investigator, sharing her discoveries reflectively, rather than 'lecturing' anybody about them. She traces ideas that have become hardened into common knowledge back to their origins and clarifies what is genuinely proven versus what is just widely, or more popularly, accepted. Each finding is then anchored in something that a reader can recognise in their own daily life, because a study that no one can relate to is likely to become a study that nobody ends up using.

**disclosure**
Charlotte openly acknowledges that her investigative style is deeply influenced by Michael Faraday, whose celebrated lectures, The Chemical History of a Candle, are still freely available at Project Gutenberg today.

## declan-fitzpatrick

**intro**
Declan Fitzpatrick writes the idea-led articles in the Achology Knowledge Hub, the ones where a single sharp insight matters far more than the weight of evidence stacked up behind it. He opens each piece with a reframe that makes a reader see something entirely familiar in a rather different way.

**bio**
Declan then stays with that reframe long enough to show why it matters far more than what it possibly first appeared to, and then he stops, because an idea that has properly landed does not need any defending at all. His paragraphs are short by design, moving a reader quickly through substantial ideas without ever feeling thin or hurried along by them. He writes for the person who already half-suspects the very thing he is saying, and who has simply never once had it put into plain words for them before now.

**disclosure**
Declan openly acknowledges that his essay style is deeply influenced by Ralph Waldo Emerson, whose collection Essays: First Series is still freely available at Project Gutenberg for anybody who's interested in reading it.

## evelyn-montgomery

**intro**
Evelyn Montgomery writes the most approachable articles for the Achology Knowledge Hub, designed for readers who may be encountering psychological concepts for the first time in their lives. She presumes no prior knowledge and always treats her readers with respect, never condescending.

**bio**
Evelyn starts with a moment, or a common experience a reader is likely to recognise from their own week, explains it in plain words, and then pulls back to show how it connects to something much larger about people. She treats her reader as a mature, responsible adult throughout, which means she explains the bigger ideas and 'harder parts' rather than quietly skipping over or ignoring them. Nothing is ever simplified to the point where it stops being true, and nothing at all is dressed up to sound cleverer than it really is in practice.

**disclosure**
Evelyn openly acknowledges that her plain, warm writing style is deeply influenced by William James, whose Talks to Teachers on Psychology is still freely available at Project Gutenberg for anyone who wants to read it.

## frederick-martin

**intro**
Frederick S. Martín writes the quote pages of the Achology Knowledge Hub, taking a single familiar line and opening it up patiently until its meaning can genuinely be felt by a reader rather than simply agreed with and then forgotten about again the moment somebody happens to scroll past it.

**bio**
Frederick works with remarkable precision, pinpointing the exact point at which a quote becomes genuinely compelling, or simply confusing, and then taking a reader straight there without any unnecessary detours along the way. He then closes on a question that turns out to be considerably sharper than it had at first appeared to be. His writing is the most concise on the whole of the site, with every single word in it earning the exact place that it occupies, a skill that is much more challenging than producing lengthy prose.

**disclosure**
Frederick openly admits that his straightforward writing style is heavily inspired by Bertrand Russell, whose book The Problems of Philosophy remains accessible for free on Project Gutenberg for anyone interested in reading it.

## isabella-whitmore

**intro**
Isabella S. Whitmore answers the questions that people quietly ask themselves before they enrol on anything at all: the commercial ones about cost, about comparisons with all the other providers out there, and about whether the reviews they happen to be reading can really be trusted.

**bio**
Isabella demonstrates the proficiency of a seasoned analyst by contextualising each inquiry within the broader reality of how training and credentials function in this domain, rather than relying on the polished narratives of promotional materials. She maintains a demeanour that is neither defensive nor promotional, explicitly acknowledging the limitations of what Achology provides alongside its strengths. Her approach involves presenting an overview of the entire landscape, thereby allowing the reader to assess its suitability for their needs.

**disclosure**
Isabella openly admits that her analytical approach is heavily inspired by Walter Bagehot, whose book Lombard Street is still available for free on Project Gutenberg for anyone who may be interested in reading it.

## jackson-hartley

**intro**
Jackson P. Hartley writes in a descriptively compelling tone for readers who know they have a problem but haven't yet identified its source or what a viable solution might be. He always begins by describing the situation in a relatable way rather than jumping straight to the answer he intends to provide.

**bio**
Jackson begins by vividly capturing the felt experience of 'being stuck', describing it accurately enough that a reader feels genuinely understood by him rather than merely diagnosed from a comfortable distance. Only then does he open the view out towards what an honest way forward might actually involve for that particular person. His writing carries the warmth of someone who has seen this struggle many times before and still cares enough to describe it accurately, relatably, and understandably.

**disclosure**
Jackson openly acknowledges that his human, thinking-aloud style is deeply influenced by Michel de Montaigne, whose Essays are still freely available at Project Gutenberg for anybody at all who wants to read them.
