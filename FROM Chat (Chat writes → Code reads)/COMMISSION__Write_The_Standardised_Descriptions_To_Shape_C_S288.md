## Your four questions, answered

**One, what a lecture description is for.** It tells a person what one lecture teaches, plainly enough that someone who has not bought the course knows what they would get, and someone inside the course can find the lecture again. Three readers: the buyer on the course page, the enrolled student in Circle, and the search engines and language models reading the page. Vimeo carries the same text because Vimeo is one of the places it has to live, not because anyone reads it there.

**Two, its shape.** Question first, then the answer, then what to do with it. Full specification in `The_Standardisation_Rule_Set.md`, now V2, in the Course + Lesson Data | MASTER folder. Read it before writing a word; it is the instruction, and this file only frames it.

**Three, what makes it citeable.** The answer paragraph stands on its own. A person who never watches the video still learns the thing. That is the whole mechanism: a self contained passage under a real question is what a language model can quote and what a search engine can rank.

**Four, where the output goes.** `Standardised Description`, as already specified. `Lesson Description` is never edited, so Karen's original stays readable beside every change. No new column.

## The shape

Three parts, a blank line between each, 90 to 120 words total, plain text only, no bullets or headings, because the same text lands in a Vimeo text box, a Circle lesson panel and a website curriculum browser.

1. **The question line.** One sentence ending in a question mark. The question this lecture answers, written as a person would type it.
2. **The answer.** One paragraph, 60 to 85 words, self contained.
3. **The closing line.** One or two sentences on what the learner does with it, or why it matters. Never a summary.

**Kain's approved example**, lesson 023-003:

> What are the four types of goal, and why does the difference matter?
>
> Goals fall into four categories. Lifelong visionary goals are the overarching aspirations that give your life or your organisation its purpose. Long term goals break that vision into targets spanning several years. Medium term milestone goals act as stepping stones you can measure progress against. Short term goals set the immediate action.
>
> The distinction matters because the four only become a route when they connect. A short term goal with nothing above it is just activity, and a visionary goal with nothing beneath it stays a wish.

## The three things most likely to go wrong, named in advance

**The repeated title.** Almost every source description opens by restating the lesson title. It goes, every time. The title renders directly above the description in all three places.

**Reporting the video instead of teaching the point.** No "In this video", no "In this lesson", no "Kain discusses". Write the substance.

**Invention.** Everything in the new description comes from the original description for that row. If the original does not say it, it is not written. A row that would need invention to reach 90 words is written shorter and flagged.
