## What Kain is asking

He is asking whether these masters can be made substantially smaller without any student seeing a difference.

## The reasoning behind it, so you can test the premise rather than the conclusion

**Vimeo re-encodes everything on ingest.** No student ever watches the file we upload. They watch Vimeo's own rendition of it. So the master only has to be good enough to feed Vimeo's encoder without visible loss, and it does not have to be an editing master.

**These are editing exports.** Nobody has looked at what settings they carry. If they are ProRes, or high bitrate H.264 at settings chosen for editing rather than delivery, they are very likely far larger than Vimeo needs.

**The honest catch, which is why this is a question and not an instruction.** Re-encoding an already lossy file adds a second generation of loss. Whether that is visible depends entirely on what the source actually is, and nobody has measured it. So the premise gets tested, never assumed.

## What would settle it

**One. The facts.** Read the technical properties of a spread of masters, say fifteen files across several courses and several years, chosen to include the largest and the smallest. For each: container, video codec, bitrate, resolution, frame rate, audio codec and audio bitrate, and file size. What we are looking for is whether the bitrate is far above what the resolution needs, which is the signature of an export never tuned for delivery.

**Two. One test.** Take a single lecture, not one queued for the run, and re-encode it to a sensible delivery setting. Then compare, honestly: the size before and after, and the two files viewed side by side at full screen. Say plainly whether you can see a difference, and where you would expect one to show if there is any (fast motion, screen recordings, text on slides, gradients).

**Three. The sums, if the test passes.** What the whole library would come to at that setting, against the 2.767 TB it is now, and what that would do to the 16.06 of 20 TB on Karen's Drive.

## What is at stake, so you can size the answer

**Not video quality on the site**, if the premise holds. What it buys is upload time across 2,146 files, and headroom on a Drive that is 80 per cent full and is the thing this whole stream runs on.

**What it does not buy is Vimeo storage.** Vimeo bills its own renditions, and those are made from whatever we send. A smaller master does not make a smaller rendition.

## The one thing that would kill it

A visible difference on the test file. If you can see it, say so plainly and the idea stops there. Kain would rather keep the big files than ship softer video to paying students, and no amount of saved space changes that.
