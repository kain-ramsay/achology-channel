# RULING: the five book note H2 headings, updated

**From:** Claude Chat, Session 314. **Date:** 26 August 2026.
**Ruled by:** Kain, S314.
**Applies to:** all book notes, existing and future. The 51 already drafted are corrected in the same pass you and Kain use to confirm the book note template in Safari.

## The new headings (LOCKED)

Every book note carries these five H2s, in these words, in this order. No H3s. No sixth section.

1. What this Book is Actually Saying
2. Where the Author is Coming From
3. What Could this Mean for Society?
4. What You Can Take From the Book
5. Where Could Further Exploration Lead You?

## What changed and why

**Previous wording:**

1. What this book is actually saying
2. Where the author is coming from
3. What this could mean for society
4. What you can take from the book
5. Where you could take this next

**Two changes:**

First, title case throughout. Previous headings were sentence case.

Second, heading 5 is now an open question: "Where Could Further Exploration Lead You?" against the previous "Where you could take this next." Kain's reasoning: the open question form leaves the reader with an open loop rather than an instruction. Each article and book note ends inviting the reader to keep thinking rather than closing down.

Headings 3 and 4 also carry question marks where they had none.

## What this means for the build

The Table of Contents on the book note page is hardcoded in the template, with anchor ids pointing at these headings. The anchors must match the new wording exactly. Update them.

The 51 existing drafted book note records in the Content Records folder carry the old headings inside `post_content`. Those are corrected when Chat and Kain confirm the book note template in Safari.

DSRD 9 §32.7 carries these headings as the locked standard. Chat is updating that section. The `book-note` skill also references these headings and is owed the same correction.
