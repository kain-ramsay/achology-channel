## 25. Is Self-Criticism Ever Useful, and How Do You Quieten It?

There's a voice in your head that's quick to tell you what's wrong with you, and lately it sounds so sure of itself that you've started to take its word for it. It begins with the mistake and ends with a verdict on who you are, and you're left feeling small and worn out. Some days you wonder whether it's the only thing keeping you honest, or the very thing keeping you stuck.

If you've been asking yourself, "Is self criticism good?", the answer at Achology is that it depends on what the criticism looks at. Looking at what you did and asking what you could do differently gives you something to act on. Attacking who you are gives you nothing. You can quieten that second voice by asking it for evidence, and you don't need to fight it.

Once you can tell the two apart, it's fine to stop and ask what the voice is for, and whether it has told you anything you can use. You'll be able to keep the part that points at something within your reach. The part that only runs you down can be questioned, and you can leave it unanswered.

## 26. What Is Self-Understanding, and Why Does It Matter So Much?

Ask yourself who you are and the answer comes out half finished: a job, a few traits, something you were once told. You'd have thought you'd know by now, and being unsure makes you a little uneasy. You suspect you don't know yourself as well as you'd like, and you want to know whether that matters.

So, what is self-understanding? It's knowing what you are as a person, how you identify yourself, and where your picture of yourself came from, so that you can see how the pieces fit together. It matters because every choice you make starts from some picture of yourself, and you can only take responsibility for a life you can see clearly. At Achology we teach mindfulness as that kind of looking: paying attention to yourself right now, to what you're doing and why you're doing it.

By the end, you'll see what a person is made of and how to check the picture you carry of yourself. You'll see where that picture came from, too. You'll also have one sentence about yourself to finish and question, so the half-finished answer becomes something you can look at.

## 27. Why Do Counsellors Need Theory, and Their Own Counselling?

Sometimes the judgement comes before the other person has finished speaking, and the guilt follows a second later. You wanted to be glad for them, and instead you were measuring their choice against your own. You haven't said a word, but you know it's shaping how you listen, and you're unsure whether caring about people is enough to be any good at helping them.

So why do counsellors need theory, and why are they asked to look at their own lives? That quick judgement holds most of the answer: theory gives a helper a reason to hear the explanation out before judging it, and self-knowledge lets the helper notice that a judgement has risen at all. Some counselling bodies name grounding in theory in their published standards, and others name personal development or self-examination, each in its own words.

Read on and you'll be able to say what a theory does for a helper, why looking at your own habits comes first, and where each one stops. You'll see what four counselling bodies publish in their own words, so you aren't relying on a general impression. Then you can decide how much of either to take on.

## 28. How Do You Actually Do Person-Centred Therapy in Practice?

Someone you care about says something heavy, and you freeze. You want to help, but you're afraid of saying the wrong thing, so you either rush in with cheerful reassurance or you go quiet and feel useless. You suspect there's a proper way to listen that nobody ever taught you.

So how do you actually do person-centred therapy in practice? It rests on three attitudes that Carl Rogers named: empathy, congruence and unconditional positive regard, which you see in how closely the practitioner listens and in how much of the direction is left with the other person. People who search for person centred therapy techniques usually hope for a list. There are techniques, but each one is a way of showing those attitudes, and none works as a trick on its own.

That matters because, when you fear saying the wrong thing, a technique can look like the answer. Once you see that the skills carry the attitudes, you can stop hunting for the perfect sentence and pay attention to the person. You'll know what reflection and silence are for, and you can try one of them in your next hard conversation.

## 29. Who Invented CBT, and How Did It Change Modern Psychology?

CBT didn't have one inventor, however tidy the story sounds when it's told that way. It had two, working apart, within about a decade of each other. Each of them started from a different place, with a different name for what they were building. What they had in common matters more than who came first.

So, who invented CBT? Albert Ellis got there first, in the 1950s, with what he called Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy. Aaron Beck followed in the 1960s with what he called Cognitive Therapy. They weren't working together, and they built different therapies, but both had broken with the same older idea in psychology.

Next you'll see what each of them contributed, and the one break from earlier psychology that they both made. You'll also see why CBT is still taught in more than one form, and what that means if you're looking for a single founder to name. By the end you'll be able to name both people and roughly when each worked, in two sentences.

## 30. Which Life Coaching Books Are Worth Reading, and Why?

Wanting to get good at helping people is the easy part, and choosing which books to trust is not. Every list recommends something different, and you can't tell which authors have actually sat opposite a real client. Each wrong choice costs weeks, and you'd rather not start again from the beginning. A way to tell them apart before you spend the time would help.

So what are the best life coaching books to start with? For someone starting out, they fall into a sensible order. Begin with a short book about asking instead of telling, move on to two books that each set out a complete coaching method, and read Gerard Egan's textbook when you want to understand the structure underneath. No book will make you a coach, and publishers' sales figures tell you how well a book sold, not whether its method works, so read them with your own judgement switched on.

Ahead, you'll see which book to open first and why, what the two full-method books each stand for, and what the textbook underneath is for. You'll also see what no reading list can give you. Then you can choose a first book with your eyes open, and know what to do with it once you have.

## 31. Can You Life Coach Yourself, and How Would You Begin?

You know roughly what you'd like to change, and you've known it for months. Each time you meant to think it through properly, something more urgent came first, and now you're more frustrated with yourself than with the situation. You wonder whether you could coach yourself, and you half suspect that if it were that easy, you'd have done it already.

Can you life coach yourself, meaning do for yourself what a coach does for other people? The answer is yes, up to a point. Coaching is built mostly from good questions and careful listening, and you can ask yourself questions and listen to your own answers. That makes the beginning considerably easier than it sounds.

Two limits are worth knowing before you begin. You can't easily see what you can't see about yourself, and going round in circles can feel like progress. Knowing both now means you can start this week with one written question, and you'll recognise the moment to bring in another person.

## 32. What Is Life Coaching, and What Does a Life Coach Really Do?

You hear the words "life coach" used so casually and confidently that you've stopped asking what they mean. When someone mentions one, you smile politely, and privately you couldn't say what actually happens in a session, which leaves you feeling somewhat embarrassed and out of your depth. Is it advice, encouragement, or therapy under another name? You'd just like someone to give you a straight answer.

So what does a life coach do? Put simply, it's a structured conversation with you about your own goals and choices, and most of it is questions rather than instructions. Prospects, the UK's graduate careers service, sums the work up as [building relationships with clients and supporting them to make positive changes](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/jobs-and-work-experience/job-sectors/social-care/training-to-become-a-life-coach).

Once you can see what happens when two people sit down together, you can judge a coach for yourself, try the central skill on a friend, and know which questions to put to any coach before you book one. Achology's article [What Is Life Coaching, Really?](/learn/helping-people/articles/the-reality-of-life-coaching/) gives the fuller definition and explains how the title works.

## 33. Is NLP Backed by Science? What the Research Really Shows

Someone you respect swears that NLP changed their life, and it leaves you unsure what to make of it. You want to believe them, yet a quieter voice keeps asking whether it only feels true because they want it to. What you're after is being open-minded without being gullible, and you can't tell where the line sits.

Is NLP backed by science? The plain answer is that NLP isn't backed by science, at least not in the way its keenest supporters suggest. Published reviews of the research keep reaching the same conclusion: the evidence for NLP as a method is thin and often of poor quality. That doesn't mean the person who swears by it is imagining the difference it made, or that every idea NLP touches on is worthless.

It does mean you can't rest on the claim that NLP has been shown to work. It's better to know that before you spend money or place your hopes on it. With the research in hand you can hear a sincere story warmly and still ask what it actually proves.

## 34. NLP or Natural Language Processing: What Is the Difference?

These three letters keep turning up with two meanings that don't seem to belong together. One sounds like computers and the other like coaching, and nobody stops to say which they mean. You end up unsure which one you're hearing, and reluctant to ask in case the answer is obvious.

What is NLP natural language processing, and is it related to the other NLP? No: beyond the letters and an interest in language, they are separate fields with different aims. Natural language processing is computer science, the branch that teaches computers to work with human language. Neuro-linguistic programming is about how people communicate and change, and it began in California in the 1970s with Richard Bandler and John Grinder.

Knowing which is which saves you from reading about the wrong one, and it lets you tell what a page or a course is really offering. You'll also see how each field stands with researchers, and which one is about the words people use on themselves. You'll leave knowing which meaning to look for next.
