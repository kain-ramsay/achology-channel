# RULING: the line standard for titles and descriptions (Kain, S181, 26 October 2026)

**Kain's words, on the Help category band:** "you haven't assigned any kind of text, line, cutoff, discipline ... when one or two words roll onto an additional line, or in a two-line title, when the second line just has one word in it. It looks rubbish ... unless you have a standard for this, I can't possibly judge." On the standard Code then put to him, rendered on all 15 categories: "Yes, that's the line standard."

**The standard.**
- **Titles.** One line wherever they fit. When one must fold, its last two words are tied (a no-break space, written by the template; no word changes), so a single word never stands alone on the last line. Of the possible folds the one nearest two thirds on the top line is taken (his S081 rule, 60 to 70 per cent, "apply this to every heading that folds"), as far as the words allow, and never under 40 per cent where the words allow it; where they do not, the no-lone-last-word rule wins (a three-word title on a phone, "Outcomes / and Expectations", 37 per cent). A single word on the top line is allowed.
- **Descriptions** (standfirsts, leads, subtitles). At most 55 letters a line (the standfirst's measure, DSRD 7 the measure rule); the last line never one word and never under 40 per cent of the longest.
