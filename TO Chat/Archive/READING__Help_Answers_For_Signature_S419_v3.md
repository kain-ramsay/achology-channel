## 1. What makes a good CBT course, and how do you choose one?
# What makes a good CBT course, and how do you choose one?

So which Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) course should you choose? The best CBT course or certification is the one that fits what you want it for, and a star rating can't tell you that. A common belief is that one course sits at the top of the ratings and the choice ends there. Achology.com sees it the other way round, because the aim you name decides which course is right.

## Which is the best CBT course or certification for you?

Someone asking this question can have at least three different aims. Some want to work as a clinician, some want a first taste of the ideas, and some want to understand and use CBT on their own thinking and with the people around them. Say a manager wants to handle her own worry before meetings, while her friend wants a job as a clinician: a course that suits the first can be the wrong choice for the second. In Achology's view, "best" means nothing until you've said what the course is for.

## What if you want to work as a CBT therapist?

Then you need a clinical route, and the bodies that set the standard describe it themselves. The British Association for Behavioural and Cognitive Psychotherapies (BABCP) notes on its [accreditation page](https://babcp.com/what-is-accreditation/) that the members it accredits hold the core professional mental health training, or an equivalent it recognises, together with specific CBT training. In Achology's view, a short online course can't take its place.

The Beck Institute for Cognitive Behavior Therapy runs a clinical route of its own. Its [CBT Certified Clinician page](https://beckinstitute.org/certification/cbt-certified-clinician/) says there are no specific eligibility requirements to enrol. Its second, clinical phase asks for a terminal degree (the highest degree in a field) in health or mental health, a licence to practise independently where required, and clinical work under supervision that totals 2,000 hours. If clinical work is your aim, begin with the body that sets the standard for it.

## What if you want to understand CBT first?

In Achology's view, CBT is best met first as education about how your own thinking works, and that's the road taught on the [applied psychology training platform](/help/achology-basics-and-identity/what-is-achology/) Achology runs. Understanding comes before technique. In Achology's teaching, feeling follows thinking, so you check a thought by asking it real questions, of yourself first.

Someone who finishes one of its courses is a practitioner, never a therapist, because what its courses offer is education, never a clinical title. What equips a graduate is the competence they've built through practice, never a certificate or a title. Achology's answer on [what the certificate proves](/help/certificates-cpd-accreditation/what-achology-certificate-proves/) sets out what its certificates do and don't stand for.

## What should you ask of any CBT course?

Whichever route you're leaning towards, a few questions will tell you more than a star rating does. Ask them of every course you're considering, Achology's included, and they work just as well if all you want is a first taste. The answers show you what you'd be getting before you spend any time or money.

- What is the course for: a clinical role, a first taste, or understanding your own thinking?
- What does the certificate stand for, and who stands behind it?
- Will you practise on yourself, or only read?
- Does it promise a result? In Achology's view, a course that does is promising what no course can give.

## Which Achology course teaches CBT for your own use?

The Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) Practitioner Course holds at least 31 hours of teaching in 119 lessons, taught online. Its aim is to turn CBT on your own thinking first, so it comes without a clinical title and isn't a clinical route. If that's the aim you named above, the [CBT Practitioner Course](/academy/cognitive-behavioural-psychology/cbt-practitioner/) is where to go next.

## 2. Is a CBT course worth it, and who is it really for?
# Is a CBT course worth it, and who is it really for?

So is a Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) certification worth it? It's worth it if you want to use CBT yourself, and it isn't the right road to a clinical job. The answer to "is a CBT certification worth it" depends on which of those two you mean. A common belief is that a certificate is worth the doors it opens, while Achology.com puts its worth in the skill behind it.

## Is a CBT certification worth it if you want to use CBT yourself?

In Achology's view, yes, when your aim is to understand your own thinking and help the people near you. Thinking comes first and feeling follows, and understanding comes before any technique, so that's what a good course is built around. It gives you the ideas and the questions for checking a thought, and the value comes from using them on your own thinking first.

An Achology certificate of completion records that you finished the course, and nothing more. It makes no claim that you're a licensed professional, and it promises no set level of skill. What gives it worth is the skill you build by using what you learned. Say you've learned to check a thought, then a colleague snaps at you in a meeting: knowing the questions is one thing, and asking them of yourself in that moment is another.

Finishing a course is needed, but it isn't the end of the road. Achology's answer on [completion versus competence](/help/outcomes-and-expectations/course-completion-vs-competence/) explains the gap between the two. A person who spends six months putting a single course to use, in Achology's view, becomes more capable than one who completes five courses and applies none of them.

## Is it worth it if a clinical job is your aim?

In Achology's view, not on its own, and that's no criticism of a practitioner course, which does a different job. The British Association for Behavioural and Cognitive Psychotherapies (BABCP) describes its accredited members, on its [accreditation page](https://babcp.com/what-is-accreditation/), as people who have completed core professional training in mental health, or a recognised equivalent, and who have done CBT-specific training too. An Achology course is education, so it doesn't stand in for that.

The Beck Institute for Cognitive Behavior Therapy offers a clinical certification as well. Its [CBT Certified Clinician page](https://beckinstitute.org/certification/cbt-certified-clinician/) says the clinical phase needs a terminal degree, meaning the highest degree in its field. It also needs a licence to practise alone where the place requires one, and 2,000 hours of supervised clinical work. If a clinical job is your aim, a practitioner course won't get you there, though it can show you whether the longer road is worth taking.

## How do you decide if it's worth it for you?

Start with the use you have in mind for it, because that decides everything else. Then ask what the certificate stands for and who stands behind it, and whether you'll apply the ideas to yourself or only read about them. In Achology's view, a course that guarantees a result is claiming something no course can deliver, since the person does the growing.

## Which Achology course fits if you want to use CBT yourself?

The Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) Practitioner Course spreads 31 hours or more of teaching over 119 lessons, all taught online. It's written for people who want to understand CBT through their own thinking, and it ends without a clinical title, so it isn't a clinical route. If that describes you, the [CBT Practitioner Course](/academy/cognitive-behavioural-psychology/cbt-practitioner/) is the one to look at.

## 3. CBT practitioner or therapist: what is the real difference?
# CBT practitioner or therapist: what is the real difference?

So what is a Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) practitioner, and is that the same as a CBT therapist? It isn't, though the two are often mixed up. In Achology.com's view, a CBT practitioner has learned how CBT works, tried it on their own life first, and now uses it to help other people in everyday life, while a CBT therapist is a clinician who works with patients. A common belief is that to learn CBT you have to become a therapist, but neither one is better than the other, because they're two different jobs reached by two very different roads.

## What does a CBT therapist do, and how do you become one?

A CBT therapist uses CBT as a talking therapy with the people they see, which is why the work is clinical. The [National Health Service (NHS)](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/talking-therapies-medicine-treatments/talking-therapies-and-counselling/cognitive-behavioural-therapy-cbt/overview/) calls it "a type of talking therapy where a therapist helps you to change how you think and act". Because it's a clinical job, the training behind it is long.

In the United Kingdom and Ireland, the British Association for Behavioural and Cognitive Psychotherapies (BABCP) accredits CBT therapists, and its [accreditation requirements](https://babcp.com/what-is-accreditation/cognitive-behavioural-psychotherapist-accreditation/) call for a minimum of four years' training in total. That includes mental health training and experience as well as specialist CBT training. It's a long road, and it's the right one for that job.

In the United States, the Beck Institute runs a certification route for clinicians. It was founded in 1994 by Aaron Beck and Judith Beck, and its own page calls Aaron Beck the pioneering developer of CBT. Its [CBT Certified Clinician route](https://beckinstitute.org/certification/cbt-certified-clinician/) has an open first phase, then a clinical phase that asks for a terminal degree, meaning the top qualification in a subject, in health or mental health and 2,000 supervised clinical hours. So if your heart's set on working with patients, a clinical road like these is the one to take.

## What does a CBT practitioner do?

It's easy to see CBT as something only a therapist does with a patient, but at Achology, the [online academy of practical psychology](/help/achology-basics-and-identity/what-is-achology/), it's taught as education about how your own mind works. Achology teaches that thinking comes first and feeling follows: the way you think shapes the way you feel, and the way you feel shapes what you do. Understanding comes first, so you learn how your thinking works before you learn any method for managing it.

Here's what that looks like in real life. Say a friend is convinced she'll make a fool of herself at a job interview. A practitioner wouldn't tell her she's wrong. They'd help her ask questions of her own thought: is there any evidence for it, is there any against it, and will this matter in a year's time?

The important part is that a practitioner has done this with their own thinking first. You can't really help someone question a worry if you've never questioned one of your own. That's why a practitioner can use the same idea to help someone solve a problem, build discipline, set goals or settle an emotion. The next step still stays with the other person, since nobody can grow on someone else's behalf.

## Can a practitioner call themselves a therapist?

No, and it's better that way. In Achology's view, the word therapist suggests clinical training, professional registration and working with patients. A practitioner course doesn't set out to give you any of those, so practitioner is the accurate word. Achology has never encouraged anyone it trains to call themselves a therapist, and its [full position on the title](/help/certificates-cpd-accreditation/call-myself-therapist-achology-courses/) is set out in its own answer.

## Which road is right for you, practitioner or therapist?

If you want to work with patients, in a health service or under a clinical title, take the therapist road, since that's the work its training is built for. It's the right investment for that aim. A separate answer on [routes into clinical training](/help/comparisons-and-alternatives/achology-vs-therapy-training-counselling/) points you towards the recognised ones.

If you'd rather understand your own mind, and be more use to the people around you, the practitioner road will suit you better. In Achology's view, it also lets you test whether the longer road is for you, because you learn what applying these ideas feels like before you give years to them.

## Where can you learn CBT with Achology?

The Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) Practitioner Course is taught online, with 119 lessons and at least 31 hours. Choose [the CBT course](/academy/cognitive-behavioural-psychology/cbt-practitioner/) when you'd like to apply these ideas to your own thinking first, with other people coming later. Look elsewhere if you need a clinical title, because finishing it doesn't give you one.

## 4. How do you become a life coach, step by step?
# How do you become a life coach, step by step?

So how do you become a life coach? In Achology.com's view, there isn't one official path that every coach follows, and a quick certificate doesn't make you a coach. Achology teaches that you build your own path: practise on your own life first, learn how people think and change, practise with real people, and describe your training accurately. A life coach is a guide, not a guru, so the work is mostly skill and character.

## What does a life coach do?

A life coach helps someone get clearer about where their life is going and take a next step. Achology teaches that everyone is heading somewhere, even the person who says they're going nowhere, so coaching starts with noticing the direction you're already on. Then the coach asks whether it's the one you'd choose. A coach asks rather than tells, because the person is the one who has to walk it.

## Is there an official path to follow?

You might expect one fixed route to qualify, but in Achology's view coaching doesn't have a single route that every coach takes. A credential from a professional body is one choice you can make, and you can still learn the work without it. Achology is an online academy of practical psychology, and it teaches life coaching as applied psychology, learned on yourself first.

The [International Coaching Federation](https://coachingfederation.org/about) (ICF) calls itself "the leading voice for the global coaching industry". It offers credentials of its own, and whether to take one is a decision you make for yourself. In Achology's view, nobody else has endorsed any coaching organisation as the world authority, so each credential is one body's own standard.

ICF's [credentials page](https://coachingfederation.org/credentials-and-standards/) says its Associate Certified Coach credential asks for coaching experience of at least 100 hours, on top of at least 60 hours of education. The Professional Certified Coach credential raises those figures to at least 125 hours of education and at least 500 hours of experience. Check the current requirements before you plan around them.

## What does it take to become a life coach?

In Achology's view, you start with your own life. A coach who has never examined their own life can't help anyone examine theirs, because you can't offer what you haven't practised. Find out where you're heading and what you want, because you can't steer what you can't see, and notice what makes you angry or sad, since that usually points at something you could act on.

Next, study how people form beliefs, make decisions and change course, and how to hold a coaching conversation. A course, books or both will do it, whichever you'll keep up. Nobody can grow on another person's behalf, so a coach can't promise anyone an outcome, and what you can offer is good questions.

Then practise with real people, because knowing an idea and using it in a live conversation are different things. Competence builds through repetition, feedback and time, and Achology's answer on how to [build real competence](/help/events-and-mentorship/build-real-competence-achology/) says more. A certificate shows you studied, and the practice is what makes you a coach worth talking to.

## What if the person you're helping needs more than coaching?

Helping is never medical care. A coach doesn't diagnose anyone, and if someone needs a doctor, a good coach says so and sends them there, since helping includes knowing when somebody else is needed. Achology's answer on [coaching versus counselling training](/help/certificates-cpd-accreditation/coaching-vs-counselling-credentials-difference/) explains where the two differ, so you know which one you're offering.

## How should you describe your training?

Describe it accurately: what you studied, for how long, and what you've practised. An Achology certificate shows education completed. It isn't a licence, professional registration or a regulated title, and claiming more would mislead the people who trust you. If you take a credential from a body such as ICF, say which one it is and what it asked of you.

It's made for someone who wants to learn coaching by testing each idea on their own life first. If a credential from a professional coaching body matters to you, look at that body's route as well, because this course carries Achology's own certificate. You can read about [the Life Coaching Certificate Course](/academy/life-coaching/life-coaching-certificate/) on its own page.

## 5. What does a life coaching course actually teach you?
# What does a life coaching course actually teach you?

So what does a life coaching course actually teach you? A common belief is that it hands you a framework and a set of tools for running sessions. Achology.com teaches that the framework is the smaller part: you learn how people think, believe, decide and change, starting with your own life, so you can hold a conversation that leaves the other person in charge. Courses differ, so it's worth checking what each one covers and in what order.

## What do most life coaching courses cover?

The International Coaching Federation (ICF) says its [Core Competencies](https://coachingfederation.org/credentials-and-standards/core-competencies/) are used as the basis for ICF certifications and inform the curriculum of ICF-accredited coaching programmes. It groups them into four domains: foundation, co-creating the relationship, communicating effectively, and cultivating learning and growth. A course built around a framework like that teaches the structure of coaching, which is a sensible thing to learn. Achology's view is that understanding people comes first, because a structure only helps a coach who can see the person in front of them.

## What does a life coaching course actually teach at Achology?

Achology teaches life coaching across seven sections, and each one builds on the one before. It starts with what coaching is and isn't, a simple staged process, and the difference between telling someone what to do and asking. Then it turns to the person: mindset, self-knowledge, how perception leaves gaps, and how beliefs, identity and personality shape what someone does.

The middle sections show coaching in practice, with full recorded coaching conversations you can watch and then see taken apart. After that the focus moves to what drives a person: their values and motives, and how what they put first today shapes what they get tomorrow. You look at your own beliefs and values before you help anyone with theirs, because understanding comes before technique.

The last two sections cover communication, such as listening, empathy and useful questions, and then goals and action: finding a direction, building a vision and planning a next step. Workbooks and open discussions appear along the way. Achology's answer on [what's inside a module](/help/learning-experience/inside-achology-course-modules-breakdown/) says what you receive with each part.

## Is coaching the same as giving advice?

No, advice and coaching are different jobs. A guide points at the road and leaves the walking to you, and a coach works the same way, asking questions rather than handing out answers. Achology's teaching covers directive and non-directive coaching to show where that line sits. Since no one can do your growing for you, a coach can promise nobody an outcome, and a good one hands your life back with a question.

## What won't a life coaching course teach you?

Life coaching is education, not medical or clinical training. Someone who completes Achology's coaching teaching is a coach, never a therapist, and it carries no licence or regulated title. If a person needs medical attention, a coach tells them so and points them towards a doctor, which is part of caring for them.

Choose it if you'd like to find out how coaching works by trying the ideas on yourself first. Anyone who needs a certificate from a professional coaching organisation should look at that organisation's route too, because this one is Achology's own. ## 6. What is the best hypnotherapy course, and who accredits it?
What is the best hypnotherapy course, and who accredits it? There isn't one best hypnotherapy course for everyone, and no single body accredits hypnotherapy for the whole world. Each school and society gives its own approval by its own rules, and none can speak for everyone. So ask which approval fits what you want: a place on a society's register, a certain qualification, or learning the subject well.

A common belief is that whichever course carries the grandest badge must be the best, as if an accreditation ranked courses for you. Achology.com teaches that a belief is an idea taken to be true, and it isn't a fact just because it's believed. A badge is one group saying yes by its own rules. A better question is what you can't see yet: who gave it, what it asked of the people who hold it, and where it leads.

## Is any organisation the world authority on hypnotherapy?

Several groups present themselves as leading voices in hypnotherapy, yet the world has no single authority on it. No outside party has named any of them the top one, so a group that claims to be the world's number one has only said so itself. That doesn't say anything against its members or its teaching. Reading what each group says about itself is the place to start, and what its badge is worth is yours to decide.

## What does each well-known name say about itself?

Two names from the United States are ones people type into search when they ask this question. The [National Guild of Hypnotists](https://ngh.net/about-us/) describes itself as a not-for-profit, educational corporation in the State of New Hampshire, initially founded in Boston, Massachusetts in 1950. That's the Guild's own account of its history, and it isn't a ranking. Whether its approval matters to you depends on the aim you started with.

The American Board of Hypnotherapy says on its [certification page](https://abh-abnlp.com/certification/) that, in its opinion, anyone wanting to induce trance in another person should be adequately trained. That's a view the Board holds about training in general, and it's worth keeping in mind whichever course you look at. It's an opinion and not a ranking of courses, so treat it as a starting point for your own questions.

A well-known United Kingdom name is the [National Hypnotherapy Society](https://nationalhypnotherapysociety.org/). Its site says its Accredited Register is recognised by the Professional Standards Authority, and that a course it recognises has been mapped to its Training Standards criteria. Those are the Society's own words about itself, and you can read them on its site. A register and a course badge are different things, so ask which one a claim is about.

In Australia, the [Australian Society of Clinical Hypnotherapists](https://asch.com.au/educational-requirements) says that to achieve professional membership you need an accredited diploma in hypnotherapy from a school whose curriculum it has evaluated. It also gives a training minimum of 1,000 hours from 1 January 2025. Each body here describes its own rules, and none of this is a ranking. The figures belong to that society and say nothing about any other.

## What does an accreditation tell you?

An accreditation is one group saying yes to a course by its own rules, so it's fair to ask what those rules are. Think of the sticker in a taxi window: it tells you which association the driver joined, and it doesn't tell you whether this driver will get you to the airport on time. The sticker is a starting point for a question, never the answer to one. A badge on any course page deserves the same questions.

Three questions get you most of the way, and you can put them to any school, Achology included. Who gave the badge, and is it independent of whoever sells the course? What did the badge demand of those who hold it, beyond turning up? And what does it say it does and doesn't lead to?

Achology's own [answer on its accreditation](/help/certificates-cpd-accreditation/is-achology-accredited-somap/) says the Society of Modern Applied Psychology, an independent body outside Achology, accredits its education. Whether that suits you turns on what you want a badge for. If you want a place on a hypnotherapy society's register, ask that society what it asks of you.

## Are there rules about practising hypnotherapy?

Achology's understanding is that most countries have no law about who may use the title hypnotherapist, though that's not so everywhere, and rules vary by place. Washington state, in the United States, has a [registration law](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=18.19.035): practising hypnotherapy for a fee, or in a state agency post, needs registration with the state unless an exemption applies. That's one place's rule, so yours may differ.

If you intend to charge clients, look up what applies where you live before paying for any course. The Washington State Department of Health's [registration requirements page](https://doh.wa.gov/licenses-permits-and-certificates/professions-new-renew-or-update/hypnotherapist/registration-requirements) lists what an applicant there must provide, such as every state where they hold or have held credentials. Your local council or health authority can tell you what applies to you. Do that first, since it can change which course suits you.

## Which is the best hypnotherapy course for you?

The answer turns on your aim. If your aim is a place on one body's register or a particular qualification, start with that body's own pages, because they say what it asks for. Its route is the right road for that aim, and Achology isn't a substitute for it. If what draws you is the way people take in what they're told, Achology teaches that too.

Say a friend tells you she's training to be a hypnotherapist, and you wonder whether you should too. A first step is to try the teaching on yourself, which is useful whichever way you go next. If you enjoy it, you'll know which badge to look at next, and why.

## 7. Can you call yourself a clinical hypnotherapist?
Can you call yourself a clinical hypnotherapist? It depends on where you live and on who you ask, since the word clinical has no single meaning and rules on titles vary by place. Before you put the words on a card, find out the rules where you live, and ask what a stranger will take the title to mean. Achology.com teaches that the second matters just as much as the first.

You'll see it on school sites, on practitioners' pages and on certificates, and the uses don't all match.

## What does clinical hypnotherapist mean, and who uses it?

Some bodies keep the word for people trained in health care. The [American Society of Clinical Hypnosis](https://asch.net/about-asch/) calls itself "a community of licensed healthcare professionals that advances the ethical use of hypnosis" on its own pages. Those are the society's rules for its own certificate. They show what the word means to that body and nowhere else, and another body may ask for more or for less.

On [HypnoTC's clinical hypnotherapy page](https://hypnotc.com/clinical-hypnotherapy-diploma-certificate/), a training company takes a different view. That's one company's opinion of its own field, and the page shows no date, so read it as a view. The same words mean a licensed health worker at one body and a label at another, and a certificate won't tell you which one you're looking at.

Rules on titles, and on working for pay, vary a lot. One American state, Washington, has a [law on registering hypnotherapists](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=18.19.035). A voluntary register is one you may choose to join, which isn't the same as a law that asks for registration. To find out what applies to you, ask your local council or health body.

Plenty of groups talk as though they were the last word on hypnotherapy, yet the world has no authority on it. No independent body has ranked any one group first in the world, so a group that says it is has only said so about itself. A badge can show what you studied, but not what your own place allows.

## 9. Where Can I Learn Gerard Egan's Skilled Helper Model?
# Where Can I Learn Gerard Egan's Skilled Helper Model?

If you're looking for a Skilled Helper course, you have three routes: the book, a workshop that uses the model, or a course. Gerard Egan created the Skilled Helper model, a three-stage way of helping a person move from a problem towards something they want. Achology.com teaches it in two courses of its own.

## What is the Skilled Helper model?

It's an approach to helping that moves through three distinct stages. [NHS Education for Scotland](https://www.appraisal.nes.scot.nhs.uk/media/z33iukuz/iac-egan-pre-course-ref.pdf), in pre-workshop material on the model, names the stages as the current picture, the preferred picture and the way forward. The first stage looks at where the person is now, the second at what they need or want, and the third at how to get there.

That gives a helper a straightforward sequence to follow. First you understand where the person is, then you help them see what they'd like instead, and then you help them find a first step. The model comes from Egan's book The Skilled Helper, which [Open Library](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL11388849M/The_Skilled_Helper) lists as first published in 1974.

## What did Gerard Egan create?

Gerard Egan created the Skilled Helper model and set it out in his book. An [interview with him](https://research.cumbria.ac.uk/en/publications/action-man-an-interview-with-gerard-egan/) in the British Journal of Guidance and Counselling described him as a professor of organization studies and psychology at Loyola University of Chicago. It's worth reading if you'd like to hear his ideas in his own words rather than through a summary.

Achology also has a biography of [Gerard Egan](/learn/helping-people/articles/gerard-egan/) on its Knowledge Hub. It's a good next read if you'd like to know more about him before you decide how to learn his model. You can read about the person first and the method second, or the other way round, whichever draws you in.

## Where can you take a skilled helper course?

The first route is the book itself. Reese, that introduces Egan's three-stage framework. Its publisher describes the focus as professional rather than academic, so it's a good choice for anyone who enjoys reading and studying independently.

The second route is a workshop. NHS Education for Scotland uses the model in a half-day workshop for appraisal conversations, so the model is used in workplace training. A workshop like that is designed for one particular professional setting, which is worth understanding before you book one. Ask any provider what the workshop covers, who teaches it, and whether you'll practise the stages with other people.

The third route is a course, which gives you teaching, worked examples and time to try the model on your own life. Courses differ in how much of the model they teach, so ask what's covered. Achology has a longer piece on [the Skilled Helper model](/learn/helping-people/articles/gerard-egans-skilled-helper-model-using-the-3-stage-framework/) if you'd like to see the three stages worked through before you choose.

## Is helping about fixing people?

A common belief is that helping means an expert who listens and then tells you what to do. In Achology's view, helping is never fixing, curing, correcting or steering someone, because those are all ways of controlling another person. A helper asks rather than tells, and hands the job of running a life back to the person whose life it is.

Say a friend tells you she's fed up at work. You could tell her to quit. Or you could ask what's going on, what she'd like instead, and what she could try first. She's the one who has to live with the choice, so the next step stays hers.

## 11. Is CBT hard to learn, and can you teach it to yourself?
# Is CBT hard to learn, and can you teach it to yourself?

So is Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) difficult to pick up, and can you work it out for yourself? You can, and how to start learning CBT is simple: you begin with one thought of your own. A common belief is that CBT is something only a therapist can hand to you. Achology.com teaches it as education, and the ideas are straightforward to follow, though applying them consistently in ordinary days demands practice more than study.

## How hard are the ideas of CBT to learn?

In Achology's view, the ideas are easy to follow. Thinking comes first and feeling follows, so how you habitually think shapes how you feel, and how you feel shapes what you do. Thinking has habits, such as expecting the worst or guessing what other people think, and they're learned patterns, never who you are. A habit that can be seen can be questioned, and that's the central idea in a single sentence.

The hard part is the doing, and that's where most of the learning happens. You'll forget to check a thought just when you need to, particularly when you're exhausted or emotionally unsettled, and that's no indication you've misunderstood the method. Understanding CBT is quick compared with building the habit of using it, which is why practice, more than reading, is where it's learned.

## Can you teach CBT to yourself?

The [National Health Service (NHS)](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/talking-therapies-medicine-treatments/talking-therapies-and-counselling/cognitive-behavioural-therapy-cbt/overview/) presents CBT as a talking therapy, one in which a therapist works with you to change how you think and act. That's CBT as a therapy, which is one road among several. In Achology's view, learning how CBT works is education, and education can begin on your own, through a structured, self-paced course or through your own practice. It's a reasonable beginning because nobody can grow on your behalf, however experienced the teacher.

## How to start learning CBT: what's the first step?

Start with one thought of your own that has recently upset you. Say you send your manager an email and she hasn't replied by the end of the day, and you're sure she's annoyed with you. That thought isn't a fact, it's a guess, and a guess can be checked. These are the checks Achology teaches, asked of yourself first:

- Is there real evidence for this thought, and is there any evidence against it?
- Do I have all the facts, or am I assuming some of them?
- What would a trusted friend say about the situation?
- Will this still matter a year from now?

Notice what you felt before you asked them, and what you felt afterwards. Try it again tomorrow with a different thought, and keep going until asking the questions becomes a habit. That's how the ideas turn from something you've read into something you do. If you'd rather have a structured route, an answer on the [first course for beginners](/help/getting-started/first-course-complete-beginner/) will point you to one.

The Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) Practitioner Course has 119 lessons and 31 hours or more of teaching, and nobody needs a psychology background to begin. It applies CBT to your own thinking first, so there's no clinical title at the end. If you'd like the ideas laid out step by step rather than found one at a time, the [CBT Practitioner Course](/academy/cognitive-behavioural-psychology/cbt-practitioner/) gives you that order.

## 24. Where should you start with psychoanalysis as a beginner?
