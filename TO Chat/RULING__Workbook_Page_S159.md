## 2. Then Kain ruled the page needs a bridge

Kain: "this is essentially a sales page ... we now have one opportunity to present the course and membership options as two viable starting points ... there's no bridge into this, it just doesn't feel smooth, it feels very, very abrupt, and very, very salesy ... It's not even remotely inviting. It's not nurturing." He asked what ClickFunnels and Kit recommend. Code's research (S159): ClickFunnels' bridge page and Brunson's Epiphany Bridge (support.myclickfunnels.com/docs/how-to-create-a-bridge-funnel/; shortform.com/blog/russell-brunson-epiphany-bridge/), Kit (kit.com/resources/blog/thank-you-pages), Leadpages ("you don't want to give them your sales pitch just yet", leadpages.com/blog/thank-you-pages-confirmation-pages), Unbounce (unbounce.com/landing-pages/thank-you-pages/), teardowns of Pat Flynn's and Ramit Sethi's pages (swiped.co). The finding: the reader has given an email, not trust, and has not yet opened the workbook, so a product card asks for a second yes before the first has paid off. A bridge, in a person's voice, carries them from "I got something free" to "I want to go further" before any product is named; the two options are framed by the reader ("if you want the whole method ... if you would rather learn by talking it through ..."), prices quiet beside the guarantee, buttons that invite ("See the full course"), never "Buy" or "Enrol now".

**Kain said yes to this order of blocks, S159:**

7. **The two paths,** each saying who it suits first, then what it is, three points, the price small with the guarantee or the $7 trial beside it, one inviting button and one quiet link. Course: the Diploma Course in Modern Applied Psychology (DiMAP), $299 lifetime, full money back guarantee. Membership: live discussion groups every day of the week, $7 for the first 30 days then $34.50 per month (DSRD 4 section 1.4).
8. **A gentle close:** no rush, the workbook is yours either way.
