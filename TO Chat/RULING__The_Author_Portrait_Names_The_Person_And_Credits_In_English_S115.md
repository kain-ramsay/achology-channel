# RULING: the author portrait names the person, credits the photographer in English, and gives its hairline room

**DOCUMENT TYPE:** ruling, filed by Claude Code, Session 115, theme session. **Date:** Monday 14 September 2026.
**Filed under Harness Rule 14.** Extends his S113 ruling that a credit line sits under the picture beneath a faint hairline, rather than replacing it.
**Shipped:** Theme 0.412.1, deployed and read back on all three variants.
**Board card:** Book note page template; Book author portraits.

---

## 1. Kain's words

> "under the author image that we have built into each article, you've got a hairline literally immediately underneath it. There's not appropriate spacing on that whatsoever ... there needs to be the author's name above the kind of the text you've got there. Having a CC BY-SA 3.0, having a code there, that's just not good. I understand we're referencing someone else's material, but what would be a better proposal?"

## 2. He was right about something worse than the code

The line read **"Gage Skidmore, CC BY-SA 3.0" directly beneath a photograph of Daniel Siegel**, with nothing anywhere saying that Gage Skidmore was the photographer.

Under a portrait, a bare name reads as the person in it. **The caption was naming the wrong man**, on 62 pages, and it had been doing so since the portraits shipped.

## 3. What now holds

**The name of the person pictured sits above the hairline**, in the body's own type. That is the fact a reader actually wants, and having it there is what stops the photographer's name being read as the subject's.

**Below the hairline, a sentence rather than a code.** "Photograph by Gage Skidmore, used under a Creative Commons licence", with the last three words linking the exact deed. Public domain reads "Photograph in the public domain". Achology's own photographs read "Photograph by Achology".

**The attribution is stronger for being readable, not weaker.** A Creative Commons licence asks for the creator named and the licence identified. A sentence naming the photographer with the deed linked does both, and does them better than a code nobody can expand, because the link carries the version.

**The licence address is derived from the record, never listed.** A table of licences would need editing every time a new one appeared in the data, and the first time it was not edited the line would silently lose its link. A licence the parser cannot read keeps its words and loses only the link, which is the safe way round.

## 4. The spacing, and why it is 16 rather than his standing 48

Eight above and eight below. **Eight is the step for things that belong together**, which is the opposite of what a hairline does, so the line read as attached to the picture rather than as separating anything.

It is **16 either side now, and deliberately not the 32 the side column took this morning or the 48 his standing rule gives every hairline**. This block is 240 wide and sits INSIDE a paragraph with the writing running round it; 96 of air in it would push the text beside it out of shape. Air scales with the measure it sits in, which is the same reasoning as the column, one step further down.

## 5. Two faults the read-back caught on the drawn mark

**It called an illustration a photograph.** The drawn name plate shared the credit path with Achology's own photographs and rendered "Photograph by Achology" under a plate that plainly is not one. It has its own credit value now and reads "Illustration by Achology".

**And it printed the name twice.** The plate reads "TARA / Brach" and the new caption added "Tara Brach" again eight pixels below it. The name line exists because a photograph cannot say who it is; the mark is made of the name and says it already.

Both were shipped in 0.412.0 and corrected in 0.412.1 within the same sitting. Neither would have been found by reading the code.

OWED BACK: nothing. Recorded so DSRD 8 carries the caption's two parts and the 16, and so the next portrait component does not inherit the bare-name caption this replaces.
