# RULING: the contents panel is the Ring (theme session, S164, the page sweep)

**From:** Claude Code, theme session S164, 13 October 2026. **Ruled by:** Kain, in the sitting, over three rounds in `previews/toc-s164`. **Session:** theme. **Board card:** the four content page templates. **Replaces:** RULING__Contents_Lines_And_Foot_Conversation_S163 sections 1 and 2 (the Drawn Bubble, its reading line and its centred heading).

## 1. How it was chosen
- **Why it changed.** Kain: the panel was "a little bit clunky, bulky. It doesn't look visually spectacular." Code's diagnosis:
  - the heading was as loud as the titles;
  - long titles wrapped onto three lines at one tone;
  - a white card sat inside a cream card;
  - nothing showed progress;
  - four shapes competed.
- **Round 1, four directions.** Kain chose the Progress Card.
- **Round 2, eight Progress Cards on one typographic system.** Kain: "your use of fonts and text sizes and spacing just isn't good". He chose the Ring.
- **Round 3, four Rings.** "Table of Contents" became a real heading in every one ("really small"). Kain chose the Header Band, then cut its "Section 3 of 8" line ("just have the percentage read").

## 2. The standard, as built (0.707.249 and 0.707.250)
- **The card:** white, with a hairline border, the panel radius and the card shadow.
- **The band across its top:**
  - cream, holding "Table of Contents" in the heading face at 21;
  - beneath the heading, "N% read" in orange;
  - on the right, a 56px ring that fills to the share read, with "current/total" in its centre.
- **Two numbers, one source.** The share read is the header reading line's own calculation, so the two always agree. The section is the panel's own current section.
- **The rows:**
  - one per section, the title at 15/1.45, under hairlines;
  - an 18px status mark at one indent: a tick on orange for read, an orange ring for current, an empty circle for to come;
  - three tones: read in mid grey, current in dark at 600, to come in soft grey.
- **Below 1200:** the ring stands down and the open and close control sits inside the band. Folded on a phone, the card is the band alone.
