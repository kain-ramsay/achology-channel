## 1. Why Security Comes at the Cost of Freedom


Some part of you has been choosing the safe option for a long while, and lately it feels less like caution and more like being stuck. Other days you ask why a safe choice should leave you feeling smaller. The reply from Kain Ramsay is short: "If we choose security, it's going to come at the cost of freedom."

## 2. Why Connection Determines Your Peace


Plenty of conversations can fill a week and still leave you feeling oddly alone, as though nobody has met the real you. You may be asking whether the fault lies in your friendships or in how much of yourself you bring to them. Here is why connection determines your peace, in the words of Kain Ramsay: "The depth of connection that we experience in our relationships is ultimately going to determine the amount of peace that we experience in our lives."

## 3. Why Clear Requests Protect Boundaries


You've been going along with things you'd rather have refused, and the resentment is beginning to colour how you feel about the people asking. Part of you fears that setting a limit would make you rude, or at least hard to like. Kain Ramsay's account of why clear requests protect boundaries is this: "We can make honest and humble requests, and this allows other people to at least get clear where they stand with us."

## 4. Why You Can Manage Prejudice


Sometimes a judgement about someone arrives before you've decided anything, and afterwards you feel uneasy that it came from you at all. You'd like to be fair, and you're not sure that wanting to be is enough. Kain Ramsay's line on why you can manage prejudice comes down to this: "But we can learn to manage our prejudices and discipline ourselves not to discriminate."

## 5. Why Understanding Beats Labeling People


Within moments of meeting someone, a verdict about them forms, and you're not sure whether to trust it or feel uneasy that it came so fast. It saves effort, and it can also leave you feeling distant from people you might have liked. Why understanding beats labeling people comes down to this line from Kain Ramsay: "Imagine if we just prioritized understanding people instead of assigning labels onto them."
