## What is on the page

One card. The Editorial index above, a hairline, then the share row as the
card's footer. The card carries the surface, the border and the padding; the
contents block inside it carries none.

## This supersedes a line of his own brief, and that is worth recording

His written brief for the Editorial index said sharing stays OUTSIDE the
contents component, with the same placement in every variant. That was right for
the comparison it governed. Once the index landed, the share row was a loose
block under a white panel, and his words on seeing it were that it looked
unruly.

**What the line was protecting is kept.** The share row is still not part of the
contents: it is a footer under a rule, nothing about the contents reaches it, and
it is the same six controls in the same order.

## The diagnosis that produced the five, because it outlives them

The row carries **six marks in two languages**: four solid brand logos, each with
its own silhouette and optical weight, and two outline interface icons drawn on
one stroke at one grid. At equal size, side by side, they cannot read as a set.
The tinted circles Kain had before were hiding it; taking them away exposed it.

**The brand marks cannot be redrawn to match**, and should not be: DSRD 7
section 5.2 keeps brand artwork out of the icon registry deliberately, and a logo
redrawn to suit a grid is a logo used wrongly. So the five answers were: separate
the two languages, name them in words instead of drawing them, let an identical
container do the agreeing, move the row inside the card, or make the whole block
one quiet line. **He chose the fourth.** The problem it does not solve is still
there and is now contained: six mismatched marks inside one object read better
than six mismatched marks loose on a page, but they are still six mismatched
marks. Worth knowing if this component is ever revisited.

## What was deleted

The other four designs, their stylesheet, their script, their PHP, the require,
the selector, the class hook on the card, and the name spans that only two of the
rejected designs ever drew. A settled question left as a switch is a question
that gets reopened by accident.

## Three faults found by measuring before he saw anything

Design 1 was six worded pills and wrapped to three lines at 210 tall, taller than
the card it belonged to, which is the fault the set exists to fix rather than a
version of it. Design 2's controls came out 38 tall against a 44 floor, because a
min-height in a flex row was being sized to content. Design 4's were 40 by my own
hand, which is under the floor too. All three corrected before the comparison was
shown.
