## 1. Kain's words, verbatim

> "I think the titles, some of them just read a little bit arrogant, to be honest with you. A little bit arrogant, maybe a little bit presumptuous rather than inviting. I'd like them to read just in a slightly more inviting and broader, yet more compelling frame ... Obviously we've only got so much space to forward these titles. But I think it could be a little bit more descriptively enticing than what they are."

He offered to settle the wording in the sitting and then chose this route instead, so **the drafting is Chat's and the choice is his.**

## 2. The five as they stand

1. What this Book is Actually Saying
2. Where the Author is Coming From
3. What Could this Mean for Society?
4. What You Can Take From the Book
5. What are Your Next Learning Steps?

## 3. Which three carry what he is describing, in Code's reading

Offered as assessment rather than as drafting, and Chat is free to disagree with it.

- **"What this Book is Actually Saying"** is the arrogant one. "Actually" implies the book has been obscuring something and that we are here to correct it.
- **"What Could this Mean for Society?"** is the grandiose one. It asks a book note to speak for civilisation.
- **"What are Your Next Learning Steps?"** instructs rather than invites, and assumes the reader is on a course.

**Two are fine and Code would leave them.** "Where the Author is Coming From" is genuinely warm, and "What You Can Take From the Book" is plain rather than presumptuous, if a little flat.

## 4. The constraint the wording has to live inside

**They are short for a reason.** Each heading appears twice: as the H2 in the writing, and as a row in the contents panel, which is 256 wide. A heading longer than the current ones wraps to three lines in that panel. The longest now is 33 characters; **treat that as the working ceiling** rather than a hard rule, and Code will measure whatever comes back before it ships.

## 5. The cost, which Kain has been told and should be weighed in the drafting

**These are not template strings.** Every one of the 99 published book notes carries all five inside its own body, and `content_gate` refuses a record that does not have exactly these five in exactly this order.

So new wording is not a switch in the theme. It is:

1. the five strings changed in `single-book_note.php` and in the gate's standard,
2. **99 record bodies edited** to carry the new headings, which is Cowork's work,
3. and a re-import or an in-place update of all 99, which is Code's.

**Nothing should change in the theme until the records are ready**, or the gate will refuse every book note on the install. Code has changed nothing and is waiting on the wording.

## 6. What is asked back

A set of five for Kain to choose from, with the three above carrying most of the work, and a note on whether Chat agrees that the other two stay. Then the sequencing of the record rewrite, which is Chat's to plan with Cowork.
